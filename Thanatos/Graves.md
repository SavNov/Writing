The wind howled through the trees as I stood beside Reyna and the other generals, my clipboard clutched tightly in my hands. Lightning cracked in the distance, lighting up the battlefield before us. It was almost poetic—this storm, this moment. Chaos, like the war that was about to erupt. The enemy was coming in force, three armies marching with precision, their numbers overwhelming ours. The rain hadn’t started yet, but I could feel it in the air. The trenches that would soon run with water, then with blood.  

Reyna stood in front of us, her gaze fixed on the horizon. Her usual calm was strained, her expression hardened. I admired her, like everyone did—her resolve, her strength—but today, even she couldn’t mask the gravity of what we were about to face.

“We’re outnumbered more than two to one,” she said, her voice carrying over the wind, steady but tinged with the weight of reality. “We have to strike one of their armies before they converge. But the question is which.”

I knew that question all too well. I'd seen the answer a hundred times in my mind already. Carnage. Absolute carnage. Carnage and bodies. I stared at the approaching armies, each one moving like a tide ready to crash into us. My clipboard was still blank, but I didn’t need to write anything to know the numbers. I could see them, ticking down, golden letters hovering above the heads of our soldiers. They had no idea how little time some of them had left.

Marcus, one of the other generals, crossed his arms, his brow furrowed as he studied the approaching forces. “The first army’s the closest,” he said, pointing to the left. “But if we hit them, we’ll be in range of the others before we can retreat.”

“It’s a death trap,” murmured Cato, another of Reyna’s trusted lieutenants, his usually sharp tongue dulled by the looming battle. “But if we wait for them to converge, we’re done for.”

Reyna’s eyes flicked to me, and I knew what was coming. She trusted my insight, trusted my abilities, but every time she asked, a part of me wished she wouldn’t. "Clay," she said, her voice softer, like she knew the burden it placed on me. "What do you see?"

What do I see? I see the end. I see tombstones. I see their names. I swallowed hard, gripping the clipboard tighter as I glanced toward the first army, the one Marcus had suggested. Fifty soldiers, counting down. Seventy of our own, dead, if we hit them first. I could feel the weight in my chest growing heavier as I moved my eyes to the second army. Ninety dead. More bodies. More names I wouldn’t be able to forget.

Finally, my gaze settled on the third. Sixty dead, fewer than the others, but still—sixty lives. Sixty families who would never see their children, their siblings, their friends again. Each loss was like a dagger in my gut. I imagined the scene playing out, the screams, the sound of metal clashing, the smell of blood in the air. I shook my head, trying to shake off the images, but they clung to me like shadows.

“We attack the third army,” I said finally, my voice rougher than I intended. Everyone turned to me, waiting for an explanation. I took a breath, steadying myself. "If we go after the first army, we lose seventy. The second army, ninety. The third... sixty."

A silence fell over the group, the numbers settling into their minds. It wasn’t much better, but it was something. Sixty. Sixty was better than ninety. It was better than seventy. It was all we had.

“That’s still a third of our men,” Marcus muttered, running a hand through his hair. “What the hell are we supposed to do with that?”

“It’s not a choice between victory or defeat,” Reyna said, her voice firm, cutting through the doubt. “It’s a choice between survival or annihilation.” She looked back at me, her eyes softer. “You’re sure, Clay?”

I hated that question. Of course, I was sure. I was always sure. That was the problem. “I’m sure,” I replied quietly, though the weight of those words crushed me.

Reyna nodded and turned back to the generals. “We hit the third army. We’ll have to make it fast, strike hard, and retreat before the other two can close in. We’ll take whatever advantage we can find.”

I could feel their eyes on me, judging, questioning. Some of them probably thought I was cold for spouting off numbers like they were statistics in a game. But I wasn’t cold. I was the opposite. I felt everything too much, and that’s why I had to stay in control. If I let it all in, if I let the knowledge of their deaths overwhelm me, I’d be useless. I’d fall apart.

Cato spoke up, his tone more pragmatic, though there was a grim acceptance in his voice. “Third army it is, then. Let’s make sure those sixty don’t die for nothing.”

The generals dispersed, going to prepare the troops. Reyna stayed by my side a moment longer, her gaze thoughtful. “You’re holding up?” she asked, not as a commander, but as a friend.

I didn’t look at her, focusing instead on the clipboard in my hands. I could already see the names forming on it, the dead writing themselves into the list as the minutes ticked by. “As much as I can.”

Reyna placed a hand on my shoulder. “You carry more than any of us can imagine. But remember—you’re not responsible for their deaths. You’re helping us save the ones we can.”

I wished I could believe that. But I didn’t. Not fully. How could I, when I saw it all coming and could do so little to stop it? I nodded anyway, not trusting my voice.

She left to join the others, and I stood there, the storm swirling above us, the weight of the future crushing down on my chest. Sixty men. Sixty deaths.

I would remember them all.
