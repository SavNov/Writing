Revision 2:
I clutched my clipboard, called Death's Ledger, in my hands as lightning cracked across the sky. Even Jupiter seemed to really not like what was going to happen down here. It was almost poetic—this storm, this moment. Chaos in the skies to parrot the chaos about to unfold below as we were pinned down deep in enemy territory with no hope of escape.

The enemy was coming in force, three armies marching with precision, their numbers overwhelming ours. The rain hadn’t started yet, but I could feel it in the air. The trenches that would soon run with water, then with blood.  

A gust of wind rattled the trees next to me as Reyna and the other generals gathered around the table, pushing around wooden figures and lines that I knew to represent platoons of soldiers. Platoons of people.

"This isn't going to be easy," Reyna began, talking to the other generals around the table. Even as a relentless optimist, I knew that was probably the understatement of the year.

Reyna stood in front of us, her gaze turned to fix on the horizon. Her usual calm was strained, her expression hardened. I admired her, like everyone did—her resolve, her strength—but today, even she couldn’t mask the gravity of what we were about to face.
“We’re outnumbered more than two to one,” she said, her voice struggling to carry over the wind as it picked up. “We'll have to strike one of their armies before they converge. But the question is which.”

I knew that question all too well. I'd seen the answer a hundred times in my mind already. Carnage. Absolute carnage. Carnage and bodies. I stared at the approaching armies, each one moving like a tide ready to crash into us. My clipboard was still blank, but I didn’t need to write anything to know the numbers. 

The clipboard was nothing more than a long-range reciever for the information I would see if I looked any of them in the eye. The numbers, ticking down, always down. MM. XX. LL.

Some of the men and woman standing here had no idea how little time some of them had left. Just how many of them had only hours to live. Some of them, standing here, I knew I wouldn't see again for a very long time.

Marcus, one of the other generals, crossed his arms, his brow furrowed for once not in anger but in tired concentration as he studied the approaching forces. 
“First is the closest,” he shouted over the wind, pointing to the left. “But even if we hit 'em real hard, we’ll be in range of the others before we can retreat.”

“It’s a murder hole,” murmured Cato, another of Reyna’s trusted lieutenants, his usually sharp tongue rendered nearly mute by the knowledge of our odds. The words 'murder hole' didn't suit him. But everyone was talking wrong today. People were using words they would never typically use. Like *minimal losses* and *risk mitigation*. “But if we wait for them to converge, we are signing our own death warrants.”

He had no idea how right he was.

Reyna’s eyes flicked to me, and I knew what was coming. She trusted my insight, trusted my abilities, but every time she asked, a part of me wished she wouldn’t. A part of her wished she didn't have to. A brief pause went by as I saw her wonder if there was any easier way to put it, but there wasn't. "Clay," she said, her voice softer, like she knew the burden it placed on me. "What do you see?"

*What do I see?* I thought, my mind darkening. *I see the dead. I see tombstones. I see their names.* 

I swallowed hard, gripping the clipboard tighter as I glanced toward the first army, the one Marcus had suggested.
*Keep it together, Graves. You can do this. You are saving lives here.*

Fifty soldiers, counting down. Seventy of our own, dead, if we hit them first.
*"Atticus Mayburn. Jonathan Snatch. Chris Wheeler."* 
The voice faded as my vision was invaded by the view of their coffins being pushed by their friends in perfect rows toward a funeral pyre.

I could feel the weight in my chest growing heavier as I moved my eyes to the second army. Ninety dead. It felt even worse.
*"Hailey Grahams. Rocky Stow. Chris Wheeler. Curduroy Maxwell."...* The names were read out by a voice I knew. Patrick Nally. Son of Hades. Announcing the dead. I heard his voice break at the last name. *"Cato...Worchester."*

More names I wouldn’t be able to forget.

Finally, my gaze settled on the third. Sixty dead, fewer than the others, but still—sixty lives. Sixty families who would never see their children, their siblings, their friends again. I imagined the scene playing out, the screams, the sound of metal clashing, the smell of blood in the air, the sound of blood sloshing back and forth in boots. The men wearing those boots not knowing how much of that blood was the enemy's and how much of it was theirs as they begged for their mothers.

*"I need a bed open, now! I have a son of Mars bleeding heavily and need bandages!"..."Open triage! Now! He's losing blood fast and needs a blood transfusion!"..."Katie, I need that blood now!"..."He's...he's gone, Andrea. He's gone."..."Andrea, I need this bed open. You're going to have to move him."..."I need this thing splinted!"*
Not a moment to even dwell on the losses until the bed was open to take another bleeding soldier. Broken legs. Gaping wounds. Gangrene. Some men would get the only mercy my father offered...peaceful deaths in their sleep. Others would gradually bleed to death in the coming hours. I'd pieced together enough from the images and the sounds to know that we were going to run out of anesthesia long before we ran out of men to treat.
*Don't think about it- don't think about it- don't think about it-!*
I shook my head, trying to shake off the images, but they clung to me like parasites.

“We attack the...third army,” I said finally, my voice rougher than I intended. Everyone turned to me, waiting for an explanation, even though none of them really wanted one. 

*Breathe, Graves, breathe.* 

I took a breath, steadying myself. "If we go after the first army, we lose seventy. The second army, ninety. The third... sixty. Either way...this isn't a battle we win. This is a battle we survive."

A silence fell over the group, the numbers settling into their minds. It wasn’t much better, but it was something. Sixty. Sixty was better than ninety. It was better than seventy. It was all we had.

“That’s still a third of our men,” Marcus muttered, running a hand through his hair as a nervous habit. “What the hell are we supposed to do with that?”

I knew he was angry before he'd even said a word. He cared deeply about his men, even if most of his men found it hard to believe that. He must've felt powerless. Three Generals. The only universe he could see was one where the third of our men that we lost were his third. And he hated losing men.

Cato didn't even speak. He just stared at me in that look of slight fear, the way he always did, thinking I was able to turn blood and bodies into casualty counts. Clearly he'd never been inside my head. 
“What we can,” Reyna said, her voice firm, cutting through the doubt. “Live, or die. That's all we can do." She knew she was convincing herself as much as she was any of us. She looked back at me, her eyes softer. “You’re sure, Clay?”

I hated that question. Of course, I was sure. I was always sure. That was the problem. “I’m sure,” I replied quietly, though the weight of those words crushed me.

Reyna nodded and turned back to the generals. “We hit the third army. We’ll have to make it fast, strike hard, and retreat before the other two can close in. We’ll take whatever advantage we can find.”

I could feel their eyes on me, judging, questioning. Some of them probably thought I was cold for spouting off numbers like they were statistics in a game.

People like Marcus, who'd always hated people making a numbers game out of the men he'd trained himself to begin with, like they didn't matter. People like Cato, who were afraid of my seemingly ability to reduce people to casualties and death tolls, like this was all some great game of Risk. To Reyna, who never liked taking the least worst option even if she knew it couldn't be helped. 

But I wasn’t cold. I was the opposite. I felt everything too much, and that’s why I had to stay in control. If I let it all in, if I let the knowledge of their deaths overwhelm me, all that would happen is people would die. Again. Like last time.

Cato spoke up, his tone more pragmatic, though there was a grim acceptance in his voice. “Third army it is, then. Let’s make sure those sixty don’t die for nothing.”

I knew what Marcus had said without even looking at him or hearing him.

*It won't be your men dying, Cato.*

The generals dispersed, going to prepare the troops, but right before Cato left, I called to Cato. He turned to look at me. I gestured for him to approach. He did.

"Feint right, not left. You'll know what I'm talking about." I whispered.

I could tell he was about to tell me that generals didn't fight on the ground. They were tactical, they moved the troops, they calculated positions. 
Then he stopped, realizing that if I was the one telling him that, then he was about to fight in a battle where little rules like who coordinated who were about to go out the window.

This wasn't going to be a conventional battle. This? This was going to be a total bloodbath.

Reyna stayed by my side a moment longer, her gaze thoughtful. “You’re holding up?” she asked, not as a commander, but as a friend.

I didn’t look at her, focusing instead on the clipboard in my hands. I could already see the names forming on it, the dead writing themselves into the list . My father's clinical style denoting every single letter of Greek in perfect rows. The numbers next to them. Seven hours. Then four. Then eight again. And then...two days. I could see it, smell it, almost taste the gangrene from the corpse. “No. But I'm getting there.”

Reyna placed a hand on my shoulder. “You carry more than any of us can imagine. But remember—you’re not responsible for their deaths. You’re helping us save the ones we can.”

I wished I could believe that. But I didn’t. Not fully. How could I, when I saw it all coming and could do so little to stop it?

"You aren't the one who has to choose who lives and dies, Arellano." I grunted through slightly clenched teeth, despite how much it hurt.

All she did was nod. It was all she could do. She left to join the others, and I stood there, the storm swirling above us, the weight of the future crushing down on my chest. Sixty men. Sixty deaths.

I felt it. It was something I'd heard children of Hades describe, that feeling of knowing someone has just crossed over. That those doors have closed behind them. But this was different. It was that familiar feeling of dread, of knowing that someone was about to cross over. But I knew it wasn't someone. It was sixty someones. I could already smell the smoke of Roman funeral pyres burning. And that steady voice, reading off the names of the dead.

The only comparable feeling there is in mortal lingo is watching a massive flock of birds drop out of the sky all at once. That something horrible was coming.

I would remember them all until the numbers in the mirror ticked down to nothing.

Because that was what happened when you were the son of Thanatos.
