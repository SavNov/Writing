Aidan was not a normal person. Even at the age of 16, he had few friends and little social interaction, not that he minded that. His fingers whirred over the keys as he wrote, mind and eyes working in tandem as he analyzed.
"He's lying." The words came out of his mouth, cold and precise, as he watched the man on the screen before him. "What do you say to those who call your mission heresy and an afront to the Muslim faith as a whole?" The woman asked. He stiffened as if wounded, then straightened again. He interlocked his fingers like he was deep in thought.

A beat passed, just for a second, and he knew in that moment that Khalid could have all the bravado and certainty in the world and still be afraid of being wrong. "...then I shall accept His correction eagerly and without resistance. But if I do not..."
Khalid's eyes locked with the camera with a fierce certainty now.
"...then it is not I who must fear the judgement of Allah. It is my home country. And for them, Allah's only mercy is instruction."
In the past few weeks, Aidan had gotten to know Khalid. His gestures, his movements, his personality. In the face of all that, Aidan had realized, the 45 men and women dead from his attacks were moreso a footnote.

They walked him out to the car—that old hatchback that looked like it had survived two world wars and maybe a civil one—and he hopped into the back seat with that familiar mix of anticipation and pessimism that wondered what the point of any of this was. Mom drove.
