Aidan was not a normal person. Even at the age of 16, he had few friends and little social interaction, not that he minded that. His fingers whirred over the keys as he wrote, mind and eyes working in tandem as he analyzed.
"He's lying." The words came out of his mouth, cold and precise, as he watched the man on the screen before him. The man was tall and lanky, maybe 6 foot 2, with perfectly cropped black hair and a winning smile as he spoke to the interviewer behind the camera. His eyes were piercingly intense, like he was boring straight into the interviewers soul. The interviewer paid it no mind.
The bottom of the video feed read Khalid $LASTNAME. He knew just barely enough Farsi to understand it was meant to mean legacy and immortality. Khalid literally meant Eternal.
"What do you say to those who call your mission heresy and an afront to the Muslim faith as a whole?" The woman asked. He stiffened as if wounded, then straightened again. He interlocked his fingers like he was deep in thought.
"I say to them that if Allah has correction in store for me, if I have strayed from His path..." A beat passed, just for a second, and he knew in that moment that Khalid could have all the bravado and certainty in the world and still be afraid of being wrong. "...then I shall accept His correction eagerly and without resistance. But if I do not..."
Khalid's eyes locked with the camera with a fierce certainty now.
"...then it is not I who must fear the judgement of Allah. It is my home country. And for them, Allah's only mercy is instruction."
In the past few weeks, Aidan had gotten to know Khalid. His gestures, his movements, his personality. The American intervention in Afghanistan that had happened when he was a teenager, the one where American soldiers had cleared an IED by deliberately detonating it, killing his entire family in the process. How he'd spent years emotionally vulnerable, joined the Taliban at 14, fought to defend his homeland. Learned what is really meant to die for what you believed in. He'd found Shariah law distasteful, though. It was crude violence to him, an attempt to force Allah's teachings onto those who did not want them, and those who did not deserve them. It was only turning people against the faith, leading more people to turn their back on their creator.
Khalid intended to change that. Without America, without the U.N., without Russia. To father his own religious sect, take Islam back to its roots, rescue people with the same faith he'd found in the middle of the desert with no purpose. The Taliban had many things wrong. But they did understand that defending your homeland was top priority, and so Khalid fully intended to take their playbook and use it to start a revolution.
In the face of all that, Aidan had realized, the 45 men and women dead from his attacks were moreso a footnote.
A call radiated out from the monitor, and initially he panicked, until he realized it was Khalid's cell in the interview. He looked pleasantly surprised, muttering in Farsi as he answered it. He exchanged pleasantries with the man on the other end. Friends. Obviously very close. It might be...oh by the way he looked it was clearly Raj, another man Aidan had grown incredibly familiar with.

Even a mildly delusional cult leader with a penchant for religious domestic terrorism had managed to find men and women who thought just as he did. He, Aidan, sighed from his office chair, finally taking his eyes off the monitor long enough to finish off the bottle of water sitting next to his keyboard. He didn't have that. His friends existed, but none of them were like him. They talked, made jokes sometimes, many of which he didn't really understand but laughed along to, but he was the fourth wheel. Every attempt at talking about the things he found interesting, that he cared about, got either looks of horror and eyes bulging as he talked half a mile over people's heads. Nobody liked spending hours every day studying cult leaders and mass shooters and rapists to understand why they did what they did. Studied how religion intercepted, warped, and changed culture. He had a bright future ahead of him. If he would only step out of his shell, they said...but he'd tried that and it never worked. He'd all but given up on the idea of having life-long friends, or even friendships that lasted longer than six or nine months. When you studied so many people for so long, normal everyday people started to look cookie-cutter.
He slowly stood out of his chair and stretched.
He had, for some strange reason, opted to go skateboarding with them. Maybe to make him mom happy, who always worried both about how little exercise he got and how hard it was for him to make friends. Maybe he had some faint hope this time would be different. Maybe because they'd promised to make it light on him, show him the ropes, nothing too intense. And maybe...just maybe...spending months in his bedroom was starting to drive him just a little bit crazy.
He showered, changed clothes, brushed his teeth, ate a filling meal, and got his skateboarding gear together, all in that order. The living room was roughly twelve feet across, and his dad lounged on the couch. Not the most academic guy, but he loved his son to the ends of the earth. Aidan smiled as his dad adjusted the helmet.
"You try to have some fun now, make some friends, okay?" His dad smiled back.
The silence passed between them.
"I know..." His dad sighed. "...I almost never understand what you're on about. It's all over my head. I just...I don't want you to live the rest of your life alone. You...you've got so much potential, but you...you worry me. If you don't ever use it to help people, really help people, then you're just gonna waste it. And I don't want you to waste it."
That cropped blonde hair and worried frown got him every single time, without fail.
"I...really hope this goes well. I want this just as badly as you do." Aidan vowed, because it was all he really could do. He was functionally living in isolation for extended periods of time. His lifestyle was considered a violation of the rights of prisoners, listed right under the banner of cruel and unusual punishment.
He wasn't lying either. He knew his current state of living was unhealthy. He just didn't know what to do about it.
They walked him out to the car—that old hatchback that looked like it had survived two world wars and maybe a civil one—and he hopped into the back seat with that familiar mix of anticipation and pessimism that wondered what the point of any of this was. Mom drove.
When he showed up at the skatepark, he was immediately intimidated. He had seen 4chan photoshop conspiracy alien invasion sites less freaky. Though maybe that was because he didn't have any idea what any of it was for. Things old movies from the 80s and 90s had called halfpipes, ramps and rails. So much concrete and steel, but every now and then you'd see a big rubber mat. And at the far corner of the park, he saw some of his friends running across the place up to him. With a new girl.
The first thing he noticed about her was the look in those dark green eyes. That intensity. Some sadness, too, but also curiosity. And just a pinch of dread. He'd never been dreaded before, unless you counted rare parties with the occasional popular girl or boy giving him this look of utter disgust. She stared at him in anticipation. She looked dangerous. She was 5'8", with long brown hair that ran all the way down to the small of her back, wearing a silver parka that was definitely concealing something just as dangerous as she was, and a pair of leather combat boots. They were heavily used, like she'd already ran a thousand miles in them. And he was still staring.
"Oh. Hello." He said, a bit awkwardly. She smiled at him and offered him a handshake.
"Hey there. You can call me Izzy." She said, throwing in a playful laugh.
"Izzy. I'm Aidan." He said, and the moment the words left his mouth, he just knew in his bones that she already knew his name. He didn't know how he knew, but he did.
He radiated awkwardness like a giant beacon of cringe.
"First time boarding?" She asked. His friends looked slightly confused, but they got the gist. He got the impression that nobody who actually skateboarded called it that.
He nodded meekly.
"Relax..." She assured him, letting her hand rest gently, reassuringly, on his arm. "You're among friends. You'll be fine."

Izzy was a show-off. Big time. She took great pleasure in doing flips and jumps and what he'd been told was called bunnyhopping. She skidded down a big and tall and intimidating rail to stand in front of him, handing him a skateboard.
"You try. I'll walk you through it step-by-step, don't worry." She promised with that kind smile. The others helped and had plenty of their own fun, but it seemed, somehow, that Izzy was in a league all her own. She needed no help. His friends mentioned in private, while she was off having a blast on the biggest ramp in the park, that while she was clearly a pro, she was also...getting nearly every name wrong. Walking the dog was a yo-yo trick, it had nothing to do with skateboarding. He was intrigued. She was very interested in helping him learn, and in the words of Craig, "smoking hot, man!"
Hours went by. He was physically drained from all the activity, but he also had maybe had a bit of fun, and not just because a very attractive girl was being very supportive and kind to him. They sat together on the big half-pipe, their legs dangling over the edge. He had barely managed to hear Izzy mutter under her breath: *God I'm turning into Emma.*
He had no idea who that was, but she seemed concerned by the idea of turning into this person.
By some act of God, he found himself smiling on the way home. Mom looked pleased.
"Make yourself a new friend?" She asked him, keeping one eye on the road and one on him.
He considered it for a moment as he buckled himself in more properly.
"I don't know yet. She seems kind." He said.
"I agree. Very kind. And very new. I don't think I've seen her around here before. I noticed she's very athletic. Did she tell you how long she's been 'boarding'?" She asked him.
He thought it over. He'd been watching Izzy the whole time without really thinking about it, but now that he did, she seemed very experienced. Able to push her body to the limit, make it do what she wanted it to, but not an experienced skateboarder. Something about that image refused to compute in his head. The way she'd called the tricks and moves by the wrong names like she was improvizing, the way she'd known his name before he'd even opened his mouth, the way she seemed to be watching him just as much as he'd been watching her.
"Not long. I think she just has a lot of natural talent." He answered. "It's like she's some kind of prodigy."
"A prodigy? You sure? Maybe she's just been skating in a different town all this time, and you've only just seen her routine." She suggested.
"Maybe. But that doesn't feel right. Something feels...off...about this." He confessed.
"Off? How so?" She gave him a quizzical look as she pulled into their neighborhood.
"I don't know. But I think I'm about to find out." He answered.

Shower. Change clothes. Dinner. Study for school. Bedroom. The details blurred into incoherence. He slept. He woke up. He tossed his sheets to the side haphazardly. As he did every day, he changed into jeans and a t-shirt and sat at his laptop. The Kaleidoscope of religious symbols that formed a circular logo on his lockscreen revealed itself in vibrant color. He opened a tab.
A message appeared in front of him on the screen:
**Introvert Academy Welcomes You, Aidan Travis.**
**We Only Accept The Best.**
**You have 48 hours to do the impossible.**
**Good luck.**
"Um...what?" He asked, too stunned to even speak. He had learned about a lot of obscure academy institutions, but Introvert Academy? Never that one. 
The message did not answer, did not explain itself, just waited there, staring back at him.
"I...okay then." He conceded, not knowing what else to do. He clicked the bolded text on the screen. He got a confirmation box. Not the usual stuff, though.
*Are you sure you wish to proceed? Once you begin, there will be no going back and no turning back the clock. This will be your most difficult challenge so far.*
His mouse hovered over the two options.
| **Yes** | **No** |
He allowed his mouse to hover over **No**.
