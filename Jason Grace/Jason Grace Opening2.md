It was the sound of thunder that woke Jason up. He rubbed the sleep out of his eyes as he eyed his sleeping cabin mates, even though he hadn't slept well in months now. Always the same nightmare. Always that same night. It was over now. He was safe. He had learned the lesson of the wolf, he had the winds now, the sky was his friend. The sky would help him like it always did.

_Something isn't right. Something's off._ He thought, feeling the words in his gut before they made it to his head.

He straightened himself, straightening his back as the Fifth Cohort's barracks snored peacefully. Though, peacefully was a strong word. The early morning light fought its daily battle with the storm clouds as the Eastern cold front moved in on Camp Jupiter pristine glass ceiling of Jupiter's favor. He breathed in slowly as he got up out of bed and began putting his armor on for Praetor Gwen's training.

Tender fingers traced the leather strap of his breastplate. The metal clasp linked with his fingers. No spark.

_Something's missing._ He realized, then quickly buried his nerves. _Without evidence, suspicions solve nothing._

That didn't stop his fingers from getting clammy. He rubbed them together to warm them up and to try a calming technique Gwen had taught him years ago. The repetitive motion eased his mind. That, and knowing the electricity was between his fingertips meant it wasn't somewhere worse. Yet this time, no spark.

_You are tired, and just woke from a nightmare. Thinking about it will solve nothing._ He chided himself.

That managed to do absolutely nothing. The hairs on the back of his neck stood up, which he wanted badly to believe was the electricity in the air, but there was none to speak of. Still, duty to New Rome did not accept excuses. It didn't accept much of anything besides total obedience, zero fear, zero weakness, zero mercy. Those were the lesson that the wolf goddess Lupa had taught him at the Wolf House.

_The nail that sticks out gets hammered down._ She had told him.

Praetor Gwen had only added to the sentiment.

"You think yourself a lone wolf, Jason, but you are not." She had said, putting her hands on his shoulders. "You are part of a pack. If you cannot learn to be part of a team, you are a threat, not an ally."

Mark Stoll knew a lot about that. Jason had been three, and Mark had resisted the idea of being part of a wolf back. He was an independent, rebellious type, all instinct and hunches. And one day, Mark had gone to sleep in the Wolf House, and the wolves had made certain he never woke up.

The memory made Jason shiver. He strapped on his armor, but he was so stuck in his own head that he snapped the strap.

"Gods of Rome." He swore quietly as he replaced the strap quickly with a spare. The pads of his bare feet left thumping sounds on the dirt before he strapped on his sandals and headed out into the rainy early morning. He turned his cursing into a prayer. "Give me discipline to face this day as I have faced those before it."

The words of Varus' President of decades ago echoed in the back of his mind like a twisted reminder of what the Legion called loyalty.

"Ask not what your country can do for you, but what you can do for your country."

He stared at the stormy grey sky, but something was missing even now. Though he didn't want to mention it.

_It's not possible for something like that to just vanish. Perhaps the storm has not arrived yet._

Having no other real option, he closed his eyes and focused on his heartbeat. If there was any electricity, it was going to be _there_.

_81 beats per minute._ He counted as the rain soaked into his clothes, which was somehow even worse than the heart rate alone.

81 beats per minutes. No variation, no change, no leaping upwards to 95 and back down to 70. He shook the rain out of his hair.

_No, something is **very** wrong about all of this. Children of Jupiter don't get soaked by rain unless they allow it._
