Jason had never imagined he'd be standing up to a goddess, but he'd realized something. He didn't have a problem. He was happy here. Mrs. Potts had turned out to be a Greek monster out to kill Leo and Piper before they knew how to use their powers. Jason hated people that exploited the weak, whether they were gods, emperors, monsters or demigods. He also happened to be the only person strong enough to level the playing field.

Mrs. Potts had eaten voltage when she'd gotten cocky. Jason had used the winds to fly Piper onto the roof of the Wilderness School. He'd taught Leo how to work with Celestial Bronze even if the kid hadn't understood that was the metal in question. He was fine for the first time in a while. He had earned this. He had bled, and suffered, and sacrificed for ten years in the Legion to have this. He didn't want to go back to New Rome, or attend their university. He wanted this.

Then, like the mother-in-law in every joke about married couples since time immemorial, Hera had shown up. She had sung him sungs of glory and honor and service to the Legion for his bleeding Roman heart. But he wasn't Roman. Not here. He had served the Legion ten years, he'd risen to the rank of Praetor, and when push had come to shove, he'd killed a titan with his bare hands and not a single innocent life had been lost.

He owed the First Legion, because that was what he called it now, absolutely nothing.

So when Hera had told him about the next battle, the next war, the one he had dreaded before and knew was coming because if Kronos could've simply grabbed a random demigod and freed himself that way he would've returned centuries ago. No, something had changed below. Something had awakened. She told him it was Gaia. And Jason realized it in that moment.

"Why are you here?" He asked.

"Well, I am a goddess, and you are a—" She said, only for him to cut her off mid-sentence.

"Shut up." He said, voice calm and gentle like a storm receding.

"You— what just— what do you—" She sputtered incoherently.

"I know that maybe your English isn't as good as your Greek, so maybe you need a bit of explanation. Shut up means shut your mouth and stop talking." Jason said, his voice still just as calm and level.

Her mouth opened and closed for a few seconds like a fish out of water before she finally settled for closing it.

"Wonderful. Now, here's the hard part. You have to stop relying on your prestige and your clout and how powerful you are and how meager I am and how lucky I am to do your bidding. You have to ask yourself the one question you've never had to ask yourself. What's in it for me? Now, why are you here?" He asked, like he was talking to a rebellious child in need of counsel.

The minute of silence that followed was worth its weight in gold.

"Alright. Fine. I… Gaia is rising. She is already beginning to stir. A new Great Prophecy has just been delivered a matter of weeks ago, and— are you tapping your foot?" Hera said softly, then turned it into a growl.

"I know, it's hard, having to finally cut down the theatrics and tell me why you're here. But I don't think you quite understand your situation. Continue." Jason said.

She was on the verge of seething, but steadied herself again.

"I need… your help. The second line reads 'To storm or fire the world must fall'. You are the only one I know who might be called a storm, so I assume it's about you." She said, growing tired of this.

"One of two, you mean." He said.

She raised an eyebrow. "Two?"

"My sister. Thalia Grace. You may have heard of her." He said.

"I… I know who Thalia Grace is, Jason. I assumed—" She growled, only to be cut off again.

"Yeah, and that's what always gets you Greeks in trouble. You assume, assume, assume, and assume again, always thinking you understand how it's all going to play out. You don't. Nobody does. Fate is not yet written. Here's a lesson for you, Greek goddess, use that immortal time scale power of yours for once in your life and spend a few days thinking things through. Instead of sending a heat wave through Arizona, think it through. You've seen empires rise and fall, so start acting like it. You tell us we're ants to you, yet when you've got a problem, it's always to us that you start calling." Jason said, now fully on a roll.

"So take a lesson from Reyna Ramirez-Arellano for once in your life. Give me one good reason not to tell you to shove it where the Eagle don't bite." Jason smiled. "Use that divine magic brain of yours to really give it a good thinking through. You've got twelve seconds before I see if this retired Praetor can still hit a moving target with lightning."
