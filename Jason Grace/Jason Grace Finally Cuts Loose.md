Jason looked across the battlefield.

Frank Zhang charged through the horde of monsters thirty times his size, not expecting victory, just doing what he knew was right.

The next Praetor of New Rome. Right there. Leading. Throwing his all into a battle that he could not win because he did good by his Legion instead of what was easy.

Jason nodded once in affirmation.

"That man won't quit as long as he can draw a breath," he said, meeting Krios' dark eyes with eyes that were as calm as the eye of a hurricane,.
"None of my friends will. None of Rome ever will."

He raised his hand.
The sky churned with power.

Lightning arced across it like it was awaiting orders, eager to leap into the Son of Jupiter's fingers and cause some damage.

"Me?" Jason said, as thunder rolled on the heels of the storm's lightning. "I've got a different problem."

Krios laughed at him. "You're outmatched. I am a titan, and you are a demigod. Your friends will be overrun, slaughtered, and fed to my brothers as spoils of our victory."

Jason gave no answer. He stepped forward, saw the marble pillar of the palace groaning under the strain of a building waiting to fall, and cracked it with his bare fist. The marble split with the sound of a cannon.

"I live in a world made of cardboard," he said. "Always holding back. Always calculating. Always restraining myself. 'Rein it in, Jason.' 'Don't lose control, Jason.' 'You might hurt someone, Jason.'"
Electricity buzzed the tips of his fingers as he felt the power roaring through him, desperate to escape him. Bolts of lightning arced across his wrists as he pressed his palms together. His eyes glowed the color of a hurricane.

He gently, carefully, moved a young member of the Fourth Cohort's dead body out of the way.

"Well, look around you, Krios. Everyone I could hurt is gone or dead. Collateral damage is no longer a concern."

And after 11 years of service to the Legion, Jason finally allowed the power to flow wildly.

Lightning surged across his arms, then spiraled out across his chest and back as it sought a ground point. Wind surged into the throne room as he finally allowed his emotions to spill out.

"What we have here is a rare opportunity for me to cut loose. Stop holding back. Because you can take it, can't you, titan? I don't have to worry about killing you. Dying is the one thing you titans can't manage to do."

Jason counted, letting his anger fill the air around him as a steady breeze quickly became extreme weather.

"Twenty. Forty. Sixty. Eighty."

The wind rose in intensity, from 20 miles per hour to forty, sixty, eighty. The air howled. The floor vibrated. The storm roared in response to the son of the sky god's call. A brick tore itself out of the ceiling and threatened to hit Krios in the face.

"One hundred. One-fifty. Two hundred."

The wind currents tore at the building, threatening to dismantle the foundation as marble and stone and limestone came apart into a whirlwind or rubble. Yet Jason as unharmed. The pressure difference between the near-vacuum he was standing in and the EF4 level winds around him should've burst his lungs, but instead he only continued to breath, in and out, slowly and steadily.

A flat-screen TV whipped in from the hallway, caught in the torrent. Jason lifted two fingers. The screen froze midair, suddenly unperturbed by the winds in the throne room strong enough to rip the fortress clean off the limestone foundation, and settled into his palm like a bird landing on its owner's arm, still lit.

A new anchor shouted over static:

"The National Weather Service has declared a National State of Emergency. Meteorologists are confirming reports of an EF6 tornado heading west through Plymouth County, New York, and a rapidly intensifying Category 2 hurricane forming over the Bay Area. Seek shelter immediately. This is not a drill! Gather your family in your home's most interior room…"

Jason pointed to the radar. A swirling green and red vortex, centered directly over Mount Othrys, its eye covering the exact spot in the throne room that Jason was standing in.

"I don't need to brag," he said. "Not about sea monsters. Not about Typhon. Not about becoming a Praetor at twelve."
He tapped the eyes of the storm on the screen.
"That… is my self-portrait."

Then he turned to the Titan of the West and slapped him across the face — open-handed.

The shockwave knocked the wind out of the Titan and sent a concussive blast down the mountainside, shattering windows, setting off car alarms, and setting off early tornado warning systems across four and a half miles. The sirens began going off.

Krios ran a finger down his cleanly dislocated jaw, smelling blood.


"How—" Krios sputtered.

"And *that*, Titan of the West," Jason growled, "was my version of a warning shot."

Krios stumbled back, disoriented, trying to reset his jaw, struggling to stay standing in the sheer winds. Jason looked completely unbothered — no, he looked like he was finally warming up. Like his entire career as a demigod of the Twelfth Legion of Rome had been him never having to push himself hard enough to actually strain his body.

"Two-fifty." Jason finished. And then with one final word he prepared his first real strike ever since he'd joined the Legion. "Three hundred."

And then, though Jason wasn't a child of Pluto like Hazel was, he could sense it. They both could. Beneath. The stone joists of the throne room were cracking under the strain.

The news anchor seemed genuinely, truly terrified for her life as the TV circled them, powered by nothing but the electricity coursing through Jason's veins.

"The National Weather Service has updated its previous statement. The hurricane developing in the Bay Area previously labeled as a Category 2 has now earned the classification of Category 6. The Coast Guard is being deployed to search for survivors. Hold your children close, ladies and gentlemen, this may be California'a Katrina."

Cracks like stone veins burst through the floor as Jason circled the wincing Krios. Nobody was going to die in this storm. It was going to be all property damage. Jason had not given the winds permission to harm people.

"Time for you and me to find out the answer to the question I've been asking myself since the age of six. What happens if I, Jason Grace, really, truly put all my strength into actualy killing someone. I'm done trying to hit you, I'm going to hit you. I'm going to throw my first real hard punch."

Jason arched his back, bowed his legs, took a sprinting stance as he felt the electricity of the punch, the raw power of it, build in the balls of his feet, push against the tendons in his heels, build with the force of his legs pushing off the ground, build through all the muscles in his chest, stream through his shoulders and finally pour through his muscular arms and out of his clenched fists.

Jason ran with everything he had, swung back, and landde the blow against Krios' ribcage with the force of a lightning bolt.
It knocked Krios off his feet, high into the air, crashing into the ground on his backside thirty feet away, but Jason was already winding up for another punch and hadn't lost any of the momentum he was running with.
As soon as Krios stood up, another blow slammed into his stomach, shattering ribs in rapid succession. Jason went again, and again. Krios' head broke a wall, then his abdomen fractured the back of Kronos' throne.

Then Jason redirected his final strong punch from Krios down into the floor. All the stone joists keeping the fortress anchored to its mountain foundation broke at once. The 300 mph winds picked up the slack.

Kronos' Fortress on Mount Othrys caught air.

Jason was tired for the first time in years, now not from politics or from leading, but from sheer exertion.
But Jason knew he had one last trick in him.

"I am Jason Grace, Son of Jupiter, Praetor of the Twelfth Legion and New Rome, Standard-bearer of the Fifth Cohort, and I HAVE HAD ENOUGH!"

Krios was on the ground now, nursing wounds that Jason didn't even want to think about. More broken bones than healthy ones. The electricity within Jason was already beginning to die down, and so was the wind.

So Jason stood over the broken Titan, lightning bleeding from his broken knuckles like ichor as he moved his broken fingers over the Titan's throat.

"Please… " Krios begged, "…spare me."

Krios coughed up a patch of black ichor.

"A wolf is strong." Jason's voice was quiet now. Steady. Calm.

He picked up Krios by the throat. The swordsman had been expecting a duel. Sword against sword. And Jason had fought him that way for a while, but knew he was outmatched. This was where Jason was strongest.

Two bare hands, a bolt of lightning, and an oath to keep with a final breath.

"A wolf has no fear." He said, almost silent as he whispered. He pushed the titan's neck as far as it would go.

"And a wolf, above all things," he breathed, holding Krios' gaze in a long, terrifying stare, "has no mercy."

And Jason Grace pushed the titan's neck just a little farther, causing a cracking sound, then a pop, as the titan dying and faded into primordial dust.

The fortress landed back on the mountain with a titanic thud, and the impact reduced it to mere rubble.
