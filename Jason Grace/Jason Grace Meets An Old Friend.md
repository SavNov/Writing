Jason knew something was off with the winds, and while the lightning was a problem he couldn't really solve yet, figuring out the winds really only left him with one man to visit.

"Mr. Chase?" Jason called out as he opened the door of the one mortal in the San Francisco Bay area who loved the winds and military history enough to actually own a Sopwith Camel biplane.

The household, as usual, was filled to the brim with artifacts. On one wall, old bayonets and rifles lay, disarmed years ago. On another, miniatures of WWII, WWI, and Civil War soldiers stood in piles that told of frequent use.

"I told you, Jason, pick one! Frederick, or Dr. Chase! Mr. Chase makes me feel old!" A voice called out from the study. Jason walked through the house. Bobby and Matthew were still at school, leaving Ms. Chase sitting at a table playing solitaire.

She waved at the demigod in Roman armor without so much as an eyebrow raise. "Give the boy a break, Frederick, he's in gear this time. And you ARE an old man!"

He adjusted his strap and resettled his gladius at his belt.

"Have you received mail recently?" He asked.

A beat passed.

"Let me take a wild guess, boy." Ms. Chase smiled at him. "You need to go west to face a god who has turned, find what was stolen, and see it safely returned."

His mouth hung open for a couple of seconds.

"The answer to your question, hero, is yes. We have. As much as my daughter delights in believing that I want her dead, I do actually care whether she lives or dies. Here, let me pull it out while my husband drags himself out of his study." She said, putting the cards away and opening a drawer under the table, pulling out a piece of parchment.

Frederick slowly crawled out from the corner office, holding a miniature airplane like it was a prized relic, and sat at the table as Ms. Chase straightened the letter out on the table. Jason looked at it curiously. The writing was pretty neat and orderly even if many of the words were misspelled. In the margins, though, little bits of Greek were scrawled. Ms. Chase appeared amused by the scratchings.

Jason had known for a few years now that Frederick's daughter was a demigod of some kind, but never details. She only ever sent letters, describing San Francisco as a bad place for demigods to be, which had always confused him. She liked talking about how Ms. Chase had driven her away, but she was making friends now and camp was nice, which was the other reason that Lupa's little tid-bit about demigods always being of two camps stuck in the back of his throat.

"What's the Greek?" Jason asked, a completely fair question in his opinion.

Ms. Chase stared at him in confusion for a couple of seconds, then sighed. "Every time. Without fail. The Romans are the ones to do with Latin, not Greek."

She began going through the passages, translating the notes in the margins as she went.

"Let's see here. 'Grover is worried about the Great Prophecy.', 'I love you Dad.', 'Something important has gone missing but I can't share details…', 'I met this boy named Percy and I think I like him.', ah, and like a spider in a kitchen cabinet, there it is. 'Why do you still stay with her?'" She read.

"You… understand Greek?" He asked, eyebrow raised.

"As much as demigods like to pretend otherwise, we mortals are not stupid. It might take us a tad longer, but we can do many of the same things. We just have to go the long way." She answered, like she'd given this answer a thousand times.

"You… don't seem surprised by any of this." He noticed.

Ms. Chase got up, walked to the wall, and stabbed a seven foot tall steel Valkyrie spear through the coffee table.

"What makes you say that?" She asked, without breaking eye contact.

Jason backed up.

"Um… a hunch, I guess."

Frederick decided to join in on the conversation.

"So what do you need, Jason?" Dr. Chase said, and he realized he'd nearly forgotten.

"I'm on a quest. The First Cohort's Eagle has been stolen. And I'm having trouble controlling the winds. I thought Frederick could help." He said, now sounding almost embarrassed.

"I noticed that. I figured you would appear here, but under what circumstances escaped me. Hang on, I wrote it down."

Frederick scrambled back to his study, searching through book after book, and got three shelves down before finally settling on an old notebook. He brought it to the table, and the notes were… not pretty. They were little more than scribbles with jargon and acronyms written in the margins and pictures mixed in.

"After learning to read Frederick's handwriting," Ms. Chase said wryly, "Greek was a breeze."
