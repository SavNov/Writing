Praetor Gwen marched Jason into the temple of his father literally at sword point. Octavian stood over a pile of stuffed animals, reading a huge codex written in a combination of Greek and Latin, looking into the future… allegedly.

"Praetor Gwen." Octavian said, like he was tasting the name, as he turned around to face them. "And Jason Grace, Son of Jupiter, Fifth Cohort. To what do I owe the honor?"

Jason wasn't sure about any of this. His gut told him one thing, but he had no evidence, no proof, and wasn't about to make a choice without all the information. He stood in the middle of the temple. Gwen smiled back at Octavian.

"Octavian, Legacy of Apollo, I hear your father is moving in the Senate to name you Augury. Does that story hold weight?" Gwen asked, diplomatic and reasonable and proper.

"It certainly would not surprise me if he were. My family has served the Legion a century now, we have earned the right to make moves where it suits us." Octavian fired back.

He placed the stuffed fox on the pedestal, and closed the book.

"Enough about me, though. I am a humble servant of the Legion and my father, Praetor. What can I offer you?"

Gwen straightened herself and Jason trembled.

"Tell Octavian your suspicions, Jason." She nudged him.

A tiny bead of sweat trickled down his back as he eyed the statue of Jupiter high above him. Marble. Intimidating. Powerful. Yet also the only real connection he had to the lightning in his veins, to the sky, to power. He didn't know anything. His gut told him a lot of things, but he didn't trust it. Instincts and guts were not the Roman way, Lupa had taught him. Evidence, laws, rules, planning. That was how Rome had survived. By spending 700 years bleeding and dying for light they would never see to eventually lay the limestone foundation of the Roman Republic.

Hunches turned Legionnaires into bodies.

Jason's strength had taught him a lot about bodies.

"It seems… irrational, I will admit. More of a… gut feeling, I suppose. A hunch. Maybe. Maybe I'm wrong—" Jason began, only to feel Gwen smack him in the back with the butt of her gladius.

He could feel the words even if he couldn't hear them.

*Get to the point.*

"I believe that the First Cohort's Eagle isn't just missing. I suspect… maybe… possibly… it's been stolen."

Octavian's eyes widened a fraction, then he steadied himself. He walked over to the codex, turning pages.

"I see. Intriguing as a theory. You will need more evidence than you currently possess to convince the Senate, however. I am not my father, I cannot simply call for a quest on a whim."

Octavian slowly arrived at a page of Ancient Greek. A prophecy older than any of them. Probably older than the building they stood in.

"I can, however, issue a prophecy to get you moving in the right direction. Let's see what we have here, something to do with theft. Something being stolen is what you need, yes?"

Jason nodded meekly, and she steadied him, putting her hand on his shoulder.

With all the snark of a boy who knew he had something they needed, Octavian began reading the Greek.

"You shall go west, and face the god who has turned. You shall find what was stolen, and see it safely returned." He said, starting to circle them as he began reciting the words from memory now.

This seemed to be going well. This seemed reasonably straightforward. That worried him.

"You shall be betrayed by one who calls you a friend. And you shall lose what matters most to you in the end."

That was what had worried him. Little catches like that. He took in a breath, and Gwen released the one she'd been holding.

The Auger-in-waiting closed the book again and looked them over, grinning wide, but trying to look apologetic.

"That doesn't seem so fun now, does it? Sounds awful. Then again, your bleeding golden heart has never been stopped by that before, has it? Or are you not the man who turned your back on nearly a thousand years of Roman heritage in the First Cohort, Son of Jupiter, to serve the backwater Fifth because you insist on betting on dark horses?"

Jason looked down again, but this time, Gwen forced his chin back up.

"Was that an accusation, Octavian?" Gwen asked sternly.

Octavian instantly looked more apologetic and concerned.

"An accusation? No, of course not. They are my comments as a private citizen. You of all people, Praetor, should know that if I suspected anything, I would run straight to the Senate with it."

Gwen ground her teeth for hardly an instant before she regathered her bearings.

"Yes, you would, Legacy of Apollo. That is precisely the behavior of yours that concerns me."

Jason felt it in his gut again. That nudge. Something was off about this.

*He knows something. Something important he's not sharing. He's too smug and arrogant for a senator's son who only just discovered the Eagle may have been stolen. He knew. I don't know how, but he knew.*

Yet he held his tongue, kept the peace.

"Thanks, Octavian. You have been very helpful." He said, heading towards the exit.

"On the contrary," He answered, making Jason pause, "you have done a great deal to help me, Son of Jupiter. And for that, you have my immense gratitude. Engage in your investigation. I will inform the Senate of your suspicions at once. Rest assured, Jason, that your report will be invaluable to recovering the Eagle, and with it, the pride of the Twelfth Legion Fulminata."

And with that, Jason was told to go.
