Jason was done. Done with this quest, done with the Senate, done with the Trojan Sea Monster, done with the prophecy, and done with the legacy of Neptune that had taken half his team for leverage and then kept them after Jason had made good on his side of the bargain.

The ship rocked back and forth beneath his boots as his Probatio seamen and seawomen—left and right—sharpened their swords like he'd taught them. He hadn't been given an A-team. He hadn't even been given a B-team. He'd been given a set of newbies from the Fifth Cohort who couldn't even sail… for a quest to defeat a Trojan _Sea_ Monster. A set of novices who couldn't even fight… to battle an army.

So Jason had acted, been wrong, failed…

…learned, adapted, overcome, moved on.

And the Son of Jupiter had built one.

The large wooden vessel slowly drifted closer to the enemy vessel where his team's other half lay in chains in the hull. So his team had come up with an idea of their own. Chains and harpoons, welded together just the right way. Taken from his time in Aeolus' Palace studying and learning about every time Rome had ever had to fight at sea. A lesson of the First Punic War.

Except this time, Jason intended to win.

He had played by Aaron's rules for far too long. Trusted him to keep his word, risked his team on strategy to gain safe passage around Circle's Island, been wrong, lost six good men and women playing fair, recovered. Aaron had betrayed him, sold him out. He was three months behind schedule already.

Over the intercom, the traitor taunted him.

"Came to surrender, Jason Grace? You don't seem the type. I'm sure you have a lot of words you'd like to say to me right now."

Jason rubbed the edge of the coin Lupa had given him in his left pocket, then brandished the sword in his right.

"Just four." Jason said, gesturing to his team as they gathered together the harpoons and aimed.

He was intrigued.

"Oh, and what would they be?" The sailor cooed, grinning.

Jason mouthed to his team.

_Now._

"Prepare to be boarded!"

Six harpoons embedded themselves permanently in the opposing ship's hull.

Thomas was first, throwing his whole weight into it, back locked as his feet pushed forward, straining every muscle in his arms with everything he had.

Sarah, not to be out-done, took the end she'd just attached to the rigging and turned the huge wheel, rapidly winding the chain around it and closing the distance between the grip and the harpoon tip.

Connor pulled as hard as he could, but in the end, he and Patrick ended up helping each other pull the chains in a joint grip.

Michael and Lily, children of Vulcan to a fault, had woven their ends to the ship's anchor and simply dropped it into the sea below.

Six Probatios pulled on the harpoons, and the two ships collided _hard_, the gap closing like a huge clamp. They were in this together, for better and for worse.

The legacy immediately stumbled backward, but his men were tough, arming themselves with swords as they dealt with this new threat.

"For your acts of valor today, Probatio Sarah, Connor, Michael, Patrick, Thomas and Lily, I grant you the rank of Legionnaire." Jason boomed, feeling the electricity spark beneath his skin.

Connor looked back at him in shock. "You… aren't a Centurion. You don't have the authority!"

Jason stared right back at him.

"Centurion Dakota is in chains. As such, as the oldest Legionnaire currently present, I _am_ a Centurion, Legionnaire Connor."

Connor smiled.

"So if I say you're a Legionnaire, until Dakota says otherwise, that is your rank."

"Alright… Centurion Jason."

"Good. Fifth Cohort, let's go get our team back." Jason ordered, then dropped his voice to a growl. "And show this coward what the Fifth Cohort Fulminata does when you double-cross them."

Below them, though none of them could see it, a single bubble popped at the surface of the water between the two ships.

Jason sensed it, and paused, then waved it off. A pocket of air, nothing more.

Sarah, Connor, Michael, Patrick, Thomas and Lily all stood at the edge of the boat, looking back at him.

Jason stood right behind them.

"Jump!"

And the six of them jumped, him using the winds to propel them over the side and onto the enemy ship as they swung their swords and began the fight. Jason followed right behind, leaping through the cold sea air and landing right on target.

His team would deal with the soldiers. Now it was time for Jason to do some pulling.

Aaron quickly fled through the door and into the lower decks, shutting the five thousand pound watertight steel door shut in Jason's face.

"I am tired, late, and done giving you second chances, legacy of Neptune. Open this door." Jason said, his voice cold and flat as the winds shifted above him. Arcs danced across his pupils as he flexed the muscles in his back and legs.

The door remained closed.

"Fine. We will do this the hard way." Jason muttered, and stood back. He took a harpoon from one of the barrels aboard, broke the glass of the door with the tip, and hung it on the window frame. He pulled as hard as he could.

Electricity and thunder ripped across the creases in his back, arms and legs where the blood leaking out of his pores sparked and caught as he pulled with all his strength, pulling the five thousand pound watertight steel door straight off its hinges bolt by bolt. With a sound like thunder cracking, the door buckled outward and came off at Jason's feet. The hallway was open now.

Aaron's pants got several shades darker.

The Son of Jupiter cracked his knuckles.

"Legacy of Nep—" Jason began, only to stop and correct himself. "No. That isn't what you are. Legacy of Pavor, I would like to re-negotiate. Here's my final offer: Release my crew, all six of them, Centurion Dakota included." Jason offered, even though it didn't remotely resemble an offer.

"Or?" Aaron squeaked.

"Or," Jason said, feeling electricity waltz up and down his sword before he reigned it in again, "I kill you where you stand."

The sea churned beneath them, but Jason steadied his nerves. This was _not_ the team to worry about being on Neptune's bad side. He had a job to do.

"Got it." Aaron whispered. "I'll get right on that, Jason Grace, Sir."

He stumbled backward and threw the gate of the brig open so quickly that the hinge bent, rapidly escorting the rest of Jason's crew out, suddenly the most polite sailor on the Sea of Monsters.

"I am Jason Grace, Son of Jupiter, Centurion of the Fifth Cohort." He said, turning back to look at the others that the first half of his crew had just disarmed. "If you ever think about double-crossing someone again, remember this. Everything that enters the wind… ends up here."

And Jason pointed his gladius at his right ear, then his left.

Something wasn't right about this, though. He had been sent here for a purpose.

_The Sea Monster of Troy shall be slain by the pride of Juno._ He remembered, and suddenly found himself hesitating again.

_Yeah, but where is it?_ He thought.

And then an explosion rattled the vessel so violently that Jason dropped his own sword.

_Well,_ Jason thought, _schist._

A massive tentacle ripped open the roof of the hallway, and Jason found himself face to face with a squid the size of the Senate building. Water drenched them all as Dakota fought to crawl out of the brig while there was still a brig to crawl out of.

The Son of Jupiter promptly crapped his pants.

He grabbed his sword, and entered a defensive stance.

"Neptune," He whispered, drenched in seawater and sweat, "whatever I have done to—"

Jason remembered the way he'd promised Neptune on his journey to duel Octavian that if Neptune struck him, he didn't know who would win in a war amongst the gods, but it wouldn't be Neptune.

"—whatever else I have done to offend you…" Jason corrected himself mid-sentence. "…I am sorry. Very sorry. I mean no harm."

For a second, he thought his apology had worked. The Kraken seemed to be hesitating. Then he recognized the look on its face. One he had received many times from Octavian… and Praetor Samuel.

_Uh… did this guy just blow in from Stupid Town?_

At what was left of the bridge, Aaron had already fled to the radio.

"MAYDAY! MAYDAY! MAYDAY! I am Captain Aaron Nell of the Dutchess of Neptune! My hull is flooded and I'm taking on water—" The Captain was screaming in a panic suitable of a Legacy of Pavor, only to be cut off as the ship rocked forward and his own hat smacked him in the face.

The Kraken almost killed Nell outright, but something stopped it. Its tentacle paused feet above Captain Nell's head. The man realized in horror what it was doing.

"No, not like this. It's not supposed to end like this!" He panted, trying to leave the bridge, but the tentacle blocked both exits.

Thomas moved a beam so that his sister could free herself from the brig.

"What's it doing? Why isn't it killing him?" Thomas asked him.

"Neptune." Jason said. "Sea god is very particular about Captains going down with the ship."

Nell tried the radio again, but the Kraken knocked it harmlessly beyond his grasp. It bounced against the front railing of the bridge, changing the channel in the process.

"—recent developments in Tropical Storm Jason have seen it progress to a Category One Typhoon. As such, the National Hurricane Center has raised its designation to Typhoon Jason. The Coast Guard has released an advisory that all sailors in the projected path should take necessary shelter."

He looked to his left, and to his right. Nell's crew had already taken all the lifeboats in the frenzy.

Sarah clawed at the deck. "Jason! I can't— I can't hold on much longer!"

There was thirty feet of distance.

Jason was never going to make it. Not when Dakota was already injured, there were empty crates everywhere, and the ship was already on the verge of capsizing. Twenty degrees of tilt, and only getting worse. He needed to be in two places at once. Or, more accurately, six. The Kraken had just rammed Michael against the deck and the boy wasn't getting back up.

_The clock is ticking, Jason._ He thought. _If you're going to do something, now would be the time._

He was terrified. Not of dying, but of losing control, letting the winds within him fully run wild, letting the lightning go free. The collateral damage would kill his crew long before it left a dent in the Kraken.

Jason eyed the Kraken as the ship quickly fell apart under the onslaught. He needed to do something. Yesterday.

Her grip failed. Sarah fell.

"No." He grunted. "I'm done being afraid. People are going to die!"

_I need a harpoon…_ He thought, searching around, and saw a few in a nearby barrel.

He dove for it, but the beast knocked it out of his reach with a tentacle and the barrel toppled into the sea.

His muscles ached, his vision blurred, and his ears rung from the explosions of the battle. Then he remembered. He closed his eyes, shut out the world, and listened.

He felt the hum of the sea, heard the waves rock, but shut it all out and listened more closely. He listened to the sky above.

_The weather report._ He realized. _Pacific Ocean. Sea of Monsters. Typhoon._

Jason pulled on every volt he could get his hands on, and made one giant harpoon for one massive Kraken. The explosion rocked the deck as light bored into his eyelids. The proximity should've deafened him from the thunder, but it didn't. Instead, he heard a massive splash, and dared to open his eyes.

The massive Kraken had a giant tunnel bored through its head right where its brain had been.

Jason ran like the wind straight to the port side where Sarah had fallen. Looking down, he saw something odd.

Unconscious? Yes. Nasty head wound? Also yes. But alive? Yes.

She'd aimed for the anchor as she'd fallen, knowing that it would seriously injure her but would break her fall. Unconscious on an anchor was leagues better than being in the sea.

He leapt downward immediately and the winds slowed him to a stop so he landed on the anchor. The others were already lifting her onto their own ship. For some reason, it was sinking, too. Then he remembered.

"In this together. For better, for worse." He grunted, remembering the very clever, very permanent harpoons linking the two ships together.

He waved at Connor, who didn't acknowledge the gesture. Jason realized what had happened.

"He looked." Jason whispered. They just nodded solemnly.

Jason gathered his crew, all of them, together at the edge of the vessel as two broken ships began to collapse into the sea. There was barely enough boards and steel between either chunk to earn the title of ship anymore. He found himself looking into the sky, gathering his courage.

Their ship was sinking. The lifeboats were crippled beyond recognition. Even if he used all his strength to use the winds to push the torn sails back to port, the strain would kill him long before they reached any shore, assuming the ship stayed above water long enough to even sail that far.

_Pride of Juno._ He remembered. His crew stared at him in anticipation.

"What are we… going to do, Centurion?" Thomas asked.

"Is this the end of the Fifth Cohort?" Lily added.

Jason summoned all his courage, and dared to trust his gut.

"If it is, Legionnaires, then let this be our finest hour. I have one last card to play."

He stared at the sky, waiting, watching, as the ship lurched again. Forty degrees of tilt. Any further, and they were going to fall off no matter what they did.

"Juno, Queen of New Rome, if I am truly your pride, if you truly seek to aid me, now would be your time." He said, voice stern and filled with authority.

He turned back to his crew, all twelve of them.

"On my mark, we jump. All of us." He said.

"Together?" Dakota sputtered, like he was insane for even suggesting it.

"Together," Jason's crew affirmed, "for better, for worse."

They all nodded as twelve Legionnaires and one Centurion waited at the edge.

41 degrees.

42.

43.

"Wait for it…" Jason whispered.

The ship lurched as a chain of boards breaking under the strain of the load formed an expanding crack from stern to bow.

44.

"Just a little bit further…" Jason breathed.

45. The ship broke beneath them, tearing in half as Jason gave what every neuron in his head assured him was his last order, and his gut assured him would be his first.

"Now!" He boomed, and the thirteen jumped as Jason willed the winds with seven words. _We will not hit the water._

The wind held them in place as the ship fell away from them and vanished into the sea with a huge splash. It slowed their fall to an agonizing crawl as gravity fought to bring them down.

Fifty feet. Forty feet. Thirty. Twenty. Ten.

Five.

Four.

Three.

Two.

One.

At first, Jason assumed the wall of white and pink had to be the spray of the sea as they slammed into something cold and hard below them. But they never hit the water.
