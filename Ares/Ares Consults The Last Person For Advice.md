ARES
The god of war is not supposed to afraid.
The god of war is supposed to enjoy war.

Ares is sitting at his desk in his war room, overlooking television screens of various war movies. Band of Brothers, The Things They Carried, footage of the Battle of Manhattan, footage of the initial invasion of Saigon. Highlight reels of humanity's best moments. At least…it should've been.

Before the Battle of Manhattan, Olympus' War Room had been a proper war room. Monitors covering the walls, Sun Tzu's Art of War written as bunches of verses scattered along tables and desks like the warrior version of an evangelical's home, huge celestial bronze braziers holding Greek fire, little pillars ignited by a constant barrage of artillery explosions into one long burning flame.

Now it was an artillery shell of its former self. Still big and grand, far better than the War Room in the White House or the Kremlin or his little place in Munich, but…lesser. He had four monitors left. Technically only two, but through the divine powers of a certain Chase, he'd managed to get split-screen going on each monitors by sending them to Athena's library with a note. The verses were completly gone except for scattered words that made no sense on their own. The braziers had fallen over, meaning that an entire third of the room was now a safety hazard. He'd already lost consciousness once detonating ten pounds of Greek fire by accident. The little pillars? Now in Hephaestus' workshop getting properly decommisioned in a way that wouldn't damage the rest of this side of the palace. A tile cracked under his foot. He snapped his fingers to repair it, but he knew it was a losing battle. This place had taken the worst beating of its entire life four and a half weeks ago now, and it needed a complete overhaul.
Kind of like him.

And in the very rear, cornered off in a literal hole of books like his sister had been in her younger years, sat the god of war, violence, chaos and destruction.
He felt like the version of himself from Wish.
Though, to be fair, the hole was the size of a living room.

In it, weapons organized by era lined the walls on racks, also borrowed from Hephaestus' workshop. Greek spears, Roman shields, Chinese rockets, Egyptian curved swords, American automatic weapons. He'd folded a curated selection of uniforms on his desk beside him. The WW2 stuff was what he liked most. The biggest war to date. Though, that also meant *the clothes* had to be at the top of the stack. White chiton, and himation. The greatest hero of the greatest war to date. The boy who had killed a titan king with nothing but a knife, knowing it would kill him as well.

For a moment, all he could do was hold the familiar white chiton and remember that the greatest hero of the greater war ever fought…had been the son of the god of thieves and messengers.
Then the traitor and spy of the entire titan war had needed to pretend to be his daughter to convince his own children to fight in a war. Ares Cabin. Refusing to go to war. Fate had managed to deliver a tactical nuke straight to his ego.
He didn’t bother looking up when the doors opened with a weary creak.

One of Hephaestus' automatons. This one, apparently, was doing its best Captain America. It had been a fun game at first. Hephaestus made an automaton, he found a way to break it. Always different ways each time, always something new. Hephaestus patched the fault, and send another one.

"Would you like to discuss honor? Glory? Patri-" It said. With the chiton *right there*, he wasn't in the mood. He simply reached over, picked up the AR-15, and dumped rounds into the thing's chest until it eventually stopped moving. He didn't even care that it wasn't smashed, he just wanted the thing out of his sight.

"No. I would not." He said, his voice bitter with resignation. He tried to add a snarky, clever comment like he'd seen in the movies so many times, but it didn't land the same way. "Eat lead, Cap!"

*Cap. Why did I have to say cap?*
He looked at the hat next to him. 
*Iraq-Afghanistan.
2003-2011.*
His son had been so happy to receive it, had seen it with such reverence, and then…Ares had ruined it. A gift from the father the boy wished he had, screwed up by the father he *actually* had. The god of war, chaos, destruction, and *not* parenting.
*Toughen up, kid! My kids have a reputation to maintain!*

He had regretted it before the word 'maintain' had even left his mouth.
The god of war is not supposed to be afraid.
Ares *was* afraid, though.

He heard the front door, readied his AR-15, and wondered why Hephaestus had insisted on sending an automaton so quickly. The grand bronze door swung on its hinge…and promptly fell off the doorframe, falling forward, smashing into the stone floor, going through, and sinking into whatever it was that this palace stood on.
Annabeth Chase stepped through, tablet in hand, hair tied up with a pencil. She gave the room a single, exasperated look, then sighed like someone about to argue with a contractor.

“I was hoping this would be one of the easy ones. Since it was so far away from the actual fighting. Now the door doesn't even work. I didn't even know those *could* fail.”

Ares said nothing. He flipped a page in the book on his desk.

She started walking the perimeter, scanning debris, murmuring architectural terms under her breath. She kicked aside a shield bent in half like a soup can and started mapping the wall angles with her eyes.

“This was supposed to be a repair, maybe replace some furniture,” she muttered, “but the foundation itself is completely eroded through. You are the only thing keeping this roof from caving in-”
A ceiling tile—a mix between concrete, celestial bronze and limestone—fell from the ceiling directly above her. He waited for her to teleport out of the way given that the tile was falling too fast to dodge.
It was four inches above her head before he remembered demigods couldn't teleport. He did it for her, teleporting her to the opposite side of the room and out of the way…making the entire room groan at the sudden shifting of weight.
"—more than it already is." She finished, only aware that she'd almost just died to a gods-be-cursed *ceiling tile* after she finished the sentence. He saw her eyes widen, and realized they were completely bloodshot. "Thank you."

She remembered who she was talking to.
"…Lord Ares."
Still no reply.

She turned toward him, tablet hovering just in front of her face. Her realtor voice would've sold a younger him pretty easily. “We could lean into the classic Roman theater look again — give the whole thing that battlefield council vibe, but sleeker. Something worthy of the god of war. In fact, if you're willing to get a bit off-kilter, we can combine Roman gladiuses to use as bayonets for AR-15s to give a blend of modern and traditional. The blood and guts of the Roman times, with the ruthless efficiency of the modern era.”

Then she realized the hole in the ground with bookshelves in it wasn't a sunken portion of the floor he'd left alone. He was *in* it, watching her from the little trench out of the corner of his eye. Her mouth made an "O" shape.
Ares finally looked at her. He wasn't angry. That was the scary part. He was just…gone.

"Looks fine to me. It's… my likeness exactly." He said, his voice betraying no emotion, which coming from the god of anger, meant pain.

She blinked. She lowered the tablet slightly.

“That’s… awfully bleak.”

He didn’t respond.

She shifted back and forth awkwardly, realizing she'd completely misread the situation. Her eyes caught on the books stacked beside him: "Bricks in the Wall: Standardized Schooling and Children Who Break The Mold", "Puzzle-Piece Brains and Raising Childre on the Autism Spectrum", "When Sarcasm Creates Distance", "Austin Bradley's Guide to Parenting". He'd only picked up the last one because it had a puzzle piece on the front and the one connection he'd put together in the store was that puzzle piece is related to Autism somehow.

She hesitated, then nodded at the pile.

“I didn’t think you read.”

"I didn't think I read either."

A pause.

She stepped a little closer, curiosity lighting in her like a match. “Who are you reading those for?”

Ares didn’t answer.

He picked up *the* hat.

“He looked at this like it was holy,” Ares muttered. “Held it like it could bring his father back. I gave it to him thinking it’d earn me a smile. I thought it was the right symbol. Something he’d remember. The father he wanted.”

“Did he?”

Ares laughed, bitterly.

“He did. Until I opened my mouth.”

Her brow furrowed. “A demigod?”

He didn’t clarify.

She sat at the edge of the hole, her legs hovering in the air over the edge.

“I didn’t know you had kids like that. Ones who… aren’t like you.”

“I don’t.”

He could feel her eyes on him.

“I mean,” Ares continued, “I… didn’t. All my sons… they want blood, they want pain, they want to prove they’re the loudest beast in the arena. And that makes sense. That’s war.”

“But not this one.”

“No.” He stared down at the hat, fingers tightening around it. “This one fights because he has to. And he hates it. Hates me for it. Even when he wins, he looks like he’s losing. Even at his strongest… he looks like this is killing him.”

She watched him. Carefully. A god of war dead inside was way more terrifying than one that wanted to kill you in your sleep.

“You want to tell me his name?”

Ares didn’t answer.

*Athena is never going to let me live this down. Hades, **I** am never going to let me live this down.*
"I… I don't know what to do." He admitted.

"You… you're asking me, the daughter of the sister you hate, for parenting advice?" She asked, her mind struggling to wrap around the absurdity of this.
"Yes." He said, like the words hurt him. "Yes I am."
"Have you tried… a token of affection? A gift?"
He glared at her, not with hate, but with pain. It must've been visible in his eyes.
*Don't mock me.*
"Yes, I'm asking the daughter of the sister I hate for parenting advice because I didn't consider bribery." He said, voice full of venom.
She raised her hands in mock surrender. He felt bad for her and her bloodshot eyes. She had to be running on fumes with all she was being asked to do, and now she was getting snark from a temperamental god.
"I'm… sorry. It was just… I thought it was worth asking." She said.
He sighed and pressed the hat to his face for the moment. He could smell the gunpowder and blood on it.
"No, I'm sorry." He said. "You're just trying to help me. You didn't deserve that."

She blinked slowly like she couldn't believe the words that had just left his mouth. He didn't blame her. *He* couldn't believe the words that had just come out of his mouth either.
"When's the last time you slept?" He asked her.
*I have a reputation to maintain.
Reputation can rot in Tartarus. I want my son.*
Her eye twitched just thinking about it.
"I haven't slept in four days." She admitted.
"Have you met the espresso machine yet?"
Her eyes met his.
"The espresso machine is the only reason I'm still conscious."

"How about… we make a deal?" Ares asked.
She studied him for a long moment.
"What kind of deal?"
"You give me a guide on how to at least get my foot in the door with him, and I'll tell your mom you're studying pillar designs for the next couple of hours." He offered.
He made a gesture like using the Force and a chunk of the stone wall rolled out, revealing a bed that she definitely hadn't sensed being there before. It was ornate, made of fine cotton and silk threads, and her eyes drooped just looking at it.

"There's no guide on parenting…" She confessed. She eyed the bed with longing. "But… I can give you pointers."

He nodded.

Autistic children often had sensory processing issues. They found portions of the world overwhelming. With Aidan, that was apparently sound, loud noises really set him off. Intellectual kids often didn't like physical activity, preferring mental exercises and complex mental challenges instead. Tactics and strategy over brute force. He dared to look at the monitors, where a young woman was leading an angry, nervous little girl behind her.

"Jane! It was a lawn mower! I was going to bring it back!"

He sometimes got little glimpses like that, his own nature shining through and showing him people.

The key players in the next big war.

*I don't care about a war right now. I just want my son.*

He looked at the familiar white chiton. A hero. Of a great and terrible war. He'd stood behind that hero as the boy had stood before the judges of the Underworld, just to make sure he got a fair trial. Now, fate was nagging him.
Another boy. Another trial. One who had done nothing wrong. It was Ares' own lapse of judgement that the boy was paying for.
Annabeth collapsed forward into the bed, simply allowing herself to fall forward and mailing her consciousness to Hypnos' doorstep.
*Then by my word as God of War, I shall pay for all I have done.*
