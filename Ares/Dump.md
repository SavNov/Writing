Yeah, no, this misunderstands. Aidan's Lie that he needs to mask who he is in order to be accepted, and that's messing with him even at page 1.
Aidan is surviving and trying very much to pass as mortal until he physically can't. The voice in his head helps him survive, with pretty much the single-minded goal of getting him to Camp Half-Blood even though Aidan has no idea. And even here, with his hands literally wrapped around a fire spirit's throat, he chooses to harm but not kill. Minimum force required to get the thing off of him. This is Percy Jackson choosing to tie the Fury down.
Clarisse is living through her own personal copy of Band of Brothers. She'd just been through a hellish war, and while her mom knows about the gods and monsters now, we get *characterization* for the girl who is a side character before this. Because we barely know what she thinks of Aidan in the back of the SUV  (Ares' Chariot, I have decided) before remembering her pride and the death of Silena knocks the wind out of her and forces her to slow the car down.
Aidan gets to Camp, and sees the mentor...lose his crap over what Aidan reveals. This is leading with Gandalf panicking over Sauron. And also setting up a side plot for later that gives us a solid opportunity to show Aidan's tactical mind and who he is when nobody is watching. Our answer? A highly intellectual college professor in a teen's body who can't resist a good puzzle.
Rachel knows something is up, that something is wrong, but she doesn't know what yet. Rachel is to Aidan what Percy is to Hazel in HOO.  (In more ways than one. He has a far stronger connection to the future through Ares than most campers, so those two WILL be solving the bits and pieces they have together.)
He gives the bird to the idea of finally discovering your godly parent and getting answers. His father died in Iraq to an IED. His mom is all he's ever needed. He doesn't really think about who his dad is until he pulls the sword-bending bit with Clarisse under stress, at which point it becomes dread as he starts piecing together that maybe the voice inside and the supernatural bursts of strength have something to do with each other.
We get the campfire scene, a visceral depiction of the camp's reaction to a god breaking an oath and whose fault it may be. Children blaming each other and pointing fingers (gods, why does that sound familiar). And also the fact that Rachel Dare sees Aidan as an unknown variable, and one she'd learned to be nervous around. Maybe a bit of a pervert, too, but he doesn't seem the type. He's a bookworm, not a degen. He seems unaware that social conventions even exist. The future mugs her, prophecy time, and Aidan gets claimed immediately afterward. By Ares. Great.
Aidan meets his father in a dream. This father is effectively dressing in the clothes of the father Aidan expects. Ares, despite being an Olympian god, *chooses* to take the form of a mortal Iraq War veteran with his blown-off legs covered by a blanket, matching the injury that Aidan believes his father died with.
The conversation does not go well. Aidan resolves to ignore Ares forever, but is also clearly scared out of his mind of having to live a soldier's life. He hates fighting. And Ares cannot wrap his mind around this. Ares tries to toughen him up with sarcasm, but it fails. Aidan takes a sarcastic remark literally and ends up deeply emotionally hurt by his father. We also see a rarity: Ares trying to a father, and not liking it when his kid's feelings get hurt. Aidan receives the hat his father allegedly was wearing when he died, and Aidan sees this as yet another manipulation. Quest ensues. Ares shows up in person to reccomend Clarisse go with him to show him the ropes, and while not happy that his son hates him, also knows pushing the subject in public will only deepen the kid's heels.
Our midpoint is where we see every threat from the rising action culminate as fate seems to hand him emotional intimacy on a silver platter with a monster, physical intimacy too. Only for it to be shattered when his divine blood, or a chunk of it in the midst of his mortal blood, burns her from the inside and she takes an emotionally vulnerable kid's first experience with physical intimacy and plunges a knife through it by informing him his mommy's been kidnapped into Samson's harem, a threat he previously did not particularly care about, and it's his fault. He gives up on being human and embraces an incorrect truth. He goes full throttle on Ares and it scares the hell out of his cabin mates...okay, this is too much...
