Clarisse had been staring at the hole in the ground where Aidan had vanished into the cold, dark Underworld for what felt like hours now. 

Percy had gone back to Tracy for supplies, but if the monster Aidan had fought the day he went to Camp Half-Blood before he'd actually gone was right, he might not be back: A son of the sea god shall turn back at the Titans' Throne. 

Percy had told her that Aidan was either dead or gone. Hades did not like being disrespected, and Aidan had apparently been doing plenty of that, leaning into the bravado of the Child of War the prophecy had foretold of. An unstoppable force of nature that couldn't be killed. Hades was opposed to demigods who believed they were immortal on principle. Without the Child of War from the prophecy, Samson literally could not be defeated. Their quest was now impossible. A fool's errand.

As loyal and foolishly stubborn as he was, he wasn't foolish enough to charge into battle against someone who they were fated to be unable to kill.

She refused to believe that Aidan was dead. She was NOT going to lose her friend, not again, not after Silena. Not ever again.

Then seconds had turned into minutes, minutes into what felt like hours. And she was still waiting by the hole in the ground Hades had made and never closed up. Her vocal chords had gone raw from shouting his name for hours and hours until all she could do was speak very softly.

"Please..." She said, having given up on the idea of Aidan hearing her. "...Hades...just..."
The words wouldn't come out. They clung to the back from her throat like sandbags. None of it felt right. Simply asking the god of the Underworld to bring her friend back sounded stupid and unlikely to work.
"...bring him back. Just for a day. Just so we can..."
Her mouth went dry as she stared deeply into the hole, wondering if Hades was even listening to a word she said.
"...so we can finish the quest and go home and never bother you again."

She waited, her hair steadily turning into a dirty mop on her head as she chose to let it out of its bandana, loose and wild and free, in case it might stand a tiny sliver of a chance of influencing the death god's decision.
Nothing. The rocks didn't move, the air didn't change.
Hades was ignoring her.
She felt her throat tighten as she fought back tears. She looked to the side, saw her bronze armor strewn across the ground where she'd left it in order to properly grieve. A helmet here, a chestplate there, a set of combat boots.
But all she could thing about now was Silena's war-torn determined expression as her best friend had worn HER armor into battle because SHE had been too obsessed with her own pride to realize there was a war going on, La Rue, and it's a stupid chariot. Forget about it!
The armor that had been in her backpack, the magic one Ares had given her to store the armor in. She'd been wearing her training armor the entire quest. Every single time she picked up her old armor, all she could think about was Silena's face, taking that fatal blow from that Drakon.
She hadn't worn it since before Silena had died. She couldn't bring herself to.
"...just tell me he's gonna be okay. Just give me that, at least. Please..." She said, suddenly very quiet. 
*Even if I know I'm not.*

She buried her head against the jagged rocks fully now, allowing a single tear to crawl down her cheek. She didn't know if that one tear would open a dam of grief that she wouldn't be able to hold back. It scared her. 
Around her, the tall mountains on either side of the valley leading them to Mount Diablo, and therefore Mount Othyrs, seemed colossal. She felt small and helpless, like a tiny girl in a big world that had no room for her. The face of Mount Diablo judged her from afar. And then, the dam broke wide gods-care-not open.

The daughter of Ares who'd never been able to lay down her pride, finally did so. 

"...I'm begging you. Just let me see his face again."

Her head hung low, pressing against the dirt and rocks so hard her face bled as it scratched her, as tears drenched her face and leaked into the hole. Her tears mixed with the blood and dirt to turn her whole upper half: hair, face, neck, a redish brown color that matched the reddish-brown stones in the valley. Aidan had told her those were where a god's oath bled into the earth's torn skin. Now she was bleeding too. She didn't care. Her back ached, along with her shoulders, her chest, everything.

"I'm begging you, Lord Hades, just one...last...time..." She sobbed into the hole.
She thought, maybe, just maybe, calling him Lord Hades instead of just Hades might stroke his ego a bit, give her something, anything.
Even if Aidan terrified her, even if this quest had turned him into a seething thing of hate and ruthlessness, she needed this. To see him again. It wasn't Silena, it would never be Silena, but for now...
...Aidan Travis, Child of War, born of the war god's forge, and right now a seething pile of hate after his mother had been kidnapped...would be enough.

She looked left, then right, studying the valley fully without caring about the quest, without caring about knocking Samson's teeth in, without worrying about tactics...for the first time ever, really. The dozens of bunkers built by Samson's men blended right into the rock.

She'd never realized just how beautiful this place was. It was utterly gorgeous. The way the evening sun caught the mountains just right, making the place shine with huge red and gold hues like burning celestial bronze. Mount Diablo. Alabaster Pass. The way the rock caught the shifting sun, forming shapes and shadows against the ground that waved around like children dancing. Samson probably had no idea. 

He'd probably seen it purely as tactics, just as she had. Just a valley with natural copper and tin deposits, excellent for making bronze for armor. Combine it with steel deposits up-north, and you could make the strongest armor short of Mount Olympus' celestial bronze stuff.

Faced with that gorgeous mountain view, the tactics of supply chain placement seemed like a distant footnote.

The storm that had been slowing creeping across that glorious October sky and eating away at the beautiful view boomed thunder. A lightning bolt struck one of the copper deposits a few hundred feet away.

*Bronze is made of copper and tin. Both are great conductors of electricity. Since your spear produces electricity...do you ever worry that one day it may spark against your armor and go through that instead?*
She'd thought Aidan was stupid when he'd first asked it. Now, seeing the lightning arc off of a copper deposit, she was dumbfounded by how she had never once thought about it before.

A draft of air rose up from the hole. Small, but there. Poisonous black smoke emanated. It burned her nose and throat, and mixed with her already reddish-brow tears to turn them the color of soot.
She didn't turn away, though. Even as her lungs burned and her eyes watered.
*I'm ready.* She thought. *Show me.*

On the smoke, a picture floated up. She caught it in her fingers with a care and reverence she usually reserved for her spear. The thought that she would treat a hunk of celestial bronze that happened to shoot lightning bolts with more care than a human was...well it was just like Aidan getting pinned down by that void spirit against the seat. *I don't know if you're just that good, or that is just heart-breakingly depressing.* Funny, or utterly tragic.

It was a picture of Aidan. Not recent. You just had to see him. He looked about nine or ten, sitting in his mom's chair with that grin on his face that you just knew meant he was being annoying but technically correct. 

A lecture hall, because *of course* Mr. college polo and black slacks Travis had a college professor for a mom and no babysitter during the summer. Clarisse laughed. There was Greek scrawled on the blackboard that seemed strange to her with its weird grammar...was that the 'Kione Greek' she'd heard Chiron talk about after Aidan had started reading the Greek names of monsters? Chiron had said it was used around the time the Christian gospels were written, which made her look back.

*I'm actually a practicing Christian. My mom teaches Theology at UofL.*
Theology. Huh.

Theologian mom, kind and sweet kid with a cheeky side, not the wealthiest kid in the world. He definitely hadn't been told to learn to manage his anger at a child.

"Lucky..." She muttered under her breath, unable to stop herself from grinning.

She held the photo to her chest, terrified that if she let go of it even for a moment the warm memory might just vanish from all existence without a trace.

She looked at the ground again. Her armor strewn across the ground. The image of Silena dying in it literally knocked the wind out of her lungs as she started sobbing again, pressing the photo against her chest like she was trying to absorb Aidan's normal life through osmosis and remove the part where she'd gotten so mad over a stupid chariot that Silena Boureguard, daughter of Aphrodite, had had to put on that armor herself and pretend to be Clarisse all the way to dying to that Drakon.

The muscles in her chest refused to work correctly. She struggled to breathe as she wept again, each sob feeling ripped from her chest against her will, just like Aidan had been ripped from them.

"Th-th-thank y-you..." She cried into the smoke, stuttering for the first time since just after Silena had died. Her tears had soaked the first rocks leading down the hole. Hades gave no answer.

She slowly stood up. She'd taken her armor off to grieve. Now she eyed it next to her. Her spear. Her electric spear.
The dumbest, craziest, most mind-numbingly reckless Hail Mary she'd ever conceived came to mind.
She had always been a total sucker for dumb, crazy, mind-numbingly reckless Hail Marys.
"Well then..." She grunted.
She paused as she picked up the helmet first. The memory hit her like an electric spear. Silena's face beneath beneath the helmet, smiling as she prepared to face a Drakon she knew she couldn't kill no matter how hard she tried. Her fingers trembled as she kept hold of the helment, fighting herself not to drop it.
She kept the helment. Dropped her whole body instead as her legs gave out beneath her, knees buckling as she knelt before the helmet and chestplate. She hadn't expected this to be this hard. Then she remembered again. The last time she'd worn this armor, Silena had still been alive. It was stupid to assume it would be just like every other time.
She placed the helmet back down, trying to apply the principle Aidan had taught her in a rare moment of being himself. Buried under thirty pounds of what she was now smart enough to realize was compensatory bravado from a kid who felt she'd never accept him for who he really was, but there. It broke her heart all over again thinking about it.
*If one part of a task is too difficult, try a different section. A smaller part. There are a lot of things that don't have to be done strictly in order.*
She'd been doing it out of order to start with, putting on the helmet before the chestplate, but she'd never had to worry about it before. It had never mattered before.
She placed her left foot gingerly in the left boot. The image grew fainter. Safer. Low risk. She laced the boot up, and then the right one. She wrapped and laced the plated leggings next. The memory of Silena gasping for air forced its way into her head, that sound burning into her mind. She waited, letting it pass like Aidan had taught her without even realizing it. The sound faded.
Then chestplate, wrapping around her and sewn tightly at the back, but she'd always used her powers as a child of Ares to will the back of the chestplate to sew itself. She didn't this time. She did it like the first time she'd ever been taught, reaching her fingers around the delicate fibers, one side at a time, threading them tightly together. A tiny patch of Drakon blood had burned the stitches. She had to bite down on the bronze front to avoid the scream of both actual pain and memory resonating through the whole valley. She steadied her breath, waited it out.
Now would be the hard part. The helmet. Just picking it up made her see that pained expression.
"I'm sorry..." She breathed, not trusting herself to say anything else.
She placed the helmet on her head. Now with the full gear on, she'd have to pick up her spear.
Her shaky fingers gripped the handle. Her fingers shook so badly she dropped it, so she tried again. She held it. Not trusting herself not to drop the spear a second time, she sheathed it in her armor. Finally, she finished the sentence she'd began ten minutes ago.
"Time to find the Hades out."
