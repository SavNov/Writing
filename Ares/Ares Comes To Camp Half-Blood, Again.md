ARES
After thousands of years of demigods, many of them strong and fierce and feared, Ares had encountered a new emotion. Uncertainty.
He wasn't a demigod. He'd never been a demigod. And he definitely hadn't ever taken an active role in his kids' lives, not unless it was to correct them or strenghten them. Not until Samson...and that hadn't gone as well as he'd hoped.
So when he'd placed a form at Camp Half-Blood, heard the familiar sound of the aftermath of the explosion he liked to arrive with, he was nervous. He'd already accepted that he wasn't the father Aidan needed, but right now, he wasn't the emotional guy. He'd never been much good with emotions beside anger, terror, spite, vengeance, devotion and reference, the honor and glory of battle and the sacrificing of yourself for a greater cause. The ability to die with honor. Comfort? War was the opposite of comfortable. A hand on your shoulder telling you everything was going to be okay? He really was out of his depth there.

"Hey, runts." He said, voice morphing into that typical aggressive tone he was known for. Little kids, runts, brats, that kind of thing. Because it was way easier to pretend you didn't care whether any of them lived or died than admit you'd just been crying over the thought of losing your daughter.
Crying? That wasn't very manly. It was embarrassing. Shamful. Crybabies were no good in a war.
Then he eyed his newest son. Aidan Travis was smiling, looking awkward but very much caring as he picked up a crutch from the wheelchair and handed it to—
—wait—
—Clarisse?
Rather than bother to run over, he simply vanished in a flash of fire and ash again and reappeared at his daughter's son.
"You okay, kid?" He asked, his voice gruff.
She gave him a look of pure anger and grief, the one he'd worn when he'd seen her dead body.
"Do I *look* okay?" She growled, then her eyes squinted in recognition. "I—um, I'm sorry, dad- I mean Lord Ares!"
He sighed internally. She was equal parts desperate for approval, which really made him feel like a crappy father, and fearful of him.
Aidan gave a single, humorless chuckle, because of course the kid knew how she felt and that he hated the fact that most of his kids felt the need to call him Lord Ares. He couldn't tell them off without seeming weak.
So he did something he'd never tried before.
"Alright, brats. Clear out! Me and La Rue are going to have a private conversation. Except for you, Travis. You stay." He announced. Kids took for the hills. That familiar centaur backed away into the Big House.
Aidan looked confused, but the boy stayed by Clarisse's side as she sat in her wheelchair.
He recognized the look of pure grief in her face. And anger, lots of anger, but mostly grief.

"Clarisse," He said for the first time ever. "...please do not call me Lord Ares. I don't want to be feared. I want to be respected, and loved. I want to be a father."
Clarisse looked stunned.
