Ever since the age of 13, Aidan had been hearing voices. Well...a voice. In the back of his mind, giving advice. It had been simple stuff at first. *Duck, left, right, dodge.* But then, as he'd proved he could take it, it had gotten more sophisticated. *It depends upon scent, hide behind the pillar, it can only hear.* Always deep, always commanding, but also...it had never been wrong. He trusted it implicitly.
He was in his second year of high school now. The flourescent lights had always bothered his eyes. His red sneakers tapped against the tile floor. The typical entendre of teens walking beside him in the hallway scoffing made its presence known as he went to the cafeteria to get lunch. His shirt was baggy and never had fit him quite right. Neither had those two-tone jeans. He guessed it was part of being skinny and lanky and generally not very fit or muscular. Unless you talked about...he chose not to dwell on it.

Aidan felt his muscles tense. This was it. He would either die here, or he would win.
*Trust the plan.*
He stared at his fingers. His hands. The ones that had held that flaming spirit without burning up, had seemed so stiff whenever he'd held a sword, had refused to cooperate in combat, had always been so sluggish.
He could hear the voice of Ares in his head, now no longer just a voice in his head guiding him from the shadows, but a guardian. A bird's eye view. All those days ago at camp.
*Like it or not, boy, my blood flows through your veins. It's who you are.*
He looked at his veins. He finally realized what he'd never even considered before, the way Ares had looked like he'd been making a clever joke, grinning that way.
*You meant it literally.* He thought.
*Now you're getting it.* The voice of Ares replied.
He looked at the interior of the cell block, analyzing. This entire building was military, was a fortress...*was an imitation*.
Concrete walls. Steel bars. Greek swords and spears and shields, all in delicate little rows. All of them unused. Samson desperately wanted to be his father, wanted to be the god of war, but couldn't. He had raised an entire army, made an entire movement, out of insecurity.
He gripped the bars of his cell. Percy had been fighting an impossible battle for ten long minutes now and had to be exhausted.
Most of the guards were now outside, watching the fight with nervous hesitation.
Samson was struggling. Keeping his ground, but struggling. Not being able to kill Percy wasn't on-brand for him. And there was no telling where Clarisse was. A guard shouted at him.
"Get back from the bars! Don't make me come over there!" The guard threatened. Heavy Greek gladiator armor.
"Tell Samson I have a message from father." Aidan said.
The guard stared at him skeptically.
"You're creating a diversion to save your friend." He said, and smiled, banging on the holding cell to mess with him. "Clever."
Aidan met the guard's eyes.
"Actually, you've got it backwards." He said. "Percy is the diversion so Samson doesn't see me coming."
The guard laughed, then realized he was serious. "Really? You? Why should the strongest son of the war god in decades be afraid of you, huh? Think he'll die laughing?"
"Tell him I have a message for him," he replied, trying to keep his cool. *You need to give Percy a rest.* "Tell him it's from father."
The guard's eyes widened. "You're not..." He stared at the son of the war god for a few seconds. "You're not lying. Give me a minute."
The guard quickly ran off to inform his superiors. Aidan felt the bars tremble underneath his touch. But it wasn't *his* touch, it was the touch of the war god dwelling within him. He looked down at his fingers. A tiny stream of golden ichor flowed through the vein on his left arm.
*Born of the war god's forge.*
He paused.
"What exactly does that mean?" He asked aloud, though he had a feeling he'd alraedy knew.
*You're about to find out.*
Samson came into the main cell block.
"They told me you have a message from Lord Ares from me. Got bored with Percy Jackson, thought I'd see what you were about after you humiliated yourself the first time around." Samson grinned, in full battle armor, but Aidan already knew he was exhausted. He wasn't used to fighting someone in the same weight class as him.
"I do." Aidan said simply.
Samson seemed almost eager to see how this would unfold. He wanted to see what this message was. But, more importantly, he wanted to hear from his dad.
Samson quickly threw open the cell door, completely convinced that his opponent was harmless.
Aidan stepped forward. This was the moment that Samson finally began to even slightly understand, even beneath all that bravado, that he had made a serious mistake.
