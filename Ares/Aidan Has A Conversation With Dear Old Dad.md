AIDAN
The father I had never needed in my life was now the one dictating my own fate to me.

My eyes opened as I felt myself crossing an invisible threshold between the mortal world and the godly one. This was Mount Olympus, I was the son of Ares. I felt it the moment I took a step.
Armor.

Heavy celestial bronze armor that I did not have the strength to wear, so I fought for each step as I stepped down the hallway.

*Anything?* I asked the voice in my mind.

I got no reply, which had not really ever happened before. Even when it chose to ignore me, which had not happened in months, I usually got some version of *Acknowledged.* just to confirm. I got none of that.

A step. Another step. To my left and right, huge racks of weapons stood towering over my head. The armor was so heavy that my legs were getting a full workout just trying to lift the boots off the ground. I looked carefully.

They were perfectly ordered. Lines of Greek and Roman blades, swords and daggers gleaming under torch light, and then some made of Celestial Bronze or a slightly darker, more volatile metal I hadn't seen before. I had only heard it mentioned in passing by the voice as I took the darker color and more visibly volatile nature together. *Imperial Gold.*

Dozens of shields caught the same light, revealing some were recently used, and all were regularly maintained.

I remembered something else the voice had said a year ago, a fight in a Civil War museum in a section dedicated to the Confederacy. *Weapons are meant to be used, not admired.*

That was how it had justified the theft of a steel bayonet used at the Battle of the Hill.

I did not remember returning that, which I had felt bad about, up until I had visited the museum a few days later and realized the bayonet was back its place somehow.

I stepped forward through the huge hall. I saw that swords were slowly becoming spears and throwing knives and javelins. Egypians curved blades. Chinese rockets. Greek fire. Gunpowder. Humanity evolving faster. Bows, guns, artillery cannons, mortars, huge warships on pedestals.

Finally, my father came into view. He was dressed in the uniform of a U.S. Army soldier, but his legs… were underneath a blanket that I recognized from photos of my dad when I was little. His legs were blown off by an IED while serving in Iraq. His smile was weary, and his fingers tapped on the arms of a throne constructed purely of Chinese rockets, AR-15s, Beretta M4s and other weapons. The backing of the chair was constructed from a mesh of Roman swords and Greek shields.

I eyed his left, where a televison screen showed a squadron of Vietnamese soldiers defending against American invasion, only to flicker to a different squadron of American soldiers staring at the forest in dread.

To his right, another televison screen showed Russian soldiers invading Ukraine.

Like the two live feeds were happening at the same time.

The screen flickered and suddenly started showing an older girl with long, blonde curls holding a dagger and shouting into what I figured must be an Iris Message. Blood was running down her face and arms, but I realized she was not injured.

Her body was under such tremendous stress that she was sweating blood.

"Reyna! You have to get Octavian to call it off! We're being overrun!" She shouted.

"Octavian controls the Senate now! I can't! Even if I was elected Consul, it would still be my word against his!" A panicked voice shouted from the other side.

I approached my father.

"Time does not flow linearly here, does it?" I asked, my voice flat. I figured this was the one person I did not have to hide myself from.

His eyes flicked left, then right, at the television screens.

"Time is certainly strange here, kid." Ares said, his voice dripping with command, but also that gruff indifference like I was just another problem. But I had hope.

Until I remembered. The voice.

Ares sounded exactly like the voice.

"You… you are the voice in my head." I said, voice steady and flat despite my emotions. "You… you are the one who… who has been guiding me all these years…"

I stared at him, and though my expressionless face didn't reveal much, he could see a glimmer of hope.

He fanned his face as he shrugged.

"Well, I couldn't just let you die, now could I? You needed help. You're a mortal, and those monsters hunt demigods for sport." He smiled wide, patting my shoulder.

I physically winced.

*Just a mortal? Couldn't let me die? Is this… there is no way this is the same person as the voice.*

He seemed to vaguely understand that he had done something wrong.

"I'm messing with you, kiddo. You're my son. God of War. Nobody really likes war, at least not all of it, but…" Ares' voice trailed as I felt my world sink. "…that's the price you pay for being the child of the war god and having an affinity for weapons. Death is the cost of achieving the kind of glory that lasts centuries. Making the kind of name that people carve into swords for good look. The kind of name people give their kids hoping they'll be like you one day."

I stepped back. The American soldiers fighting for their lives in the Korean War sixty years ago and, for their efforts, getting a name in a block of stone thousands looked at every day vaguely thinking about honor on Memorial Day, seemed fixated on me. My armor felt like it weighed triple.

"I… I… well…" I said, still flat, but suddenly very quiet.

Ares studied me carefully.

"Kid, it's going to be fine. Toughen up a little." Ares chided, smiling almost aggressively. I could suddenly feel the tension in the room. He jabbed my shoulder, and I fought the urge to wince.

"I… I am not… I do not…" I said, words suddenly failing me as I felt this wall of emotion surface. It came over me like a wave, drowning out all my senses and making me want to scream. Ares was so close to me. I felt like I wanted to hit something.

I hated the feeling. It felt so… opposite of me, like a magnet's south pole resisting another south pole, stronger and stronger as the opposite end got closer and closer.

The room blurred.

"I know this may not be… exactly… as you envisioned, but I'm your dad, Aidan. You can't just ignore me forever. You have to fight. You were born to be a warrior." Ares said, steadying my by the shoulder, but I didn't want to be touched on the shoulder, I didn't want any of this.

Then again…
*When has anything ever gone my way before? It has all been steadily downhill since I was 13.* I thought.

I should not have been shocked by this. *Of course* my father was the god of the one thing that had consistently sucked about my life for the past three years. The clashing of swords that made my ears hurt, the constant side-stepping and dodging and kicking that made me wheeze and groan, convincing myself that I had to keep fighting even when I wanted to quit, to try peace. Fighting when I did not want to.

Ares reached back, and took the hat off his head. My dad's hat. The one he'd died in 16 years ago. The years on it kept blurring and changing, like it couldn't decide when my dad had served.

Ares handed it to me.

"Here. Have it. You'll grow into it soon enough. It'll help you fight." He said.

I picked up the hat with reverence, my mind finally shutting up. I did not care about its use as a weapon. I did not know what it did. I was not eager to find out. This was the father I wanted. A silver lining on a thunderstorm.

The world faded, and I realized I was gaining consciousness.

Yeah, my first day at Camp Half-Blood made me wish that spirit thing had kissed me into oblivion.
