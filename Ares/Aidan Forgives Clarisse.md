AIDAN
**Oh, Can You See The Love Tonight? Because I'm Really Searching For It.**
At least the eldritch monster I apparently saw didn't degrade me afterward.

Silence. Darkness. Then light and noise.

I woke up to the taste of cookie dough. Not the pre-baked dough they package for eating, the raw cookies your mom makes right before she puts them in the oven. That taste. I looked around.

Flowers. 
People staring at me in concern. 
Clarisse; A disgruntled boy with black hair, sea-green eyes and a cheeky grin that was now mowed down into a frown; A younger boy with blonde hair who kept absentmindedly making the same three pointer into the trash bin with a paper ball only for it to bounce back out as he kicked the bin to dislodge it.

*Am I dead? Did that eldritch monster make me seriously forget that I died?* I thought.

*No, you are not dead.* The voice chided.

Then a thousand sounds rushed through my mind at once like a toddler trying to drink from a fire hose.

A deafening clanging sound of metal against metal.

People asking if I was okay.

Clarisse getting angrier and more arrogant as her technique got faster and I did not.

Myself thinking that I was going to die.

Me telling Clarisse, in a voice way too flat for her to hear the urgency in it, to take it easy on me.

Campers complimenting my skill and urging Clarisse to go harder.

The sound of my body hitting the stone floor of the training arena.

Somebody calling over a medic.

*I do not believe you.* I spat mentally.
It was the first time I'd ever said that to it.

*Wait. What even are you?* I thought.
I remembered myself parrying the blow, the voice panicking just slightly as it told me how to do it.
Clarisse coming that close to killing me had not been something the voice anticipated. It had been improvising just as much as I was.
*A daugher of Ares nearly killing her student. This is progressing more quickly than I anticipated.* The voice said, like it had forget it shared a mind with me.
I was being stone-walled by my own guardian. Oh well.
*Are you a hallucination?* I thought.
I didn't get a reply.
My gaze drifted around the room.

"I'm…I'm sorry." Clarisse whispered.
*She is fighting the urge to cry.* The voice explained.
At first I thought of that as a good thing. She had nearly killed me. She deserved to be a bit sad over that. I stopped myself.
The group couldn't work out whether to hate Clarisse for generally being the Ares Cabin bully and almost murdering a camper…or to feel sorry for how utterly curb-stomped she clearly felt.
Clarisse La Rue did not apologize to anyone. Even I had noticed she didn't seem the type.

Hades losing his mind over Jamie, a girl I was already starting to forget the specifics of, leaving wherever she came from, getting through the Underworld, and somehow surviving in the mortal plane without getting smited by anyone for two whole weeks. Or was it three weeks. Or one? I couldn't remember.

Somebody smacked me.
"You okay?" The boy doing his best Sisyphus impression with a paper ball and bin asked me, meeting my eyes with an intensity I didn't like.

I looked at the candles on the walls, the torches in the braziers, and realized it was dark out.
"How long was I out?" I asked, my tone calm and flat.

Clarisse stared at the floor like the thought of meeting my eyes was scarier than sparring with me.
"Half an hour." She said.
That did not make sense.
"It's dark out." I pointed out. She nodded. "It was not dark out when I passed out."
The boy who I'd seen practicing that three-pointer paused for a moment.
"It was 7 PM when you got here." He said.
*But it was five when I—* I thought, then paused. *Timezones.*
*New York and Louisville are both on Eastern Time.* The voice said.
*Okay, smart-aleck, but GMT differences don't account for time differences shorter than an hour.* I thought back.
After several minutes of quiet argument about timezones, I realized most of them had already left. Besides a guilt-striken Clarisse.

I thought back. Clarisse had known what Chiron was thinking of when I'd first described the thing…I think. It was getting fuzzy even now.

At the same time, though, I remembered why I didn't kill. Why I didn't hurt monsters that were trying so hard to hurt me.
*My Lord has commanded me not to judge.* I remembered. *If your enemy is hungry, feed him. If your enemy is thirsty, give him something to drink. For in so doing, you are heaping burning coals on his head.*
Deciding who lived and died? That was *not* my call.
"Clarisse…" I said, trying to make my naturally flat voice and make it softer, more soothing.
She turned to meet my eyes like a dog anticipating a beating.
"…you didn't know. You had no way of knowing. This is not your fault." I said.
"How can you say that…when I almost killed you?" She asked, both confused and also angry that I *wasn't* getting mad at her. "I sent you into a panic attack. You almost died on your first day at Camp Half-Blood, and it's my fault!"
I locked eyes with her.
The coals were certainly burning alright.
"I'm Autistic. My tone of voice, my facial expressions, they rarely line up with what I'm feeling in the moment." I said, letting my naturally flat tone evolve into something toic, grounding. "You misinterpreted what I said. And you want the truth?"
She leaned forward, just an inch, but it was enough.
"I forgive you." I said.
She picked up a knife and buried it up to the hilt in the wall, but made no move against me.
*That…wait…how?* The voice asked.
*You have your show.* I thought. *I have mine.*
