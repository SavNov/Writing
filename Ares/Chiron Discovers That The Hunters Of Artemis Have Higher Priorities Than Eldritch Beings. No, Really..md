AIDAN
As I took my shoes off, prepared to head to bed after a long, draining day of gaining a little bit of acceptance from my quest mates, I received an Iris message.

*Oh well.* I thought.

I sighed in resignation and turned to the shimmering image. Chiron. He was sitting in his wheelchair with a novel on his lap labeled Moby Dick.

*He is weary.* Ares noted.
I figured taking his word for it couldn't hurt in this small case.

"How did it go?" I asked, my voice flat and a bit tired.
“I notified the Hunters of Artemis an hour ago,” Chiron said. “They’ve informed me there are… larger fish to fry.”

I stared at him, expressionless like I was very much tired of not being. It should've seemed insane. Insane had kicked my butt today. I decided not to question it.

“Larger fish than one of Tartarus’ big three rivers. Okay then.”

He didn't answer that.

“Speaking of rivers… the Mississippi is currently burning.”

I gave a single, slow blink to indicate I'd heard him.

“…How does a body of water combust?”

“My question precisely.”

“Oil spill? Explosives?”

“This is the Hunters of Artemis, Aidan Travis. Not the Coast Guard.”

“…Did someone think they were Sully?”

“Not the FAA either.” He sighed, rubbing the bridge of his nose. “I thought that went without saying. But then again… you are a son of Ares.”

I paused.
*Ouch.*

Not oil. Not explosives. Not jet fuel.
I closed my eyes. I could see the river in my mind, and even though I didn't know which part was currently burning, I did know about the firefighter's triad from 3rd grade.

You needed three things to start a fire and keep it going. Oxygen, heat, and fuel. I removed confounding variables.

"Is the whole river on fire?"
"No. Just a section of it." Chiron said, arching an eyebrow as he, and the Ares within me, tried to figure me out. "What are you thinking about?"

Oxygen. Heat. Fuel.
Obviously the river itself couldn’t burn. It was water. I remembered just enough about chemistry to understand that you needed hydrogen, but also carbon, to get a chain reaction. Oil was nothing more than one overkill carbohydrate. Hydrogen and carbon both loved forming bonds, and making bonds made energy. That was all a fire was. 
But… minerals could combust under the right conditions. If the temperature was high enough.
And where else could you find more natural minerals than a riverbed?

Fuel. Done.
Now, heat.

What could possibly burn hot enough to set a mineral deposit ablaze?

*Maybe ask a fire*, Ares said.

I didn't like understanding what the god of war was putting forward.
I really didn’t like that it made sense.

“Do you know where the river started burning?” I asked.

“The Hunters have tracked it from Pennsylvania, moving southwest. They say it began three days ago.”

Three days ago. Really? This just keeps getting stupider.

“Is the fire growing, or is it moving?”

“It expanded to about eighty feet in length, then began moving downstream.”

I looked at the Iris image, thinking fast.
This can’t be real. It can’t be this straightforward.

*I am inside your head*, Ares said, *and I still do not understand what you are getting at.*

"Tell the Hunters to drop Greek fire so that it intersects the leading edge of the fire two feet below the water line. Then, tell them to detonate it." I said, my voice still flat, but now strangely calm and authoritative.

Chiron stared at me for five seconds straight.

Then he nodded. Slowly.

“I don’t have Annabeth Chase yet, she’s still on Olympus. But you…” His voice was even. “You may be an adequate substitute.”
[Later that night.]
===
I had just managed to catch a hint of sleep when I was suddenly woken up by the glow of an Iris message.
*Chiron.* I thought. *At this hour, though?*

*Not Chiron,* I realized, watching the girl step into frame. *Thalia Grace.*
Combat boots. Black hair. Electric eyes. A spear like Clarisse’s, only older.
*And probably a bit deadlier, if she's a daughter of Zeus.* I thought.
She was stepping about five feet away from whatever lense caught the light in Iris messages, so that she was in full view. There were a couple of girl behind her, with an expression on their faces I didn't recognize.

She was exactly as Percy remembered her…and made La Rue look chill.

“I’ve got one question for you, Aidan Travis,” she said, like she was getting ready to see if spears followed the same rules in Iris messages that light did.

“Yes?” I asked. Voice only neutral because of the way my mind worked. Hands still.

She stared for a long moment, shaking her head in disbelief.

“How the Hades did you know that would work?”

That stopped me.

*Wait. It worked?*

My own thought overlapped with Ares’ at the exact same time.
I felt sick.

“…It worked?”

“We detonated the Greek fire like you said,” she muttered. “Something glowed. Then sank straight to the bottom of the river. A few seconds later, the flames burned out. All of it."

Her voice tightened.
“So I’ll ask again. How did you know that would work?”

I didn’t answer immediately. I didn’t move.
"One question." I said.
She nodded uneasily, like I was testing her patience.
"One."

“Your Hephaestus kids found nothing, but your Hades campers felt something. Yes, or no?”

She blinked. Turned. Said something to someone offscreen.

Then she turned back.

“…Yes. And I’m going to assume you saw that in a dream, because if you didn’t, my head’s going to explode.”

That didn't sound like something I wanted to happen, which from the way this quest was going, meant it was only an inch short of being fate-ordained.

A pause.

“A spirit of the River Phlegethon,” I said flatly. “Burning its way downstream through the Mississippi.”
I looked at her through the mist.
“You knocked it out. Hades pulled it back down to Tartarus.”

She waited.

"Which of course means…" I sighed. She rolled her eyes.
"That something else may crawl out later on. Yeah, I figured as much. Now, to hunt your spirit of the River Lethe." She grunted, like she was sick and tired of living through world events. "Anything we should know before we face it?"
I met her eyes.
"One thing." I said. "Record everything. Even the obvious. And trust your notes more than your own memory."
Her mouth fell open. Her voice was a bit more reserved and shy now.
"Got it. We'll keep that in mind."
