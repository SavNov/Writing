AIDAN
**I Had Always Kind Of Known This Was Going To Happen.**
Me and Clarisse walked together to the campfire, her arm on my shoulder to steady me. In hindsight, it was a rare sight. She'd stowed her armor away. The walk took about five minutes for us both.
When we got there, word had apparently gotten it. Two deliciously awful statements:
Aidan Travis is 16.
Aidan Travis is unclaimed.

Clarisse put it best: "So, which god broke the oath on the River Styx that Percy turned down immortality to get?"
Given I was unclaimed, I sat among a bunch of Hermes and unclaimed campers. From what I gathered, it wasn't unusual for gods to be a few weeks late. Olympians were busy. Minor gods often didn't think their children cared who they were. The Styx apparently tolerated this as long as someone didn't go more than a season unclaimed. After that, things were bound to start going wrong for the god or goddess.
"Look, man," A son of Hermes that had attempted to pick my pocket almost as a greeting assured me, "Hermes isn't like this. He's cool about claiming his kids. No matter who it turns out to be, you'll always have a home here."
I sat down on the warm stone bench. A campfire song began in the corner. Then I caught the eyes of a girl. A redhead who looked like she'd been snatched straight off the set of Brave, with a t-shirt covered in various stains that acted as a glowing beacon of "I am an artist. No, I do not care what you think about my art."
She met my eyes as if I were a total question mark to her, and a dangerous one at that. I understood that.
"Hey, who's she?" I asked my young kleptomaniac friend.
I pointed to the girl.
"She is Rachel Elizabeth Dare. And she hates you." He replied, whistling under his breath. "Or is madly in love with you. You know how girls are."
I did not, in fact, know how girls were.
I eyed her shirt.
Jagged lines forming crazy colored images. A red and gold glow of a forge. A lightning bolt coming down in a valley. A prison cell block.
"And she is?" I asked him.
She proceeded to shout over the entire ampitheater.
"Rachel Elizabeth Dare. I *can* read lips, you know. You aren't subtle."
I stared down at my feet. However, now boiling under three hundred sets of eyes, I couldn't really think straight.
"You hate me." I stated flatly. My tone made it sound like a fact of the universe.
"I'm unsure of what to make of you. That's different." She corrected.
"What's that shirt about?" I asked her across the ampitheater.
"Oracle of Delphi. When the future isn't mugging me, I paint it." She said.
My eyes couldn't keep away from that forge. My heart was racing.
*How on Earth? Where did she—* I thought.
Unfortunately, I failed to consider that it was unclear I was looking at the forge compared to simply staring at her chest like a brain dead moron.
Yet, to explain that would've meant explaining why a picture of a forge bothered me so much.
Are you a pervert? Was a much easier question to live with than Why do you care about the forge?
Sensing that I was a giant question mark already, she gave me the mercy of not commenting on it.

Chiron had returned in the past ten minutes, now resting in his wheelchair, sitting near the fire. The boy with sea-green eyes now had what I could only describe as a sad smile on his face, clasping his hands around each other, staring at a photo of what I assumed to be either a close friend or girlfriend. I couldn't see the photo, though I didn't need to. He had a ballpoint pen in his jacket pocket. He appeared to have a section of ampitheater carved out entirely to himself.
"Oh? Him? That's Percy Jackson, dude." My klepto friend explained, attempting to pick my pocket again. This time, I humored him and allowed him to go for a grab. He realized my pants pockets were empty. "He took on Kronos one on one, and actually beat him. Whole bunch of other quests, too."
I sighed.
"Also, curse you, can a son of Hermes not catch a break?" He added, realizing my pockets were empty.
"You pick my pocket and then get mad at me for being broke." I said flatly. "How rational."
He sighed, and then I saw Rachel smirk. It was tiny, but very much there.
"My bad. You're right. Sorry, dude." He said. Then, totally unrepentant, completely changed the subject. "So, uh…do you think you could…um…?"
*This poor man.* I thought.
*Indeed. He has a crush on Rachel Dare, who will absolutely walk all over him.* The voice said.
"Do you even have a name, or shall I just call you Klepto?" I asked flatly, not entirely joking.
"Christopher. Sorry, should've lead with that." He apologized.
"Campers!" Chiron's voice boomed across the ampitheater as he banged his hoof against the firebed for attention. "Let's be civil tonight. There are many unknowns, and arguing will get us nowhere. There is no doubt we will see a claiming tonight, but after such an event, there must be no hostilities between cabins."
A single head went up in Hephaestus Cabin.
"Yes, Ramses, setting fire to Athena Cabin counts as an act of hostility."
Ten kids around him groaned in unison: "No fair!"

I got the sudden, terrible feeling that to this camp, violence *was* the norm.
"Gods I could really use Annabeth right now…" Chiron muttered, unaware that my sensitive ears could hear him.
I had no idea who Annabeth was, but if Chiron was wishing for her right now, then my best guess was she was either dead or busy, and typically kept the peace at Camp Half-Blood.
"Pace." Chiron said, gesturing to the quickly growing tension among four hundred something quickly-arming campers. "Lend me a hand here."
Pace, an older boy with blonde hair and grey eyes like…well, like most of the Athena Cabin, stood up and raised his arms.
"Look," Pace said as amicably as he could. "It shouldn't matter what his mom or dad did. He'd a demigod. Like any of us. As such, we should treat him with respect. And we should definitely not insult the gods without rhyme or reason, should we?"
Chiron cringed. Pace clearly knew exactly what needed to be said, but his oratory skills desperately needed work.
It was an ending so foolish that even I could see there was really no other way to answer him.
You did not end something like that in the presence of a group of highly volatile campers…on a question.
Thirty campers started bickering with each other. The sound droned out any coherent argument, stopping it dead in its tracks. Bickering became arguing.
"And why not? If a god broke an oath on the River Styx, then they should pay for it, god or not, just like any of us have to!" A child of Athena insisted.
Nobody had expected the first shot to be fired by an Athena camper. That proved disastrous. Because if the camp voice of reason was joining in, then it was fair game to everyone else.
"I'm getting Annabeth back from Mount Olympus after this. I don't care what needs redesigning still." Chiron groaned.
*Step back.* I told myself. *Intervening will only make this worse.*
"I think Hermes, the god of **deception**, broke the oath. Nothing that guy likes more than spiting other people." Someone suggested.
"Our dad would never—" The Hermes Cabin stood up.
"I'm thinking Hecate…" A son of Aphrodite recommended. "I mean, she hates Percy, right? Why would she care about some stupid oath?"
"Hey! Leave our mom out of this!" The Hecate Cabin stood up.
"Hey now, no need for shouting, I'm sure this can be settled—" Chiron boomed, only to be cut off.
*You are an outsider. This is not your battle. Patience.* I thought.
"Oh, but you know who really hates Percy?" A daughter of Apollo called out, with pure malice in her eyes. Hippocratic oath had never met her.
A hundred voices went quiet. They all realized it at the same time.
*YOU JUST MADE AN ENEMY FOR LIFE, BOY!*
He wasn't sure if that was someone talking or the voice speaking within his head.
"Ares!" Fifty campers said at the same time.
"Of course the divine-bone head breaks the oath on the River Styx. Probably can't even spell Styx!" Three children of Demeter laughed.
He doubted any of those three could spell Styx either.
Then a sword was drawn. Then another. Then another.
"You leave my dad alone!" Clarisse shouted, raising her spear. Thirty campers ducked instinctively, though I had no idea why. It wasn't like it was some magic spear. "My dad hasn't ever liked Percy Jackson, but he wouldn't violate an oath on the River Styx just to spite him!"
*This will not happen, this will not happen.* I thought.
*It's happening.* The voice muttered.
Two hundred campers fought against each other. Chiron pounded his foot on the hearth, but it was no use. Blood was in the air.
Then, a voice rang out through the dining pavilion.
"DROP YOUR WEAPONS!"
*You really should not have done that.* The voice said.
*I know.* I thought. *Don't remind me.*
Every single weapon in the ampitheater, sword, shield, grappling hook, spear, dagger, knife…clattered to the floor and stubbornly stayed there.
"Huh…?" A camper wondered.
"Who…?" Another asked.
The voice had been five times louder than mine, and twice as deep.
Eyes turned to Chiron, who shook his head. Then to Percy, who appeared to be doing an Excalibur impression of trying to free his recently-drawn sword from the stone bench in front of him. It wasn't working.
Then they turned to me, the unclaimed kid that nobody knew the father of.
*Crap.*
I closed my mouth one millisecond too late, revealing my deed. I looked across the ampitheater to Rachel Dare, Oracle of Delphi, to see what her reaction was. Her eyes were pure, Radium green. Her mouth opened, and her voice carried over every other in the ampitheater.
"A child of war, born of the war god's forge,
Shall heed the call of the west, to the mountains' guard,
Where a god's oath bleeds into the Earth's torn skin,
To defeat a rival all his own.
The forged shall stand where fates entwine,
Shall walk where gods and titans bled,
A god's wrath shall fall upon his son,
As the living meet the dead."

Her friends caught her as she fell, escorting her into a seat as she coughed up green mist and sputtered like a lawnmower fresh out of gas. She smiled at me, like: *See? The future mugs me.*

Then my father chose the worst time in the history of humankind to claim me. A hundred campers, minimum, stared at a spot just above my head. I looked up. A seven foot bronze spear surrounded by a glow the color of fresh blood rotated, suspended on the air itself. Chiron was going to make the announcement of my heritage, but I missed it, because I knew that symbol anywhere. Ares. The war god. Battle, combat, conquest, all the things that had royally sucked about my life in the past three years all rolled into one. 
*Hey, look! We have a child of war right over here!* My father seemed to say.
I just turned sheet-white…and fainted into the horde of Hermes/unclaimed campers below me.
