AIDAN
**As the son of a theologian, I expected to at be good at least something in a giant camp full of activities.**

Clarisse gave me the tour. 

She was a terrible tour guide.

As we walked along the stone path going through the camp, I saw all kinds of things.

A group of teenagers practicing basketball where three-pointers went in automatically. Nope.

A different group of attractive boys flexing their muscles and trying to catch the eye of some dryads. Not my style.

A few girls discussing war plans as they stared at a big map.

"What is the map for?" I asked Clarisse, my tone as flat as ever, which I didn't realize made me sound angry.

She raised an eyebrow, looking at me. She was still in her battle gear.

"The war games. They're scheduled to happen tomorrow. Everybody is getting excited." She said.

The words 'war games' made me tense up. That did not sound like a fun experience.

"You don't seem enthused." She noticed. "Huh. Weird. Even Percy kind of looked forward to it."

Strategy sounded interesting. War did not.

"So do you have any…special talents? Anything you've always been…supernaturally good at?" Clarisse asked me.

The image of myself, at 12, getting cornered by bullies and terrified of the *thing* happening flooded into my mind. Watching my limbs move of their own accord. Broken nose on one, broken ankle on the other. No idea how I did it.

*So how do I word this without getting thrown in a juvenile detention center?* I thought.

*Pretty simple. You don't.* The voice said.

*Good plan.* I thought.

"What you saw this morning…" I said cautiously. "…I don't know how I did that."

It is wonderful being so emotionally inexpressive by default. People don't realize you're hiding something.

"Alright…" She said, playing ball with this development. "…let's see if you can do it again."



*This is a terrible idea.* I thought, decked out in battle gear, heavy shield and armor, trying not to drop my sword…again.

*Have a little faith in yourself.* The voice suggested. *You've got me to make sure you don't die.*

*Don't die. Very comforting.* I thought.

The arena was a large space, about sixty feet in diameter with dozens of benches overlooking it from all sides. Clarisse was in…wait…

"This is training gear." She'd said, as I had equipped the chestplate. "You'll get proper stuff later."

…the same armor I was. The helmet, chestplate and boots. All of it.

*She was wearing training gear back there?* I thought.



"Just…do what comes natural to you…" She said, like I was an assignment she was not eager to be left with. I was the newbie. People like her were not supposed to be stuck sparring with people like me.

She raised her own sword. She started with the basics. A slash to my left, a slash to my right. I sidestepped so it wouldn't simply smack me in the face.

Her technique began to advance, and I began to falter.



"He'd doing great for a newbie!" One of the Ares campers called out from their sparring match across the arena. Each slash of metal against metal made my ears throb in pain.

I tried to stay focused. She bobbed and weaved.

*As pathetic as it would look,* I thought, *I'm down to just surrender.*

*You are correct. That would make you look pathetic.* The voice answered.

The repeated slashing of swords was giving me a minor headache already. I was very much done with this. Clarisse pushed harder, delivering strikes that to her probably weren't problematic but to me were almost making me drop my sword.

"Easy, easy!" I said, my voice too naturally flat for my urgency to carry.

"Oh, is this too easy for you?" Clarisse asked.

I got the terrible feeling something was about to go very, very wrong.

"Yeah, c'mon Clarisse, really give it to him!" The Ares campers urged.

And suddenly I was helpless. Dodge after dodge, coming too fast now to sidestep, so instead I had to parry. Each clang made me ears beg for relief.

Then a switch flipped within her.

I was wincing, visibly wincing. "Clarisse, hey, you're going too far!"

My knees felt weak. I couldn't take any more of this. If she went any harder, she was going to take my head off.

She swung her sword, fast and hard in what I vaguely understood to be a side-slash.

*I am going to die.* I thought. *This is it.*

*Fiddlesticks.* The voice cursed. *Hang on.*

*If you parry the blow head on, hard as you can, you will block it.* It instructed.

I raised my sword, and in a fraction of a second, I felt strong. I felt the dread build in my gut as I wondered what injury she'd walk away with. I slashed forward as hard and as fast as my arms would permit.



The resulting collision was so loud that the sound made me instantly hit the ground, and every person in the camp was suddenly staring at me and Clarisse sparring.

"What the Hades was that?" I heard an older boy mutter in the vicinity of the Dining Pavilion.

I only realized what I'd done after I'd done it. Clarisse stared down at her sword in shock. I was on the ground in a heap.

"Are you okay, kid?" One of the kids muttered.

"Not possible." Clarisse whispered. I didn't know why, and I didn't care. "It has be a defective sword. A bad forge. An impurity in the metal."

Campers rushed over to see what had just happened.

"What's not possi—" A son of what I took to be Hephaestus said, only to stop himself. "Gods of Olympus!"

She placed the sword reverently on the ground, and it fell within my line of sight.

It took me a moment to figure out what what just happened.

The sword Clarisse had been holding was now bent in the middle, an indentation like a blade had smashed into it.

The new little notch in the bronze blade…was a full inch deep.



*Nice strike.* The voice commented, with clear pride.

I did not feel proud.

I was on the ground, shaking like a leaf.

"That, pal," Clarisse said, nudging me with her foot. "Is what happens when you pick on Clarisse La Rue."

Then, to her surprise, I didn't get up. I didn't move.

"Hey, it's Aidan, right? You okay?" A daughter of Athena asked me, offering a smile.

My brain had completely shut down. I was barely moving.

She picked my hand up. I didn't respond.

"Is he…hey, hoops, get over here. I need a doctor." She said.

The relevant son of Apollo trudged over.

"Is he…no, catatonic seizures don't present that way. I think he… Hoops placed a hand on my back, and seemed to use whatever kind of magic sense Apollo campers had to read my vitals. "Wow. Full-blown panic attack. He's out properly."

"A…panic attack? He was winning. Why would he be having a panic attack?" Clarisse asked, both kind of irritated and confused.

Hoops felt for a few more seconds.

"Panic attacks…" Hoops said with some venom now, looking directly at Clarisse.

"are the appropriate response "…you know, when your trainer tries to murder you."

My body was picked up and lifted onto a stretcher, moved to the infirmary.

Except I had no way of knowing that.

Because I'd just gone into a panic attack and passed out.
