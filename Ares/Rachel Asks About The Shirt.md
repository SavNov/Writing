AIDAN
After I had passed out, I had woken up in my new 'home': Ares Cabin.

It was a blatant middle finger to the idea that war and violence were supposed to suck. An Army cot meant to hold recruits going through PT more than teenage campers. A machine gun turret was mounted down the middle hallway of the cabin to be able to hit any intruders who dared enter unannounced head-on and without mercy. It was built like a barracks, but somehow worse, with camo walls and distant sounds of gunfire and artillery shells emanating from what had to be the ether.
The sound alone gave me an immediate migraine. I stumbled out of bed, having passed out *twice* in the span of twelve hours, stepped down the middle of the hallway like I expected every floor tile to explode—because I did—and made it out alive. Behind me, Clarisse pretended to not have been watching me from the furthest bunk down.

"Going already?" She grunted, attempting to look stern but I could tell that stern was the furthest thing from what she was.

*If I am going to die on this quest, I may as well make some friends.* I thought.

"The sounds of gunfire are giving me a migraine." I admitted, voice flat and steady.

"You get used to them." She assured me, with a smile that was not quite a smile.

*I doubt it.* I thought, stepping across a—
*Do not lower your foot.* The voice, Ares I now knew, boomed in my head. Given I already was nursing a migraine, I rubbed my ears as I kept my foot from touching the ground. I looked down.
Landmine, dead ahead.
I gingerly stepped backwards onto a tile I knew was safe.
"Thanks." I muttered, thinking aloud.
"Don't mention it." She said, sounding a bit weary, but not unkind.

For a moment, I considered bringing up that I had actually been talking to the Ares inside my head, then decided not to.
*Getting institutionalized now would be a bad idea.* I thought.
So I simply nodded in acknowledgement, and stepped more carefully forward.

After I left the Ares Cabin, and took note of the still-bitter campers grumbling and complaining with each other, I aimed for the one place I was willing to bet nobody else would be: The art studio.

The Apollo campers were currently trying to pick up their bows, so none of them bothered me as I walked in.

The art studio was a grand building, with big ornate limestone walls and ceramic siding as easels were everywhere covered in various paintings, couches sat in the corner covered in paint and broken pencil lead, but the big green line in the middle of the room going from right between my feet through the middle of the floor and to the opposing ceiling was very clear. The left half, with its various paintings of the gods in all their glory and various quests and musings, including several paintings of campers mid-kiss who I was sure would gladly kill each other before they considered doing so in real life. The right side was murals. Dozens and dozens of murals lining the walls, the floor, some even lining the ceiling, and every single canvas drenched in imagery to the point that my brain struggled to process it all.

A black-haired boy I had come to know as Percy Jackson stood on Mount Olympus, holding Zeus' Master Bolt. A girl in bronze armor holding a buzzing electric spear as she stood atop a boat labeled the CSS Birmingham. I had been to a Civil War museum and read about the Confederacy.
That boat did not exist.
For one thing, the city it was named after—Birmingham, Alabama—was not founded until 1871, six years after the Confederacy had already fallen.
But a tired artist had needed a boat, so here we were.
In another frame, a girl I recognized as looking similar to the Annabeth from Ares' War Room, same blonde hair, same grey eyes, same camisole, same dagger, kissed Percy Jackson at the bottom of Camp Half-Blood's lake.
"Girlfriend." I realized. "Where is she now?"

What I saw next literally knocked the wind out of me.
A forge, like the one on Rachel's shirt, but instead of being surrounded in the orange and yellow glow I would associate with Hephaestus, this one had a distinct heavy red glow to it. Ares. Hephaestus' forge made anything he wanted. Ares' forge made weapons of war. And for some stupid reason, I reached into my pocket, knowing it would be empty…
…and found the hat. It was sitting on my dresser, and now it was sitting in my pocket.
My lips formed the words *How?*.
I steadied myself on the nearest wall.
*How had Rachel painted the forge that I— oh.* I thought, then realized mid-sentence. *She is the Oracle of Delphi.*
Then another immediately on its heels.
*That is why she has been looking at me that way all day. She wants to know what I am.*
Ares did not comment.

I found a chair, sat, and tried to work out how the *Hell* I had managed to be so astronomically unlucky.

Time passed.

About twenty minutes later, Rachel stepped into the art studio behind me, seeing me give a thousand yard stare to a filing cabinet.

This day had not been supposed to go this way.

"That must be one fascinating filing cabinet." She remarked.

She stood behind me, placing a hand on my shoulder, realizing I hadn't reacted.

"Are you… are you in the middle of another panic attack?" She asked, both confused and worried.

For a moment, I considered not responding at all. I had far too much to worry about at the moment, and was in no mood to talk with her. Then I paused. I realized I could not do that. I could not simply *not* talk to the girl who had painted the forge I had been born in.
I slowly nodded no.

"I would take Hecate or Hermes over Ares any day." I said flatly, though I knew I was anything but emotionless. Being emotionless would have made this easier.
No, I was Autistic.

She sensed sadness.

"Now, look, as much I liked the thought that my chest was just *that* good, you aren't the pervert type." She said in a more teasing tone. "So what on my t-shirt of t-shirts has intrigued you so?"

I went completely quiet, and she realized I'd stiffened.

*Please don't ask.* I thought.

*She's going to ask.* Ares said.

*Shut up.* I told the god of everything I'd hated about the past three years.

To my astonishment, the god of everything I'd hated about the past three years kept his trap shut.

"Lightning bolt? Prison cell? You know, you seem like the type to be intrigued by prison design…" She said, studying my expression carefully, so close to my face that I felt her breath on my cheek.

I was an Autistic male who's expression and tone rarely matched his real emotions.

It was moments like these that stopped rarely from being never.

I was blushing heavily.

"Are you…oh, you are! Adorable! Unfortunately, I consider it my life's solemn duty to make Annabeth Chase hate me, so…"

I stared down at the floor.

I felt her hand on my shoulder.

"It's the forge." I admitted flatly.

She sat down on front of me, taking off the shirt, which made me close my eyes until she informed me she was wearing one underneath.

I looked at her, and at the shirt.

"You're hiding something big. I can tell." She said, her voice soft and kind.

I nodded like the admission physically hurt me.

"What does born in the war god's forge actually mean?" She asked me softly, cradling my chin.

"I've been… fighting monsters for three years now. I think that's what it means." I said flatly.

Her eyes widened as she formed a "Wow" with her lips.

"Technically the truth, but you're still hiding the whole truth. You must be terrified of people finding out." She realized. "You don't have to tell me if you don't want to. I'm just… curious about the future."

I nodded no, and she stepped back.

"Please don't tell anyone." I said flatly, but emotionally I was pleading.

She nodded no.

"Not a soul. I'm the Oracle of Delphi. I have to be good at keepong secrets or else the future changes entirely."

I figured that was a pretty good assurance.

"So… I have a question." I said flatly.

She nodded reassuringly.

"Sup?"

"When you're the Oracle, I noticed that the spirit kind of… seizes your body."

She nodded. 

"The future mugs me."

"Do you hear Apollo's voice in your head or something?" I asked.

She arched an eyebrow.

"Why do I get the feeling this question matters vastly more to you than it does to me?"

"Do you?" I pressed, tone unwavering.

She nodded no.

"Does he speak to you?" She asked.

*I do not need to tell you how to answer that question.* Ares said.

"No. I just didn't know how being Oracle worked."

"Wonderful." She smiled. "Because you know what?"

I didn't answer her. I'd gotten trapped with that more times than I could count.

"You're good." She whistled. "I don't know how being Oracle of Delphi works either. I just kind of wing it and act like I knew it was all going to happen from the start."
