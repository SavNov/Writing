AIDAN
Rachel stepped into the art studio behind me, seeing me give a thousand yard stare to a filing cabinet.

This day had not been supposed to go this way.

"That must be one fascinating filing cabinet." She remarked.

She stood behind me, placing a hand on my shoulder, realizing I hadn't reacted.

"Are you… are you in the middle of another panic attack?" She asked, both confused and worried.
I slowly nodded no.
"I would take Hecate or Hermes over Ares any day." I said flatly.
She sensed sadness.
"Now, look, as much I liked the thought that my chest was just *that* good, you aren't the pervert type." She said in a more teasing tone. "So what on my t-shirt of t-shirts has intrigued you so?"
I went completely quiet, and she realized I'd stiffened.
*Please don't ask.* I thought.
*She's going to ask.* Ares said.
*Shut up.* I told the god of everything I'd hated about the past three years.
To my astonishment, the god of everything I'd hated about the past three years kept his trap shut.
"Lightning bolt? Prison cell? You know, you seem like the type to be intrigued by prison design…" She said, studying my expression carefully, so close to my face that I felt her breath on my cheek.
I was an Autistic male who's expression and tone rarely matched his real emotions.
It was moments like these that stopped rarely from being never.
I was blushing heavily.
"Are you…oh, you are! Adorable! Unfortunately, I consider it my life's solemn duty to make Annabeth Chase hate me, so…"
I stared down at the floor.
I felt her hand on my shoulder.
"It's the forge." I admitted flatly.
She sat down on front of me, taking off the shirt, which made me close my eyes until she informed me she was wearing one underneath.
I looked at her, and at the shirt.
"You're hiding something big. I can tell." She said, her voice soft and kind.
I nodded like the admission physically hurt me.
"What does born in the war god's forge actually mean?" She asked me softly, cradling my chin.
"I've been… fighting monsters for three years now. I think that's what it means." I said flatly.
Her eyes widened as she formed a "Wow" with her lips.
"Technically the truth, but you're still hiding the whole truth. You must be terrified of people finding out." She realized. "You don't have to tell me if you don't want to. I'm just… curious about the future."
I nodded no, and she stepped back.
"Please don't tell anyone." I said flatly, but emotionally I was pleading.
She nodded no.
"Not a soul. I'm the Oracle of Delphi. I have to be good at keepong secrets or else the future changes entirely."
I figured that was a pretty good assurance.
"So… I have a question." I said flatly.
She nodded reassuringly.
"Sup?"
"When you're the Oracle, I noticed that the spirit kind of… seizes your body."
She nodded. 
"The future mugs me."
"Do you hear Apollo's voice in your head or something?" I asked.
She arched an eyebrow.
"Why doI get the feeling this question matters vastly more to you than it does to me?"
"Do you?" I pressed, tone unwavering.
She nodded no.
"Does he speak to you?" She asked.
*I do not need to tell you how to answer that question.* Ares said.
"No. I just didn't know how being Oracle worked."
"Wonderful." She smiled. "Because you know what?"
I didn't answer her. I'd gotten trapped with that more times than I could count.
"You're good." She whistled. "I don't know how being Oracle of Delphi works either. I just kind of wing it and act like I knew it was all going to happen from the start."
