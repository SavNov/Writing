Words he hadn't been meant to hear.
"I… um… I think I like him."

And another voice haunting him in his dreams like a Siren.
"Have you not grown tired of being the gods' pawn, young Son of Hermes?"
And he *was* tired. He was angry. All those summers watching as Hermes did nothing as his mother drank herself into illness to make the visions stop, the gods did nothing about all the unclaimed campers even as resentment grew, tossed them around like ragdolls. The ones who wanted the quests never got them, and the ones who wanted nothing to do with the gods, like him, were pracically drowning in them.

Then he'd taken the coward's way out. Made the deal with the devil.
*Steal the Master Bolt and the Helm of Darkness. Force them to care about their children. No harm shall come to you for it as surely as I live.*
The field trip to Mount Olympus. A rare act of the gods wanting to deal with their children. Taking a chance on the Son of Hermes.
He'd betrayed their trust. And Kronos hadn't lied. By the time they'd discovered what he'd done, he was already using Backbiter to vanish without a trace. Assembling an army of rogue demigods who'd been betrayed just like him, monsters eager to see the titans rule again. *Kronos, they are right where you said they would be. Your plan worked flawlessly.*

Capturing Percy and Annabeth. Begging them to take his side on the war. They didn't, because they were young and innocent and had no idea just how eager the gods were to discard them.
The moment he heard what they were after, though, and why, Kronos had needed to give the orders for just a few minutes because he was non-linear. The Golden Fleece. Tantalus in control of Camp Half-Blood. Saving Thalia's tree.
Kronos had given him the exact words to say to Percy, because the only ones he could think about were: *If you can make the ship tilt hard enough to the side, you can fetch the key and get out of her and bring Thalia back.*
The Titan King had gotten more controlling when he'd struggled. *The gods lay out one quest about someone you care about and you fold like a pair of pants. Have you no integrity?*

"Obeyed Kronos knowing it was the wrong path forward." The voices commented.

That had been the first time he'd really, truly thought that he was on the wrong side of the war. But he was still angry. And Kronos used that expertly.

*I lost someone I cared about. Not a pleasant feeling, Big Z, is it?* He'd thought. *But demigods don't get do-overs. Maybe it's time gods didn't either.*

Now, though, he found himself wishing that when he'd considered jumping overboard, he'd done it.

They took to the Labyrinth, an easy way into Camp Half-Blood, and literally anywhere else. The walls constantly changed and shifted. His men mapped it out alongside him. He was the son of the god of messengers and travelers, that Labyrinth would take him wherever he willed it to.

He mentally skipped ahead. The rest blurred together. Beckendorf dying as the Princess Andromeda blew up. Blackmailing Silena into doing more spying.
*Go ahead. Tell them everything. See if they believe a word you say after they find out you're a traitor! We've all lost people we care about, Silena, we move on!*
*We move on.* He'd thought, knowing full well that he was doing the opposite.

He remembered the last moment he'd felt fully in control of his own body.
"Thalia Grace is alive. The Golden Fleece brought her back." Silena had said.
He'd dropped to his knees instantly. She wouldn't lie to him. Thalia was alive. And that was all he could think about.
Thalia is alive.
Thalia is alive.
**Thalia Grace is alive.**

Kronos had replied.
"Another piece comes into play. Bitterly ironic that none of the eldest gods managed to keep their oath. But that is why I have come. To be their judge."

Luke Castellan had become a prisoner in his own body. His limbs had sent troops to march on Manhattan while Typhon distracted the gods. Typhon was not to destroy the gods, merely delay them. No. Mount Olympus was his, Kronos', to take, as were the gods.

The rest played on hyperspeed, blurring so quickly that the Judges could see easily but he could not.

"I am a traitor to the gods." Luke declared quietly. "Killer of demigods, harbinger of the end of Mount Olympus, servant of Kronos. I have nothing to say for myself besides that I was blinded my own anger and grief."

The Judges nodded.

"It is settled then." They echoed in unison.

He waited for the verdict he knew was coming. The Fields of Punishment. Torture forever.

"The Court of the Dead has reached its' final decision. Luke Castellan, Son of Hermes, traitor to Olympus and servant of Kronos, is sentenced to the Fields of Punish—"

An explosion rattled the air next to him. He felt cosmic power.

*Of course,* Luke thought, *Kronos insists on torturing me for my treason himself.*

"Now just wait a minute, you three. I've got something to say." The voice boomed.

Not Kronos, he realized. Ares. The god of war.
His punishment was about to get a lot more specific and cruel.

He turned to see Ares in… military dress. Formal. A number of medals of his chest, but no weapon in his hand.

"God of War, if you could just allow us to fini—" The mask on the left boomed, only to be cut off.

"All soldiers who die on the losing side of a war fall under my domain." Ares stated, like it was an order. "Therefore, I DO get a say in what happens after they die."

The Judges reluctantly allowed Ares to continue while Luke tried to figure out what was going on.

"And do you know what I say, little mino?" Ares commanded.

The Judges were silent.

"I say he gets a fair trial. Not… whatever the devil this was." Ares stated.

Ares teleported in an explosion of shrapnel to stand in front of Luke.

"Let's try this again. Luke Castellan, Son of Hermes, HERO of the Second Titan War, how do you plead?" Ares said.

He tried to say guilty, but it didn't work.

"Innocent." His mouth said.

"A hero? Do you have any idea who you are referring to, Ares?" The right Judge demanded.

"He killed himself with his little sister's dagger, stabbed right in the Achilles' Heel, in order to stop Kronos from destroying Olympus. I don't think there's any other word for a man like that besides Hero." Ares shouted.

“You want to sentence him for that? You want to talk body counts and destruction? Let’s talk about the First Titan War.” Ares growled, drawing closer to the Judges of the Court of the Dead.

“My father dragged every Elder Cyclops, Fury, and Hyperborean giant he could find out of Tartarus and turned them loose on the world. You want to put him on trial? See how well he fares under your dainty little scale?”

“Or is this court only here to punish the losers?”
Total silence in the throne room. The gods did not care. All demigods were pawns. Except Ares did.
Ares only pressed on.
“You think we beat the Titans by playing by all your fancy rules?”

The Judge in the middle sighed.
"That was because…" The Judge said, but stopped themselves. "…he led an army against Mount Olympus. So many dead, and so many scarred."

"That IS what war does, no matter which side you stand on." Ares said.

"He defied the divine order! There are laws in place! There must be justice!" Another Judge insisted.

Ares had no reply. Laws and justice and order weren't his area.

Seeing their opportunity, the left Judge spoke up.

"The boy has already confessed that he is a traitor to the gods. He has already accepted his punishment. Your intervention means nothing."

Another flash of light, the sound of pages being turned.
A woman with grey eyes and blonde hair, dressed like an attorney in a black suit, appeared to the right of Ares.

"Do not mistake humility for guilty." She said, like she'd been litigating criminal cases all her life.

"What the— why are— why are you helping me?" Luke asked, utterly bewildered.

Athena—goddess of wisdom, truth and justice—studied him and smiled.
**"Son of Hermes, you have ran to our aid more times than you can count. Let us run to yours."**

Never in all Luke's life had he ever imagined the gods would notice or care about all the quests he'd endured to help them with their squabbles.

"Laws serve the people, not the other way around. Laws inform people of right and wrong, they give instructions on how to punish and how to help, they guide what we ought to do with what we have and what the state ought to do with what it has. And, at the moment, this court appears to be using the law as a whip to tear a young demigod down rather than see him for all he is." Athena said.

"This is irrelevant to—" The Judge attempted, but the goddess shut him down.

"No. You do not get to instruct the goddess of wisdom and truth on how a court for discerning a demigod's true nature ought to be run." She said. "Now, let us look once more at his life, this time with the benefit of hindsight."

At the threat of the Court of the Dead, the one standard where justice and fairness for the dead was required without exception, losing its legitamacy due to corruption, the Lord of the Underworld was forced to intervene.

In an instant, the entire court room was drenched in shadow so thick it clogged Luke's breath and made several gods cough, every source of light went out, and even Apollo's light flickered for one moment. Smoke drenched them all from head to foot.

The voice, flat and cold and deathly still yet tougher than steel, that Percy Jackson had cowered to all those years ago, spoke at last.
"I am Hades, Lord of the Underworld, the Rich One, the Hospitable One, the Fair One, the Judge over the Dead, Guardian over the Tomb of the Titans. And I have HAD ENOUGH."

That cold, stony expression betrayed nothing. No feeling, no care, only indifference. Yet those words carried more anger than even the most seething of expressions.

Luke Castellan could feel his chances of Elysium slipping through his fingers like the shadow now choking the throats of the gods that had chosen to help him after all these years.

"Yesterday, I made a promise to all of Olympus. I promised that all who died in the Battle of Manhattan, demigod, mortal and satyr, would be offered Elysium if they chose to have it. That includes Luke Castellan. As the god of war has so bluntly stated, all who die on the losing side of a battle are at Ares' jurisdiction. Ares, in accordance with this reasoning, has declared that the boy is a hero." Hades said, voice flat and cold and dead, like he was settling a dull legal dispute instead of deciding the eternal fate of a demigod mere meters away.

"In addition," Hades went on, now circling the Court of the Dead. "I have not forgotten the Great Prophecy. And its very words name Luke Castellan as the hero cursed blade shall reap. The product of a war that would never have broken out if three bickering brothers could manage to keep their word and not sire a child of the eldest gods to reach sixteen against all odds at all."

"And so—" The god's eyes met the left Judge's with a burning intensity. Not fiery. No, cold. Icy. The way liquid nitrogen can cook marshmellows. "Luke Castellan shall go to Elysium."

The right Judge cowered slightly as the god towered over him easily. "And he shall go there proud of all he has done."

"Because I, Hades, God of the Underworld, Judge of the Dead… "

Hades stooped down to stare directly into the middle judge's eyes.

"…unlike this court of knaves…"

He allowed the words to make their mark.

"…refuse to call the Oracle of Delphi a liar."
