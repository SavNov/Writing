ZEUS
He was taking a risk of Olympian proportions.
His fingers traced the edges of his throne. This was it. This was the end. He had mere days until his Master Bolt would go missing and he would discover himself at the other end of it. Samson would charge into the throne room, armed to the teeth, or maybe sneak some poison into his cup while he was drinking at one of the many great Olympian parties. He didn't know, and that was the scary apart.
His throne room was lined with scenes of his own valor. Himself running Father through with his own scythe. Him standing in the wreckage of Father's throne room, Greek fire purning behind him on Mount Othyrs. Him holding his Master Bolt high in the air, Poseidon just slightly shorter than him, and Hades standing on the step below him on the stairs of Mount Olympus. Him, graciously of course, agreeing to a truce with a smaller, shorter Perseus Jackson, Master Bolt in hand, happy that the demigod had returned his weapon, and himself saving the world from all-out war. A final painting in the corner of the room, turned backward so he wouldn't have to look at it.
Hevel, all hevel.
Mataiotēs.
Paintings made for terrible weapons. They lied, too.
He flicked his wrist.
The final painting flicked around and spun in the air to face him.
Him, both of his brothers, his sisters, all of them with their great Elder Cyclopes-forged weapons. In order to fully capture how grand and powerful the titan tornado was so that defeating it would look impressive, the mansions below were microscopic. The gods, even the elder gods like him, looked like little more than annoying gnats pestering Uncle Typhon for attention.
*Fight me, uncle!*
*Ooh, me next, me next!*
It was meant to make them look like little Davids beating their Goliath, the power of the gods despite formidable adversaries.
All it made him feel was dread. The memory of how close he'd come to losing Mount Olympus, of having his own kingdom saved by a young son of his brother, a little demigod. He felt like he was walking on eggshells. How long until one of his brothers turned on him, made a grab for his power just as he'd made grabs for theirs for so many millennia? That was why they were forbidden from having kids, after all. World War 2. The last big war. With so many children of the Big Three fighting, the Earth had become functionally uninhabitable. The Japanese emperor had said it best in a movie thirty years after:
I fear all we have done is to awaken a sleeping giant and fill him with a terrible resolve.
And that is what he was about to do. Push against a rule the Fates had written out millennia ago, and try to pry water from celestial bronze. *No god shall interfere with or intervene in a demigod quest.*
*A King cannot show favoritism to his own children.*
*To strike against the throne of the titans once more risks a war Mount Olympus is in no state to fight, and cannot currently win.*
He raised his Master Bolt. Miles below, through the storm, Clarisse La Rue asked Lord Zeus for a favor. A single bolt of lightning. Something he would never have given the time of day before. A demigod asking for the spawn of his signature weapon?
Even if Clarisse's electric spear could store the voltage, that meant giving a daughter of the war god a weapon powerful enough to knock him off his throne if wielded correctly.
*She is not my child. Not directly. She is my granddaughter, and this is a once-in-a-lifetime event.*
It sounded shaky and selfish even to him.
He hoped it would be enough justification.
*It was a natural bolt of lightning. You may believe that everything that happens in the sky is my personal responsibility, but I am a king, and some things are beneath my purview.* He would lie.
He didn't know if it would work.
His hands shook around the weapon an Elder Cyclopes twenty times his size had decided to forge for a puny god. His very greatest weapon wasn't even forged by his own hand. It had been a gift.
Now, he passed that gift onward, and prayed that this would not be another error.
He threw the bolt, the lightning erupting from it and down below to where the girl had requested. *Puny girl.* He thought. That Elder Cyclopes fired back at him. *Okay, puny god, I will humor you.*
His Master Bolt returned to his finger tips again like Riptide did to Percy.
*Go forth, daughter of Ares.* Zeus commanded in his mind, knowing she couldn't hear him. There was a big, nasty storm in the way, with plenty of lightning. Plausible deniability for the Olympian that had to be impartial, never choosing sides. *Use my lightning as my hands are tied, be courageous as I yield to fear, do what I cannot.*
He eyed the painting of Father defeating Ouranous with that vile Scythe. Then the painting of himself defeating Father with that same Scythe. Killed by his own weapon.
*Give me hope, daughter of war, that fate can be bent. That my end is not already written.*
He watched Perseus Jackson lift that Master Bolt high in the painting that Poseidon had left on his wall to remind him that he could not be so reckless with demigods. Then the propaganda piece of Samson holding that same Bolt, lightning cracking across the sky to meet it as he stood atop Mount Diablo in triumph. The caption read: JOIN SAMSON'S ARMY! HE'S THE NEW ZEUS!
He trembled with rage, letting his Master Bolt roar with power and spark electricity, disintegrating bricks and leaving jagged cracks in the wall that he allowed to remain, but he knew that was just Fear's way of playing pretend. Giving Pandora that box had been an entertaining bit of sadism. One that he had now paid for with enough interest to make whoever held that account richer than Hades himself. He'd been so bone-headed back then that it made Samson look wiser.
Maybe that was why Samson was rising to power and acclaim so quickly. He would make a better King of Olympus, maybe. King of Diablo. Even the name Olympians had been childish, a time from his far younger years. You certainly didn't see the Titans calling themselves the Othyrsians. They thought they'd all been so high and mighty back then, able to take on the world. Foolhardy teenagers. He could hardly blame the demigods. The truth was, they were small fish in a very large, very unkillable pond. But that was why this was so important. He had to show he'd changed, that he'd learned. That he could be a real King of Olympus. Heavy is the crown, and his subjects were strong enough to rebel now.
*Otherwise...I may lose everything.*
