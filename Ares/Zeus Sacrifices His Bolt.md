ZEUS
He was taking a risk of Olympian proportions.
His fingers traced the edges of his throne. This was it. This was the end. He had mere days until his Master Bolt would go missing and he would discover himself at the other end of it. Samson would charge into the throne room, armed and ready, or maybe sneak some poison into his cup while he was drinking at one of the many great Olympian parties. He didn't know, and that was the scary apart.
His throne room was lined with scenes of his own valor. Himself running Father through with his own scythe. Him standing in the wreckage of Father's throne room, Greek fire burning behind him on Mount Othrys. Him holding his Master Bolt high in the air, Poseidon just slightly shorter than him, and Hades standing on the step below him on the stairs of Mount Olympus. Him, graciously of course, agreeing to a truce with a smaller, shorter Perseus Jackson, Master Bolt in hand, happy that the demigod had returned his weapon, and himself saving the world from all-out war. A final painting in the corner of the room, turned backward so he wouldn't have to look at it.
Hevel, all hevel.
Mataiotēs.
Paintings made for terrible weapons when you were up against the work of an Elder Cyclopes. They lied, too.
The paintings made him seem like he was born to be a hero. A gracious king. A savior.
But he knew. He remembered.
He closed his eyes, and remembered.

"You are a king, little Zeus." Mother said, smiling down at him as she draped him in a grand robe. "You will do great, wonderous things."
The robe covered his boyish face for a moment, making them both laugh.
"Father will be mad." He said, though it had the same tone modern teens reserved for "Dad's totally gonna kill us".
She nodded.
"Your father has lost his way. Strayed too far. It's time for someone else to rule." She said.
When Mother had said he was a king, it had not been a question. From the day he was born, he had been told he was going to be a king.

The image changed again. Years blurred together. Feeding his colossal father a strong emetic. Saving his brothers and sisters. Being the hero. Kronos hunting him for years. Being on the run. A scallywag from justice. It had all been fun and games.
Then he'd declared war. And it had stopped being so fun. 11 years of Titanomachy. Fighting the big man was fun until you nearly lost your sister. Until your brothers Poseidon and Hades had argued about who was better.
Until you found yourself on a quest to enter the darkest, most horrific place ever created. And free three Elder Cyclopes from their chains who had been imprisoned there 11 years. And hope they didn't make you lunch.
He saw the painting of himself raising his Master Bolt for the first time, making it crackle with lightning. He'd had some fun with it, making all the onomatapoeai (I tried, GPT, I tried) noises a 13 year old god was due. *Zap! Bam! Sh-ck!* All glory and splendor in paintings.
He'd been utterly terrified of the Elder Cyclopes, and the hairs on the back of his neck had been standing up long before Hades had put on that Helm of Darkness. Hades had been delighted by the idea of venturing into Tartarus. "It's gonna be awesome! There's gonna be cool monsters and everything!"
Zeus had decided approximately three seconds after meeting that Elder Cyclopes that Hades could have the Underworld. No complaints from him.
Standing in front of his father, telling Father that it was his time to take the throne, raising that Scythe as the Elder Cyclopes held him down. The painting didn't quite capture how badly his legs had been shaking the entire time. Or how, the first time he'd wielded it, he'd missed the first strike. And the second. Only on the third had he actually managed to leave a small cut in Kronos' left big toe. Death by a thousand cuts, because each cut was just that tiny. He rectified that with a snap of his fingers, revealing young him in all his terrified, shaky glory.
He faced the painting of himself standing on the steps of Mount Olympus, Poseidon on the left and Hades on the right. All smiling.
Finally, soaked in sweat, and utterly delirious with both exhaustion and pride, he'd collapsed onto the throne of Mount Othrys. He'd woken up sitting in a palace on Mount Olympus, because his brothers and sisters had talked to the Elder Cyclopes while he'd been asleep, all eager to rule the world and make their mark. The moment the question came down to who got what, though, then there'd been trouble.
Every single one of them knew exactly what they wanted. He wanted to be Lord of the Sky, both to have Mount Olympus as his throne and to not be within a hundred-mile radius of that dark place ever again. Poseidon had always looked at Oceanus fondly, been at home among the stormy seas as they tossed and turned. And of course, Hades wanted the Underworld. He was fascinated by all the scary monsters, the darkness, he actually thought he could guard the pieces of Kronos.
They'd fought over all of it anyway. He said he wanted the sea, Poseidon said the Underworld seemed cool, Hades talked about how nice it would be to sit on Mount Olympus.
He rectified the painting with a snap of his fingers, showing himself and his two brothers bickering over things they didn't even want.
"And now, the modern era." He sighed, as if his entire life were nothing more than a documentary someone else was watching.
He flicked his wrist.
The final painting flicked around and spun in the air to face him.
Him, both of his brothers, his sisters, all of them with their great Elder Cyclopes-forged weapons. In order to fully capture how grand and powerful the titan tornado was so that defeating it would look impressive, the mansions below were microscopic. The gods, even the elder gods like him, looked like little more than annoying gnats pestering Uncle Typhon for attention.
*Fight me, uncle!*
*Ooh, me next, me next!*
It was meant to make them look like little Davids beating their Goliath, the power of the gods despite formidable adversaries.
All it made him feel was dread. The memory of how close he'd come to losing Mount Olympus, of having his own kingdom saved by a young son of his brother, a little demigod. He felt like he was walking on eggshells. How long until one of his brothers turned on him, made a grab for his power just as he'd made grabs for theirs for so many millennia? The Japanese emperor had said it best in a movie thirty years after:
I fear all we have done is to awaken a sleeping giant and fill him with a terrible resolve.
After the age of Titans, the age of Giants, and after so much of the age of Gods, Zeus allowed himself to remember the last time he had tempted fate.

World War 2 had broken out. Children of the eldest gods fighting. In hindsight, it seemed incredibly foolish. It was nearly a carbon copy of the war that had broken out a century before. Gods fighting, children of those gods fighting, divided by their natures and fragmented along sides.
Children of the sea god lying in boats on June 6 as German machine guns on the Normandy shore drowned them in bullets. Any of them would've been dead, but they were demigods. The water of the ocean spray healed them even as it soaked with the blood of their friends and brothers. German soldiers, some mortal, some monsters, some demigods fighting for what they viewed as the legacy of Germany and its great gods.
Children of Hades trying to survive the death camps, some German for what they called the long-nosers, some American for the Japs, sensing each execution long before they heard about it as monsters and demigods fought side by side to rid their country of the plague within its borders. If only they knew what Jews meant when they called their God long-of-nose.
Patient. Slow to anger.
One mortal man without a drop of divine blood managing to do more damage to the continent than the whole century of demigod quests before him.
German Luftwaffe pilots, mothers he'd taken a chance on in the midst of a country being actively buried by its own leadership, now summoning lightning from the Heavens above to strike down RAF pilots playing the same game they were.
Because three immortal, perpetually bickering brother kings had not been able to learn the lesson the American Civil War had broken out to teach them:
A fight among children of the Big Three cannot be won. Just as a fight between the Big Three couldn't be won.
The only ending to a real war among the gods.
**MUTUALLY ASSURED DESTRUCTION.**
So they'd made the pact. Sworn the oaths to each other. No more children of the eldest gods. Three kings had realized there was no point in being a king without a kingdom.
And then, fifty years later, a young Sally Jackson had won the heart of the king of the sea. He had risked his whole kingdom to have a son. On the same day that Percy Jackson had been born, the Oracle of Delphi had cried her song:
A half-blood of the eldest gods
Shall reach sixteen against all odds
And see the world in endless sleep
The hero's soul, cursed blade shall reap
A single choice shall end his days
Olympus to preserve or raze

Because when you break an Oath on the River Styx.
**Fate bites back.**

And that is what he was about to do. Push against a rule the Fates had written out millennia ago, and try to pry water from celestial bronze. *No god shall interfere with or intervene in a demigod quest.*
*A King cannot show favoritism to his own children.*
*To strike against the throne of the titans once more risks a war Mount Olympus is in no state to fight, and cannot currently win.*

Finally bite back, even as he knew this may be the last nail in a very ancient coffin.
He raised his Master Bolt. Miles below, through the storm, Clarisse La Rue asked Lord Zeus for a favor. A single bolt of lightning. Something he would never have given the time of day before. A demigod asking for the spawn of his signature weapon?
Even if Clarisse's electric spear could store the voltage, that meant giving a daughter of the war god a weapon powerful enough to knock him off his throne if wielded correctly.
*She is not my child. Not directly. She is my granddaughter, and this is a once-in-a-lifetime event.*
It sounded shaky and selfish even to him.
He hoped it would be enough justification.
*It was a natural bolt of lightning. You may believe that everything that happens in the sky is my personal responsibility, but I am a king, and some things are beneath my purview.* He would lie.
He didn't know if it would work.
His hands shook around the weapon an Elder Cyclopes twenty times his size had decided to forge for a puny god. His very greatest weapon wasn't even forged by his own hand. It had been a gift.
Now, he passed that gift onward, and prayed that this would not be another error.
He threw the bolt, the lightning erupting from it and down below to where the girl had requested. *Puny girl.* He thought. That Elder Cyclopes fired back at him. *Okay, puny god, I will humor you.*
His Master Bolt returned to his finger tips again like Riptide did to Percy.
*Go forth, daughter of Ares.* Zeus commanded in his mind, knowing she couldn't hear him. There was a big, nasty storm in the way, with plenty of lightning. Plausible deniability for the Olympian that had to be impartial, never choosing sides. *Use my lightning as my hands are tied, be courageous as I yield to fear, do what I cannot.*
He eyed the painting of Father defeating Ouranous with that vile Scythe. Then the painting of himself defeating Father with that same Scythe. Killed by his own weapon.
*Give me hope, daughter of war, that fate can be bent. That my end is not already written.*
He watched Perseus Jackson lift that Master Bolt high in the painting that Poseidon had left on his wall to remind him that he could not be so reckless with demigods. Then the propaganda piece of Samson holding that same Bolt, lightning cracking across the sky to meet it as he stood atop Mount Diablo in triumph. The caption read: JOIN SAMSON'S ARMY! HE'S THE NEW ZEUS!
He trembled with rage, letting his Master Bolt roar with power and spark electricity, disintegrating bricks and leaving jagged cracks in the wall that he allowed to remain, but he knew that was just Fear's way of playing pretend. Giving Pandora that box had been an entertaining bit of sadism. One that he had now paid for with enough interest to make whoever held that account richer than Hades himself. He'd been so bone-headed back then that it made Samson look wiser.
Maybe that was why Samson was rising to power and acclaim so quickly. He would make a better King of Olympus, maybe. King of Diablo. Even the name Olympians had been childish, a time from his far younger years. You certainly didn't see the Titans calling themselves the Othyrsians. They thought they'd all been so high and mighty back then, able to take on the world. Foolhardy teenagers. He could hardly blame the demigods. The truth was, they were small fish in a very large, very ancient pond. But that was why this was so important. He had to show he'd changed, that he'd learned. That he could be a real King of Oly…mpus. Heavy is the crown, and his subjects were strong enough to rebel now.
*Otherwise…I may lose everything. Olympus may bow to Diablo as Othrys once bowed to us.*
