AIDAN
At first, all I knew was that I was falling. The air smelled like death, cold and soot. The wind smacked me in the face over and over again as I fell. I quickly realized where I was going.
And it enraged me.
…
The God of the Underworld faced me head-on, unafraid.
"Do you understand why you are here?" He asked.
This was the face of the man that had stopped me from killing Samson right at the moment I could've taken the swing and ended it all. Who had stopped me from saving my mother at the one moment I allowed myself to believe none of this was hopeless.
My fingers shook with rage, but not merely rage. That wasn't what this was. Not entirely.
This was the feeling that after my mother had been taken from me, nothing else in this world mattered. Not Samson's life, not mine, not Percy, not Clarisse, not Chiron. Not any one of the countless girls and boys who'd been absorbed into this huge tragedy. It was a terrible, cold feeling that made words themselves feeling like a distraction.
So no, Hades, I did not feel like talking to you right now and no, I do not understand why I matter to you so unbearably much that you would go out of your way to snatch me away from killing Samson and saving my mother right when I was so inconceivably close.
His tone made me pause, though. Just for a fraction of a second.
It was unlike that of Ares, or Clarisse, or anyone I'd ever met besides my mom sometime, really. It was stone-cold to match his stone-cold face. I was so angry. My fists clenched so tightly my knuckles were white.
Every time I closed my eyes, I saw that same pained expression of my terrified mother enduring Samson circling her like a predator observing his prey. That cocky, arrogant grin. I'd been so close this time…and now Hades was in my way.
I was very much done pretending to be a hero.
"Because my dad demanded a favor. A god's wrath shall fall upon his son." I quoted, voice cold and flat but also deeper and darker now.
I looked around me. I was in this twisted man's throne room, all skulls and green and death and skeleton servants and centuries-old stone bricks.
Hades did not smile. Did not laugh. Did not argue back.
He instead seemed to be…like me? His tone was cold and flat like mine, not putting any deliberate effort into making his own emotions come through. He studied me like I was a child having a tantrum. Not with pity, but with concern.
"No, that is incorrect."
I expected him to say something else, but he just sat there, on his throne, staring at me. Letting the silence build between us. I wasn't feeling chatty. I wanted my mom back, I wanted to kill Samson myself. I wanted justice. My legs trembled. I glared at him, eyes full of hate.
Neither of us said a word. A minute. Two. The torches burned faintly in the fireplace as people cried down below us, begging for it all to be over. Hades didn't look fazed. Then again, I didn't look fazed either, yet I knew I was. On top of every pained weep, I imagined my mother's expression, down there, crying with them for Samson to stop.
Three minutes. Five.
Hades did not speak.
I did not either.
He simply drummed his finger absentmindedly to pass the time and waited for me to crack, like the silence was river slowly pouring over and eroding my rocky surface.
Patient. Unyielding.
I had the sudden, terrible feeling that Hades simply would *wait a thousand years* for me to open my mouth and yield.
I stood firm.
Ten minutes. Fifteen. My hands began to unclench. My body ached. My eyes finally drooped.
"Because I angered you." I guessed again, voice unwavering in its flatness. "With my bravado, and performance, pretending I was immortal."
It was what Percy would've said. But Percy had always said that Hades was the most misunderstood god.
He paused for a full twenty seconds.
"I have watched Olympus for many centuries now, son of…" He said, voice firm and cold and flat, but I sensed he was trying to find the right words. "…Elisa Travis. Bravado and performance and spectacle do not fool me. Not anymore."
*So you knew then. You heard my prayers. You know me.* I thought.
"You do not expect me to behave like my father. To want honor and glory." I realized slowly.
He nodded no, but his expression didn't waver.
"I know who you are, Aidan Travis. I was listening to every word you prayed. It is not often that I receive prayers from demigods, especially those in the throws of a quest that occurs so far beyond my domain." Hades said, every word unwavering. "I knew what you really wanted behind all of that bravado. I knew you were making no effort to fool me. I can hardly blame a demigod for his actions taken under duress."
I wasn't watching his face. And that was a strange relief, because he wasn't putting any information into his facial expressions. Nor his tone of voice.
*Under duress.* I thought. It felt like such a strange way to describe it, but not wrong. *Yes, under duress.*
"After all, if fear of failure, fear of not living up to one's own potential, discounted our heroics, then my brothers would not be sitting where they are now." Hades said flatly.
The idea that Zeus had fallen into the same trap I did. Or Poseidon. Pretending to be happy to free the world from Kronos while they felt crushed under the weight of what they were being expected to do. That nearly knocked the wind out of me. But it also confirmed a sneaking suspicion I'd had about them since I'd first read up on Greek myths after getting attacked by a wind spirit two years ago. Gods were people, too.
My chest heaved.
"You…you were listening. You…you understand me. You…you don't blame me for what I've done?" I asked, my tone betrayed nothing, but as two neurodivergetn people often did, we understood each other in that strange way. The words told enough of the story.
Hades nodded no.
"For killing an Empousa with your bare hands? Olympus clapped. For being immune to Charmspeak? Aphrodite was genuinely unnerved. For deceiving an electrokine descendant of Zeus into knocking the lights out to give you an opening to escape? Zeus admired such cleverness. The gods have been watching, son of Elisa, and they do not yet know what to make of you. The prophesied Child of War, who wants nothing to do with combat. You are a living contradiction. You have done something that has not yet been done before." Hades said, voice not wavering from its flat and dead register.
The torches dimmed.
"You have compelled indifferent Olympians to work out which side they are on. To stare down their own failings and reckon with them. That is a feat that the Fates attempted to accomplish with Percy Jackson, but now you have forced even the boy's longest enemies to yield."

I felt special, just for a second, then remembered what I had to look forward to.
"My mother. He took my mother. And now I have to kill him to get her back, because I am the only one who can. Why did you stop me?" I asked.
Hades met my eyes.
"Who are you?"
"I am…" I thought for a long moment. "Aidan Travis. Son of Ares. The Child of War, born of the war god's forge."
A beat passed.
"A demigod. Son of Ares. The one destined to defeat Samson. The only one who can."
Another beat passed.
"I am…the Child of War. But…not really. I don't like fighting, and swords are always so loud, and I don't do good under stress and…"
Hades looked me, and gave me a deliberate smile of encouragement.
"…and I am alone. Lonely. Yearning so desperately for connection that the time I lost myself in the tender embrace of an Empousa, I was willing to die to feel its touch. My dad hates me. My dad doesn't understand me. I don't want to be a warrior. I don't want to fight for honor and glory. I want to stay home, read my books, study, learn. I don't want to…"
Hades ascended from his throne, walked over, and held me in his arms like a father would. I leaned forward, head in his chest. Hades felt…strangely warm. His arm went around me.
"…be a demigod, half-blood. I just want to be me. Is that…is that okay? To just save my mom, go home, and forget this quest ever happened?"
"Nephew of mine, son of Ares and Elisa Travis, I would not have it any other way for you. It is not your job to judge others. It is mine. And Hades says thus. Those who do heroic things, those who do what they do not want to do, in order to fight for something braver than themselves. Those who do not wish to be remembered for their deeds. Those are the men and women that Elysium was first built to hold."

"So why am I here?" I asked, voice still flat but now with a deliberate softness in it.
"You are here because you are a demigod, just as Samson is, trying to disprove his point that demigods can defeat gods by coming at him as a demigod. You have nearly lost yourself trying to be what you assume you must be."
Hades didn't move away. There was a mutual understanding between us that a deeply emotional moment could happen at the same as a philosophical dissertation because the rules dividing the two things were written for people who did not function in the same ways we did.
"Only I can defeat him." I countered. "A rival all his own."
"Prophecy says one thing. Logic necessitates another. Which do you choose to follow?" Hades asked me.
I paused. I felt the torches in the braziers burn slightly brighter now.
"If I follow the prophecy to the letter, nothing will change." I realized.
"Indeed. You will take Samson's place. You will become exactly what Camp Half-Blood already believes you to be."
I looked down at my feet through Hades' cloak.
"We only understood what the mountains' guard even was after we crossed into California. Why would we assume we know what the prophecy means by 'A rival all his own'?" I asked myself.
"Now," Hades said, allowing his face to reflect his emotional state as he pushed his lips into a wide smile. "you have begun to understand what the theologians before you took decades to grasp."
I paused for a full ten seconds.
"Time can only be experienced forward. But only understood in reverse."

I looked inside of myself. Remembering all the times Ares had helped me, had defended himself within me. I had a portion of the god of war inside of me, and Samson would only truly be defeated by a god. The valley leading to his fortress was described as a god's oath bleeding into the earth's torn skin.
Ares' broken oath. Not claiming me.
"The rival isn't me." I realized, stunned.
I looked at the golden lines buzzing just beneath my skin. I pictured the spirit, a spirit, of what I now knew was the River Lethe. And her brother, the River Phlegathon. She was smiling at me. Talking about how Samson would feed upon me.
An Oath on the River Styx broken by the Olympian god of war.
*She didn't flee because she knew Samson would kill me later. She fled because she knew she couldn't.*
"I understand what that prophecy has been trying to say now. At least, in pieces." I admitted.

Hades released me. I didn't fully understand the details, but I didn't need to. I had enough now.
I eyed the ceiling.
"We'll…be able to talk again, right?" I asked, suddenly nervous. Even if I didn't show it naturally, sweat glands didn't lie.
"Without a doubt. You have a hard battle ahead of you." Hades said.
I nodded.
"I do. But somebody else has it worse."

Hades snapped his fingers, and a branch descended from the ceiling, wrapping one of its smaller branchs around my wrists. It carried up the hole in the ceiling I'd dropped out of, golden lines bursting into my veins as Ares within me readied himself for what we both knew was coming.
He and I just knew that I wasn't going to come out at the same place I'd entered. That wouldn't make sense. Hades keeping a hole into the Underworld open any longer than necessary could cause serious issues if anyone was stupid enough to fall into it.
*Yes. Very stupid.* Ares agreed, and I could hear him grinning.
*I was distracted by the giant wall of flesh and bronze trying to murder me. Cut me some slack.* I thought back.
Ares was not going to let me live that down for a while.
