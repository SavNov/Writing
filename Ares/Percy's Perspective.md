PERCY
**From the moment he'd seen Aidan arrive at camp, Percy had been nervous.** He'd known Aidan was different, unusual, strange. He had this aura that reminded him of Nico almost. That distant, cold look like he was a kid with way too much to carry and way too long to dwell on that.

But of course, he was Percy Jackson. Expecting things to go well for him now was like standing in the middle of No Man's Land and being shocked when somebody starts shooting at you. It had been four weeks of Iris messages with Annabeth as she helped her mom redesign Olympus after the Second Titan War had wrecked everything. You didn't realize how much you'd gotten used to having someone right by your side at all times until they were taken away, and it didn't matter by what.

He was technically supposed to be at home right now, to recover from the biggest war he'd ever seen, all the friends he'd just seen die, all the faces he'd never see again.
You know, teenager stuff.
Then, Rachel of all people, had sent him a letter. He hadn't shared his address with her. The letter had arrived anyway.
He prayed to Hermes that the Oracle of Delphi couldn't simply see his home address the same way it saw the future.

He remembered opening it, both terrified, and intrigued.
*Trade offer: Date for presence at Camp.* With a date, the fruit, sitting at the bottom of the letter for plausible deniability.
All he'd been able to think was: *Annabeth is going to kill me.*

Rachel, though, hadn't immediately taken him aside to reveal her message. Instead, she'd pointed to her newest shirt. "Like it? Made it myself."
It was an explosion of color and shadow that looked like somebody had vomited all over a modern art project…and improved it by some act of Apollo. At the bottom, a girl in bronze armor summoned a lightning bolt, spear raised. Decorative depiction of Thalia, obviously, even if the weapon was a bit off-kilter. Next to it, a cell block of ornate rows with various guards in Greek and what appeared to be Roman armor carrying various weapons, similar to what he'd seen aboard the Princess Andromeda, but this time with more dramatic flair. And then, near the center of her…you know…a big red and gold forge, the vat of molten metal that looked way too much like celestial bronze next to the anvil that looked very recently used.
With the benefit of hindsight, Percy had only now began to understood that the shirt…*was* the message.
"It looks very nice." He said with that awkward smile. Then, in his head, he'd said about twenty phrases in Ancient Greek to Aphrodite that will not be mentioned here as they are offensive to readers of all genders, sexes, orientations, races and personality types.

Then things got even weirder.
Aidan had showed up, sixteen years old and unclaimed, and been different. During his sparring with Clarisse, she'd gone too far and nearly murdered him. He'd had a panic attack, while he was ahead, and then parried her killer blow so hard he'd left an inch-deep notch in the training sword. It was bronze, but still. People should *not* be able to do that.
Now Clarisse, *Clarisse*, had apologized to him and looked utterly crushed under the weight of what she'd just done. He'd stood in the infirmary, watching the kid wake up, only for him to stare off into the ether for seven solid minutes before Percy had finally gestured for everyone to go, and Clarisse had chosen to stay behind. He'd seemed stressed and unnerved, despite pulling off the most impressive parry the Ares Cabin had ever seen.

What had just happened took the cake, though.
The conversation about who had broken the oath that Percy had turned down *immortality* to get—godhood, immortal, live forever, be super powerful, the works—to get from the gods. Aidan was sixteen and unclaimed. Whoever his godly parent actually was, they were three good years past the mandatory deadline. Thirty-three months if you got technical. Conversation became argument. Malcolm Pace had tried to keep the peace, but it hadn't been enough. Once the Athena Cabin started slinging verbal ammo, everyone else had decided it was fair game. Hermes, then Hecate, and then…the one god he'd always suspected hated him enough to break an oath on the River Styx just to prove that Percy couldn't tell him what to do.
Ares.
Then weapons had been drawn, and people were at each other's throats. He'd drawn Riptide fully expecting to have to defend himself.
Then a booming voice the volume of a rock concert and with enough bass to make his bones to a little tap-dance inside his skeleton had given the order: "DROP YOUR WEAPONS!"
And every single weapon in the ampitheater had clattered to the ground like a rebellious kid acting out only to play all pleasant once mommy and daddy were home. Riptide suddenly weighed several hundred pounds and sank into the stone of the floor in front of him.
Everyone suspected Chiron at first. The centaur had nodded no.
Percy was the next thought. He nodded no.

Then everyone realized at roughly the same time that Aidan's mouth was still open. He'd immediately closed it. Campers had wondered how he'd done that and what that even was.
Percy'd known, though, and really did not like the implications.
Children of Ares, the stronger ones more in-tune with their powers, could forcefuly disarm opponents in active combat. Weapons were their domain, after all. How this kid that had sucked at sparring until he very much *hadn't* had managed to forcefully disarm two hundred opponents at once? That he wasn't even trying to guess.
And as if Percy's life couldn't get any more strange, Rachel Dare had issued a prophecy on the spot:
"A child of war, born of the war god's forge,
Shall heed the call of the west, to the mountains' guard,
Where a god's oath bleeds into the earth's torn skin,
To defeat a rival all his own.
The forged shall stand where fates entwine,
Shall walk where gods and titans bled,
A god's wrath shall fall upon his son,
As the living meet the dead."

She'd collapsed into her friends, who had steadied her on her bench, and then gave Aidan this cheeky grin like they were sharing a joke.
The universe seemed to echo within his mind: Weirdness levels reaching maximum overdrive.
A giant holographic seven foot bronze spear appeared over the kid's head.
Claimed by Ares.
The kid had taken one look at the huge spear, realized who it was, turned the color of hardening cement, and fallen forward into the horde of unclaimed and Hermes children in below him like Rachel Dare's collapse had been taken as a challenge of who could be the most dramatic.

*Hey! Look! There seems to be a child of war right over here!*

Absolute carnage had broken out. Sheer, non-violent carnage as campers desperately tried to free their swords and spear from the stone of the ampitheater. Children of Apollo were forced to abandon their quivers on the ground as they suddenly weighed so much that children of Ares he'd seen lift engine blocks and carborators like sacks of potatoes tried and failed to get even a single arrow out of them. Punching and kicking.
Then the sky fell, pigs flew, and Hell froze over all at once.
Clarisse stood up, raised her spear, and shouted over everybody else:
"EVERYBODY! LISTEN UP! WE NEED TO RESOLVE THIS *PEACEFULLY*!"
