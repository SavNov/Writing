CLARISSE
**Clarisse eyed him.** Nervous. Concerned. Bitter at her own dumb luck.
“I saw what happened on the bus. Well… part of it. That monster. Did she—?”
He met her eyes.
“Not. One. Word.”

She blinked.
“I don’t even know whether that means you’re just that good…", she imagined this boy smooth-talking that thing into doing...whatever the two had done in that seat, "or if it’s honestly, utterly heartbreaking.” She scoffed. “Really?”

Then she saw the look in his eyes.
And that shut her up.

Clarisse had never been great at quitting while she was ahead. Her whole life, the fight *was* the point. Learn when to stop punching—that had always been her problem. The kid at the age of 8 that she'd pummeled literally black-and-blue at school, and had to spend a week in suspension for afterward. *Clarisse! Unacceptable! You need to learn how to control your anger!* *I am trying, mom! I swear! I just...kept going and going and lost track!*
The Daughter of Ares had never been able to swallow her pride.
Silena.
She would've died to that Drakon a thousand times over, no questions asked, nothing off the table, if it would bring Silena back. Even for a day.
Aidan wasn't the only person in the car with baggage in the trunk. He was just the only one strong enough to swallow his pride and show it.
She didn’t ask again.

Father—no—Lord Ares had given her a mission: *Find the kid. Get him to camp. You will know him when you see him.*
Where to be, when to show up, and nothing else. Classic Ares. She wasn’t even sure the kid knew who *he* was yet. She only knew his name because when a teenage girl shows up after a fire alarm just went off with a huge spear and asks for the name of a kid in the line, teachers listen. But she’d found him. Clothes battered, burned, and with monster spit in places she didn't feel ready to ask about. Parts of his slacks with holes like the fabric had been grabbed and ripped out. She didn't even know what that void was.
The kid was still alive. Depressed, tired, angry, but alive. She'd followed orders and gotten him back into her Camp Half-Blood issued black SUV.

She glanced over at him. Blue polo, slacks, stiff posture—he looked more like a debate club president...no, more like a college student who'd just left a calculus lecture...than a demigod who’d just chokeslammed a fire spirit through a bus window. For all she knew, he *had* just come from a calculus lecture. Pretending to be mortal to keep the monsters away.

He looked angry.
And sad.

That picture thing she'd seen on the cellphones of one of the mortals popped into her head uninvited—*I know what I must do, but I do not know if I have the strength to do it.* Some dumb picture the kids had laughed at.
He wasn't laughing.

He didn’t say a word the whole ride.
Clarisse thanked the gods for that. She wasn’t great at small talk. The Athena kids always wrecked her in arguments anyway. She preferred to conduct her conversations at spear point when necessary, and stick to frying campers with her own electric spear at the war games when not.

For the first time in her entire life, even including the dozens of stories she'd heard, the journey to Camp Half-Blood was smooth as butter. Just one drunk mortal blocking a tunnel, screaming that the tunnel was gonna blow up and everybody needed to back away.
She'd closed her eyes in traffic, sniffed a bit. *No explosives. Not even a gas main. He's crazy.* Then, she *gently* nudged the guy with the bumper. He tumbled to the ground in a heap, unharmed and snoring like all he'd needed was someone to tell him it was time to go night-night. Cars turned to avoid the sleeping drunkard. This was New York. Most locals had gotten their Master's in Parallel Parking.

Job done.
But that didn't do anything to ease the bit of nervousness in the pit of her stomach that she realized was getting worse. He was alive. At *16*. *16*, and he either had lost track of his quest, unlikely, or hadn't been claimed yet. Maybe he was the child of a minor god. That happened more often than most people thought. Minor gods had children, and they weren't in the loop about the latest from Mount Olympus, so they didn't realize they had to claim their children.
*Children of minor gods do not choke-slam flame spirits straight through plate-glass windows built to survive knifing, nor survive...void spirits? gravity spirits?* she genuinely didn't know what to call the thing, it was like nothing she'd ever seen before. *...he is something different.*
Chicken and the egg. Children of minor gods typically weren't spotted by monsters until later on. Their scent was too weak to attract attention. And so were they. Too weak to attract attention. Sometimes you met a kid who pretended to use the Force to open doors because their parent controlled doorways and passages. Childre powerful enough to throw flame spirits out of windows and survive black hole spirits point-blank did not survive to *Sophomore year* without help.
She hadn't seen anything like it, even in drawings, and there were drawings of Curses for crying out loud. No demigod would ever meet *those* unless they were somehow dense enough to end up in Tartarus **alive**.

She didn’t know if he fell asleep or just stared out the window the whole ride.
But when Camp came into view, she saw the way his shoulders relaxed, just barely.

Like someone who didn’t believe in safe places, but still needed one anyway.

They passed the pine tree. The boundary line shimmered. He didn’t even flinch.
She parked.

Not one word passed between them.

Clarisse didn't know what she'd thought he'd say, but it wasn't nothing. This was Camp Half-Blood. If he really was as powerful as she thought, then he would be right at home, finally safe. He'd start training. He'd meet friends. He was 16 now, he'd...get claimed...
...oh gods...
If a major Olympian god had broken an oath on the River Styx, had failed to claim their child for three years after the mandated deadline, she didn't know what she would do. Fortuna, Nemesis maybe. Something managable. Something that would explain it. Fortuna, maybe. Maybe he was incredibly lucky.
His face said no.
Nemesis. Goddess of revenge and pay-back.
He looked exhausted from giving revenge and pay-back, not rejuvenated.

Campers would welcome him. He'd go through orientation. Chiron would ask questions. They'd find out what he was good at, and why. And when the ampitheater filled with campers and everyone looked to that familiar group of unclaimed children who's 13th birthdays were only a couple of weeks ago so it was fair enough that their parents were a bit busy, the kid would get claimed. By a lesser god. Hopefully.
Maybe.
Her job was over.

But even as he walked toward the Big House, back still straight, blood still drying on his collar, Clarisse sat in the driver’s seat a little longer, trying to convince herself what she’d just seen wasn’t what she thought it was.

Because if it was…
If she was right...

Then things were about to get a whole lot worse before they got better.
