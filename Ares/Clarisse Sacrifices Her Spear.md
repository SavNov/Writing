Getting to the fortress wasn't hard, it was a giant structure in the side of Mount Diablo, easy to find, and guarded by dozens of demigods in armor that weren't expecting their opponent to be 160 pounds and 70% of it muscle.
The hard part was getting the audience with Samson to begin with.
She adjusted Silena's—no, not Silena's—*her* armor for the fight ahead.

The fortress had two entrances. One facing South, the other North. She didn't need to be a daughter of Hades to understand that the fortress went straight through the bottom of Mount Diablo from one side to the other.
She desperately tried to silence her nerves. She told herself this was just like the training she'd done so many times before. She was just training, yeah, that was it. She was training, and getting lightning into her spear was the objective.
She picked up her spear. It's electricity buzzed in her hands. But she knew it wasn't the voltage she needed. She raised it high in the air, and waited.
Two hundred feet east, a lightning bolt from the storm above arced against a copper deposit.
*This is stupid. This is never going to work. You can't expect lightning to do what you want it to do.*
She searched the deposits, looking for a group that was close enough together that lightning could arc between them.
"I'll have to trick it, then." She said, processing how utterly stupid that sounded, treating lightning like it was a person. Something that could think. Something that could be tricked.
Her spear arced with electricity in protest.
*It's a magic spear. That's different.*
As she stepped forward, the clinking in her armor reminded her again of Silena. She pictured seeing Silena topple to the ground, nursing that final strike from the Drakon as she'd died.
*No, stay focused. This won't take long.*
*Clink, clink, clink, clink, clink...*
Then she saw it. A huge grouping of brown and silver and red rocks she knew had to be next to an old mine. It took her eight full minutes to get to it.
This is where the lightning had struck, and she saw the scar it had left before. The deposits weren't closer together, but they were bigger, able to catch and redistributes lightning much more efficiently, getting much better arcs.

She flipped the spear between her fingers.
Aidan's words came to mind, another thing she'd found stupid at the time but now found oddly insightful.
*So if Zeus's father Cronus killed his father Ouranous with his scythe, and got cursed to be defeated by his own offspring...and if Zeus killed his father Cronus with that same scythe...and if Samson really is planning on building his own Mount Olympus on Mount Diablo...shouldn't Zeus be absolutely terrified of being overthrown?*
No, the idea was absurd.
Zeus wasn't scared of anything. He was an arrogant, prideful king of the gods that frequently ruined the lives of others in order to look good, or out of plain cruelty.
She eyed the lightning in the sky, how the storm seemed angry...yet despite the tall mountain of Mount Diablo, and Mount Othrys in the near distance, the lightning failed to hit either. When a bolt of lightning did attempt to strike, as she heard the thunder boom in her ears, she watched the lightning get...diverted? away from the mountain like it wasn't allowed to hit it.
She tried the dumbest idea she'd ever considered, because she was in desperate need of incredibly stupid ideas that would work. A lightning bolt to charge her spear. The electric spear was flashy, and it was cool, but it wasn't fatal. Sure, it may have burned a few lower-level monsters, even a drakon after she'd wounded it in her anger, but a person? A son of Ares she wasn't even fated to be able to defeat? No, that wasn't going to work. She needed lightning, real lightning. Not some electric spear. Something from the sky.
Since calling Hades his Lordly variant had seemed to work so well, treating him with respect, she tried it again.
"Lord Zeus, I..." She said.
*This is so stupid. Zeus doesn't care about mortals. This is never going to work. He's probably laughing at me just for trying.*
"I...um..."
*And yet try I will, because I don't really have a choice.*
"...I need a favor."
She traced the inside of her palm with her thumb, spear in her right hand buzzing with electricity threatening to escape.
"Stay." She ordered the electricity.
The electricity arced against the tip of the spear and back again, but didn't leave the tip.
"Good spear." She muttered, unable to believe the words that had just left her mouth.

She dragged the spear against the ground, letting the static electricity build up because she knew every single volt would count.


*For the only time in history, then or since, a daughter of Ares had summoned lightning from Mount Olympus. And gotten it.*

"I...um...don't know what to..." She whispered.
She looked at her right hand. The spear was so hot that it had seared, then melted, the edges of her skin. She'd thought the tiny burst of pain had been a little bit of electricity singing her arm. Instead, the handle of spear had just fused with the flesh of her right hand.
"Well...I'm committed now." She said. It didn't hurt. None of it did, even as the burning heat seared her flesh. She had completely destroyed the nerves in her hand. She couldn't move her fingers on that hand.
It freaked her out for a moment, but she realized it didn't matter. It wasn't like she was planning to write poetry or something with that hand anyway.
Her foot hit something. She looked down. Right next to Silena—no, her—boot, the lightning bolt had revealed a small backpack. Tough, leather, strong. She pried it open by using the tip of her spear and her other hand, revealing war rations, photos, and letters. This had probably been buried here a long time ago, only to have a lightning bolt literally disintegrate the dirt guarding it. She picked one of the letters up.
The letter between two lovers, written decades ago by the fact that the little letter was signed June 6, 1944. She recognized the date like any good child of the war god would.
*D-Day.*
The letter was long and poetic and extremely fluffy to her, but she wasn't dissing that. Not anymore. All her mind could focus on, though, was that final line.
*We are in this together.*
She looked up at the sky. It was total coincidence. The story of this couple had happened while her own grandmother had been in her teen years. That lightning bolt had been one of several dozen to fall in the past hour, one of several to fall in the past ten minutes. There was no way of knowing how big of them actually were when you were hundreds of feet away, or if you'd heard the boom of a different lightning bolt as yours had come down.
Luck. Chance. Something something monkeys + Shakespeare is what the smart Athena kids would gush about. She didn't care about being smart right now.
All she cared about was her plan working. And by the looks of it.

Zeus did too.
