**My first day at Camp Half-Blood made me wish that girl had kissed me into oblivion.**
I swear, I did not intend to acquaint myself with an eldritch horror.

The giant blue building with the picture of various Greek gods on the front seemed so blatantly obvious that there was no point even looking at anything else. Not that I wanted to.
*Shick- clang! Bang!*
Knives straight to the eardrum. Real weapons. Hitting each other. With speed and force. I tensed up and winced.
*Find the centaur.* The voice said.
*A minute.* I retorted, still trying to relax my fully-tensed muscles.
I found the centaur drinking a glass of sweet tea on the front porch of the big house as Clarisse trailed behind me.
"Hi." I waved.
The centaur waved back, smiled with pure warmth.
"Hello, young one. I am Chiron, centaur and teacher of demigods. Clarisse has told me very little about you, though I suspect that is because she knows very little about you herself." Chiron said, stretching out his hand and gesturing to the seat next to him.
*Cool. A chair.* I thought.
*He is asking you to sit.* The voice said, very informatively.
*Oh.* I realized.
I sat.
"So please, young one, what's your name?" Chiron, centaur and teacher of demigods, said.
"Aidan Travis." I answered. The girl's, or would it the creature's? saliva still dripped down my cheek.
A beat passed. Chiron realized I wasn't about to say anything. He adjusted.
*This is going well.* The voice said, deep and instructive.
"Tell me, what is your story, Aidan Travis? What have you seen?" He asked, sounding incredibly curious.
My mind rolled back through three years and two dozen fights with monsters.
"Where should I start?" I asked.
Chiron sounded mildly confused, but humored him.
"Well, I suspect already you have a long story, Aidan Travis. Why don't you start at this morning and we'll work our way backwards?" Chiron said.
He was trying to meet me in the middle. I respected that.
"I got out of bed and mentally prepped for my field trip to New York Public Library way up…" I said, then remembered I was in New York now. "…here."
Chiron gestured for me to continue.
*Tell him everything, with the exception of what should be obvious.* The voice said.
*Yes, I don't feel like being institutionalized today.* I thought.
I told him how my mom had made me breakfast before heading to work to teach her college class, how I'd read a good book on Calvinism-
"Calvinism?" Chiron asked me, very surpised.
"Yes." I said. "It's all about finding out how much choice I really have in this world."
Chiron muttered something under his breath about how the average 13 year old wasn't thinking about these kinds of questions, but allowed me to continue.
I made the strategic decision not to correct him about my age until I'd at least finished answering his question.
-and strategized on how to deal with Jamie.
"Jamie?" He asked.
Clarisse finally chimed in. I hadn't sensed her coming. That didn't feel right.
"Are you talking about the void thing?" She asked me. I nodded.
"She called herself Jamie." I said.
Even Chiron seemed confused.
"Void…void thing? Please, elaborate." He said.
I told him what I knew.
She could only reveal her true form when no mortals were around to see her. Once she did so, she was nearly impossible to escape. When she had pinned me down, it had been like standing next to the event horizon of a black hole.
"A black hole?" Chiron asked.
Clarisse seemed to picture something utterly eldritch, then nod her head no.
"Not what you're thinking. This thing could fit in a bus seat." She said.
Chiron rubbed his nose.
As I thought back, I felt it. Remembering her true form in my mind's eye, I could feel my mind being pulled toward it. Drawn forward. I felt the edges blur.
I grabbed a pencil and paper out of my shirt pocket—a mind like mine needed it—and closed my eyes.
*Eye witnesses lose up to 50% of the visual acuity of their memory by attempting to translate it into words.* I remembered.
*Clever.* The voice commented, sounding mildly impressed.
It was awfully easy to draw with just a pencil when your subject was monochrome. I sketched the black void woman as quickly as I could, taking it in layers because I didn't know how long I would remember her for and a full but inaccurate picture was better than a partial and accurate one when you were trying to spot the thing in real life. After three layers, I remembered to write 'Jamie' and 'from bus' at the bottom in case I lost those, too. I ended it by writing 'It steals memory'. It took about five minutes.

After the last layer I could manage, I stared at it.

“What's that?” Clarisse asked.

I shrugged.

“I… don't know. Seemed so important before." I said.

I read the words at the bottom.

*Jamie. From bus. It steals memory.*
Jamie? The monster that had flirted with me for the past two weeks? I didn't remember her being on the bus.

*Wait.*

*Draw for comparison.* The voice suggested.

It was… a dumb idea, but I did it anyway. I pictured the girl in my mind, and drew her figure in layers like I'd learned to do. I was no artist, but it looked sort of human. I didn't have colors, only a pencil, so instead I wrote ‘purple eyes, green shirt, blonde hair, khaki pants’ in the bottom margin.

Chiron photographed the picture of the void girl with an urgency that confused me, and ran inside the Big House on his hooves.

When he returned, he looked… unnerved.

“I have copied the image to my computer in hopes of preserving it." He said, his voice slightly shaky.

Clarisse looking thoroughly rattled, not by the girl, but by the way Chiron was acting. Chiron was rattled, too. He eyed me with absolute seriousness.

“You will tell me every single thing this Jamie did in the past two weeks. Now." Chiron demanded.

I read off the mental list. She'd been hunting me, gauging my powerset, but I'd always been too quick, too clever, especially with the voice’s help, though I didn't mention the voice to Chiron. I detailed the three dozen encounters I'd had with her.
She was flirty, smooth, and something else, too: Forgettable. Teachers never asked her for homework, and when 'Jamie' appeared on the roll call, they gave this look of deep donfusion as if some kind of clerical error had just occurred.

I stared at the void creature I didn't remember drawing.

"Chiron…what is going on?" I asked. "You seem…'spooked'."
Spooked did not sound right coming out of my mouth, but the right word wouldn't come to me.
He stroked his beard. "I am…unsure. The only creature I can imagine causing memory issues would be something from…"
"You aren't serious." Clarisse interrupted. "That is crazy. How would something like that even get to the mortal plane in the first place? Hades would lose his—"
She paused for one terrible moment.
"Mind." I finished, not realizing that was not the kind of sentence you wanted to finish.
Chiron bolted into the Big House again. "I am contacting the Hunters of Artemis immediately. Clarisse, give him Orientation. If anyone asks where I've gone—"
"Olympian business. Got it." Clarisse said, though I could tell this was not the kind of girl who took orders easily.

She smiled at me nervously.
"Welcome to Camp Half-Blood." She said, like she was trying to hold it together. "Follow me. I'll give you a tour of Camp. And whatever you do…"
"Do not discuss the eldritch abomination. Understood." I confirmed.
She nodded.
"Good. You learn fast. Let's figure out what you're good at. Hopefully, your orientation will go more smoothly than your conversation with Chiron."
