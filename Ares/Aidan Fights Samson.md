AIDAN
The huge mountains seemed to stretch endlessly on either side. The fortress was…not fully guarded. That should've been the first indication that something was already wrong about this.
I stepped forward, brandishing the sword I'd been given. Small, light, something I could reasonably use without much training.
Three guards stood at the entrance.
*They are shaken. Something has happened. Something that has rattled their confidence.* Ares said.
One of them had a strap undone in their armor as I approached.
The one on the left, strap fully attached, grinned at me weakly.
"What are you here for?" He asked, mockery clear in his tone.
"I am here for a meeting with Samson." I said.
All three cracked up laughing.
"What? You? You must be joking!"
"Tell him I'm the Child of War he's been expecting." I said, my voice naturally calm, flat and even, unwavering.
They looked at each other.
"Okay, sure, if you are really this Child of War from the prophecy, prove it then! Get past us! Don't worry, we'll go—" The man on the left said, but before he could finish his sentence, I swung my sword.
*Left. Before he gets his guard up. Hit the chestplate, knock him off his balance.* I thought.
He leaned to the left a few inches, then stabilized himself.
"Well played, kid. Now, let's actually—" He tried again.
What followed was a chain of commands from Ares, and I followed from memory. Thirty monsters defeated had taught me how to perform each move individually, but I'd never chained them together before.
*Left. Uppercut. Strike down, 45 degrees. Dodge left. Sweep with feet clockwise. 90 degree turn right, duck to dodge sword swing, strike opponent wrist, disarm. Dodge right, allow opponent blades to jam together. Strike upward to break lock and destabilize blades, kick upward to break weakened hold. 30 degree turn left, parry incoming blow to abdomen with side swipe, kick downwards, lunge up, strike helmet with force.*
I looked at the three men. Two now laid on the ground, disarmed, and the third was nursing a nasty concussion.
*Objective achieved.* Ares said, sounding proud.
*We are not out of the woods yet.* I thought back.

The second one tried to stand up. I placed my sword point to his throat.
"Stay down." I warned. My voice flat, which to the uninitiated, sounded absolutely ruthless.
…
She was not important right now. He looked to his left, then his right. A dozen highly experienced medics and plenty of ambrosia. As long those medics weren't stoned out of their minds, they'd be able to patch a wound, and use ambrosia to heal the rest long before she succumbed to it.
*Swing right. Parry. Disarm. Knife three inches below right breast. Puncture lung.*
"You can't just barge in here like—" She shouted, eyes full of fury as she swung her knife.
He executed the instructions in a good-enough sequence.
Within seconds, she was wincing in pain and toppling to the ground, clutching the knife in her chest.
"Tell the medics you have a puncture in your left lung, and breathe out even if it hurts." He instructed, voice calm and flat.
She looked equally confused, angry, and compliant. Sure enough, as he walked by in search of Samson, that was exactly what she told the medics and they began treating the wound effectively. Within ten minutes, it would be little more than a bleeding cut.
"Samson! Identify yourself!" He shouted.
The voice echoed across the cell block.

Samson was smiling wide. He appeared to be enjoying himself. He was cracking his knuckles, armor gleaming under the torchlight.
"Now this…" He said, strutting over from his little corner of the cell block, eyes shining with pride. "…this will be a treat."
I should have leaned into the bravado.
*No. I do not have time for this.* I thought.
*Perhaps there is hope for him yet.* Another part of me thought.
I could feel it, two sides of me at war. One side that saw Samson as a target to be gotten over with so I could save my mom. Another that…
"How did you pick your name?" I asked flatly, deliberately moving my lips into a kind smile.

I could practically see his brain short-circuiting in real time.

"I… what?" He asked, genuinely caught off-guard.
*Not the most orthodox play,* Ares said, *but if the shoe fits.*

"Your name. How did you pick it?" I asked, deliberately shifting my voice into something neutral, disarming, peaceful.

He looked at the various propaganda posters. Himself from a dozen angles, flexing muscles, landing three-pointers effortlessly, holding Zeus' Master Bolt high.
"Samson. He's the strong one. He's tough, and brutal, and powerful. Just like a son of Ares should be." He said, like he assumed this should be obvious.
"That is what I thought too, when I encountered the story in Sunday school. Strong, confident, brave. I never liked that story." I said, keeping my tone careful, measured.
Samson smirked.
"Of course you wouldn't. You might a son of Ares, but you're not THE son of Ares!" Samson boasted.
"I know." I said simply.

Samson was all volume and bravado and strength. But I was not made of the same thing. I was erosion, silence, and patience. And I allowed those forces to work. The human mind, at least one like his, did not like silence. People had mountains of things to say to avoid the silence, the awkwardness, the feeling that you really should be saying something. Silence was uncomfortable.
I allowed it to bore into him as I stood across the cell block from him.
"…children of Ares are supposed to be strong, powerful, not afraid of anything. Don't you have any pride? Any sense of boasting?" He asked, visibly on-edge, but bracing himself now.
I did not answer.

"You're the one the gods sent to defeat me! You're supposed to be stronger, faster, smarter! We're supposed to fight!" He insisted. He pounded his sword against the ground to emphasize the point.
Receiving silence as a response to something you said, for several thousand years, had been the social signal that you had said something wrong or distasteful.
I said nothing still. The torches glowed in the pin drop silence.
"Say something, curse you! You're supposed to be like me! I mean… I guess if you prove yourself in combat, we can work together, fight against the gods, build our own Mount Olympus right here on Diablo!" He announced.
I felt like I was watching a stubborn child slowly realize that nobody cared about a word that was coming out of his mouth.
I waited twenty seconds. Finally, right when I determined that he was doubting himself, I struck.
"Who are you, Samson?" I asked.
"Really? Look at one of these posters! I'm Samson! Son of Ares! The strongest, fastest, bravest of my kind!" He proclaimed.
I allowed the silence to bore into him just as Hades had done with me. Maybe I could not save him. Maybe this was a fool's errand.
But my own curiosity would not permit me not to even try to.

His lip trembled, just slightly.
"Okay, maybe that's not the perfect description."
