"You have no clue what just happened, do you?" Clarisse said, trying to stop herself from laughing again as she stared at me curiously.
"No, not really." I admitted, shrugging.
"That speech. You don't know what that is, do you?" She asked.
She fought back tears of laughter.
"No, what is it?" I asked.
"It's the Boyfriend Speech." She said.
I gave her my best puzzled look.
"Huh?" I asked.
"Take care of her, give her what she deserves, make her happy, say whatever you need to say, it's the speech dads give to their daughter's new boyfriend." She explained, snorting with laughter again. She tried once, twice, three times to stop laughing.
My mind refused to compute this arcane witchcraft.
The Boyfriend Speech. I took out my notebook and wrote it down next to a definition to review later.
"But I'm not-" I started. She nodded.
"You aren't." She confirmed.
"Does he think-" I tried again.
"Maybe, yes." She admitted.
"But you already have-" I tried a third time to get a full sentence out.
She gave THE snort. The snort you get when someone snorts with both their nose and mouth at the same time and it ends up sounding like...
...a pig snort. She blushed slightly.
"Yes, I do. Chris Rodriguez." She answered shakily. Campers finally gathered the guts to head back into the camp after Ares had told them to scram.
Maybe my father had absorbed just a little bit of my nature in the process of spending three years in my head. That was fine with me.
The world could use a little bit more Autism.
"So...you two *aren't* dating?" Sherman Yang asked us both. Clarisse rose to her full height in her wheelchair.
"Um, no." I said.
"I have a boyfriend already, you dolt." She grunted.
Immediately, 500 dollars worth of money changed hands across four dozen campers.
"You were...you were betting...why I am not surprised by this?" She muttered.
The Aphrodite kids were a dozen of the four dozen campers. The worst offenders, to nobody's surprise.
"They gamble on love?" I asked.
She sighed, head in her weakened hands.
"It's a tradition that will continue long after both of us are dead. Permanently, I mean." She said, having to add permanently to that sentence with reluctance since she'd already died once and technically I'd already been to the Underworld.
Then I looked behind me.
Chiron was holding a twenty dollar bill in his fingers.
"Et tu, Brutus?" I asked.
An Athena camper groaned and handed him a second twenty dollar bill.
"Wait, why?" I arched an eyebrow.
"I made two bets. One, that you weren't dating Clarisse." Chiron said, relaxing in his wheelchair.
"And two?" I asked.
The son of Athena muttered that betting had been a stupid idea as he walked off.
"That the moment you found out, you would say, quote 'Et tu, Brutus', quoting Shakespeare." Chiron smiled. "Obviously, I won on both counts."
"Surely that can't be ethical for the Camp Activities Director to be gambling with the kids." I said, deliberately arching my eyebrow again.
Chiron smiled.
"Maybe, but I've been doing it in bits and pieces since the American Civil War and Olympus has yet to bring it up, so I'll stop when they tell me to stop." Chiron said.
This Camp was weird.
