If he'd been worried about the noise in the library they were headed to, the bus ride was thirty kids talking, and ten kids screaming. 

Behind him, three kids argued about some kind of board game he'd tried to get into but just couldn't. Next to him, a girl with long, blonde hair and purple eyes was smiling at him. She'd been flirting with him for about a week now. That's when he'd known.

She smiled too narrow, worried she might open her mouth too wide. Too clean. Too efficent when smiles weren't meant to be. The Voice hadn't managed to beat him to it. *Another one.* He'd thought.

The voice that had guided him since the age of 13 had confirmed it. *Do not be afraid yet, her kind cannot reveal themselves in the presence of mortals.*

Even if she was sitting less than six inches to his left, she was harmless. At least, that's what he hoped. 

"Hey there, cutie." The girl with the purple contact lenses said, though they weren't contact lenses. Monsters didn't need lenses. Their eyes were whatever the viewer needed them to be. That was what the voice had said. 

He looked back at her. He got no response from the voice, so he answered. "Hi, Jamie." He smiled. Pretended to be normal. She couldn't counter the human civilities without revealing herself.
That, and he had learned that flirting back always made things worse. At least, his brand of it seemed to.
He heard it. A knife being unsheathed.
*Not a threat.* The voice instructed, deep and commanding.
His nerves calmed down without even knowing the source. He trusted the voice's judgement. Implicitly. If the voice said it wasn't a threat, then it wasn't. End of story.
...
*Locate.* The voice said. He remembered the knife being unsheathed. Given the proximity, he looked at Jamie first, checking her arms to see if they were reaching for a weapon. They weren't. He searched the bus front to back, letting his vision blur. It was an unreasonably efficient way of identifying movement for an initial screen so you could focus on looking for colors of interest only where it was important. A vitally important shortcut when your brain didn't process visuals at the same acuity it handled sound. Three seats behind, a boy flicked a knife in his palms. Up and down. Back and forth.
*Threat?* He thought.
*Not yet. He will clear the bus eventually.* The voice answered swiftly.
Clear the bus. The girl next to him.
*He's in league. Monster?*
*Naturally.* The voice answered.
...
*She is trying to restrict my movement.*
He leaned on Jamie's shoulder in return, reciprocating like he'd learned the past few weeks. After the female monster a year ago that would've melted him alive with a kiss, he was not making the mistake of escalating again.
The bus slowed to a stop.
...
*Out! Now!*
He was three rows down the aisle when the boy yanked the fire alarm, forcing everyone to clear the bus. The alarm disoriented him and forced him to plug his ears. Then he realized...he was trapped.
*Get out through the window, or you're dead.* The voice ordered.
*I know, I know, I'm working on that.* He thought back. He searched for an exit, but he could barely hear himself think, much less find an exit in a moving group of people in a confined space. Not to mention, each buzz of the alarm felt like two searing irons pressed against his ear drums. He winced at each one, tensing up.
The girl had gotten into the aisle before he did. And now she was getting handsy, which was worse. She hadn't been this gutsy or brazen before. She was probably confident that she was about to win.
*Maybe it wouldn't be so bad to just-*
*We are not having this conversation for the third time.* The voice said, like it was a fact of the universe like gravity and fire.
...
"Don't worry, hun, I've got you." She said, wrapping her arms around him protectively as the line moved forward. She shoved him to the side and into the seat, swaying him back and forth. People moved around them. His heart raced. She was pinning him to the seat.
He told himself that his heart was racing with the adrenaline of knowing he was moments away from death, and not because a cute monster was fulfilling a fantasy he'd had since he was 10 years old.
...
Sister.
He realized what was really wrapped around him wasn't a girl at all. Her mouth opened. A black void where her throat should've been.
Adrenaline poured into his veins.
...
The teachers had probably taken one look at the scene of the cute girl sitting next to him on the bus, flirting with him, then pinning him into the seat and figured it was teens being teens. And a guy standing there glaring at them, swiss army knife in hands? Probably an angry brother.
It wouldn't be enough. But now he was cornered. He imagined those lockers again. Those boys nursing broken ribs. It was going to happen again. The last boy left the bus, and he found the girl warping into pure void. There war no escape. He could feel his body being pulled as if he were standing on the surface of a neutron star. The flames of the brother burned into his skin as he became lost between them. One burning him alive, the other crushing him to death against its terrifying pull. He imagined this was what it was like to stand next to the event horizon of a black hole. The borderline-infinite gravity pulling you forward as the heat from the fusion-burning corpses of stars cooked you from the sides and behind. Each one pulling and twisting in a different way, but both towards the same goal: His destruction.
*You are going to pay for this.* The voice said. It wasn't vengeance. It was the same way you picked up the heavy weight at the gym knowing you were going to feel horrible tomorrow. His gym trainer was warning him the weight was too heavy for him. But it was the only way to get out of this situation.
And suddenly he wasn't burning anymore. He pushed up, grabbed the boy by the throat, and squeezed until the flame was forced to yield. The void tried to eat at his skin, but the gravity felt almost soothing. It steadied him. He threw the boy forward. The window broke. The boy dropped to the ground nursing a properly broken back.
...
He should’ve said something. Fought back. But his brain was busy having a crisis about whether this counted as dying or second base. He would've opened his mouth, but he was afraid she'd try to seal the deal by kissing him to shut him up. Which, to his own deep and profound shame, was exactly what he wanted her to do.
*Don't even think about it.* He didn't know if that was him thinking or the voice.
