Rey closed her eyes, and in one final bid to track this untraceable Jedi-who-might-not-be-a-Jedi-anymore, looked through the Force.

As she willed the shadows to vanish, she found the avian Fosh woman, standing before her, back turned as the woman looked down at an active game of Craps in play.

Rey did not recognize the place, but it looked to be a drinking hole/bar/gambling den. Her eyes scanned the room, looking at all the details to learn as much about this place, and her target, as possible.

It was definitely a gambling den. Men, women and androids of more species and models than she had ever seen in one place before. On musky wooden tables old enough to have served in the Clone Wars, games of Craps, Poker and Blackjack were played for petty credits.

The one called Vergere watched the table in a way that told her that she was not staring at the table, but staring past it. Hiking boots, brown raggedy slacks, and a camisole with so many holes you'd think she was trying to draw attention to yourself. But Rey knew that wasn't why her target was wearing it. Because she was a hunter, and she'd worn the same outfit before. In a place like this, it served as perfect cover.

Then through the reflection of the glass, Rey realized her target was smiling.

"One would think that having a way to see into the future would make you wealthy, powerful, wise, but that isn't how it works. The Force takes as much as it gives, especially when you apply it the same way we do."

*We.* Rey thought.

Rey searched for who the woman must be talking to, but found no one. Then she realized Vergere was talking to *her*.

The hairs on the back of Rey's neck stood up. This had never happened before. Ever. She didn't dare speak. Maybe she was wrong. Maybe she didn't quite understand this situation as well as she thought she did.

"Yes, I can sense you, Padawan. You are not subtle, especially to someone like me."

*Breathe in, breathe out. In on the up breath-* Rey thought, trying to steady her racing mind, only to be jolted out of her own mantra by the impossible.

"Adopted a mantra, have you, Padawan? I imagine something like 'In on the up breath, out on the down breath.' give or take a few words?"

Rey stopped breathing, not daring to inhale.

Not even the mantra that had kept her alive for seven years could save her from whoever she was hunting now. So she gave up on subtlety.

"How are you doing this?" Rey blurted.

"The better question, Padawan, would be 'How are you even alive?' or has little Organa gone searching through the records again without reading the whole tale?"

Vergere didn't move from the Craps table, simply spoke without her lips even moving. She didn't need to move. The power she had over Rey right now was greater than any combination of lightsaber training could hope to achieve.

Rey said nothing.

"Or maybe you neglected to ask that question because you already know the answer. I suspect the old man has been up to quite a lot in the thirty years he's been dead. His Master invented the concept of defeating death. And the scattered nature of my mind tells me that his solution was not yet perfect."

"You see me as a lesser you." Rey said through gritted teeth, both afraid… and angry.

The Fosh wagged her finger as she placed a bet. Five hundred credits.

"No, Padawan Rey, I have already met you. In another world, you could've been my daughter. But in order for me, the one person you have something to learn from still, and the same mentality, to take you on as my apprentice, you will have to prove we have something in common. Prove yourself as a worthy Padawan. Rule of One."
