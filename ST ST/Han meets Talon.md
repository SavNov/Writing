Ben Solo's father had taught him one very valuable lesson: Keep the girl talking and nobody explodes.

"Who are you?" He asked.

She eyed him, body thin and lithe as she calmly ignited the red blade, covered in tatoos from head to toe and proud of that.

"To you, Solo, I am death." She said, swooping down from her place at the top of the railing like a trained trapeez artist…

…and nearly killing him on the spot as her lightsaber missed his throat by inches, him dodging her just in time.

*WWHD? What Would Han Do? Hando? Hondo? Why am I thinking about a weapons smuggler right now?*

"But you may call me Talon for as long as you remain alive." She said as he tried to get his bearings. Supply closets, a few blasters he'd be long dead before she let him grab, some boxes.

*In lack of a plan, keep talking.*

"Are you an angel?" Ben asked.

Talon actually paused mid-strike because she was so shocked by the question, both of them now standing on a stairwell.

"What?"

"They're the most beautiful creatures in the universe. They live on the Moons of Iego, I think. They are good and kind, and so pretty they make even the most hardened spice pirate cry…" Ben finished.

He had no idea if any of that was actually true, but he didn't care.

*Please work please work please work.*

Talon placed the blade of her lightsaber against his throat.

"I've never heard of any angels." She growled.

He leapt back onto a railing, walking along the edge towards the stack of crates.

"You must be one!" He said, as she sliced the pipe he'd been using for cover clean into two. "You just don't know it yet!"

And then…

…Talon laughed.

A good long, hard chuckle that moved outwards from her chest through her neck, her arms and then her legs before finally echoing back into her core.

"You're a funny little boy, Ben Solo." She said.

They both eyed the huge lava pit down below.

"How do you know so much?"

Ben bluffed.

"I listen to all the traders and starpilots that come through here," He said, gesturing to the hangar a few hundred meters off the horizon, "I'm a pilot, you know. And someday… "

He gestured upward, hoping this last part wasn't mere bluster.

"I'm gonna fly away from this place."

Talon's eye twitched. She wasn't fighting him, not anymore, instead she seemed to be planning ahead.

"You're a pilot?" She prodded.

Ben simply pointed at the place a few kilometers away where his Falcon had been stranded.

"Mm-hmmm. All my life."

Talon looked around, saw the time, realized he'd been stalling, and cursed.

"How long have you been here?" She shouted, back to business.

Rather than give her a straight answer, he decided to be a bit evasive, and assume she was talking about how long he'd been pulling stunts like this.

"Since I was very little." He said, attempting to count on his fingers. "Three, I think. My Mom led the Rebellion, and my Dad teaches piloting now."

She rolled her eyes.

"You're a slave?" She muttered, like she couldn't believe someone like him could settle for so little.

If Ben had been a bit more observant, he may have noticed that the idea of being a slave seemed to seriously get to her.

"I am a person!" He insisted, not about to be boxed into anything. "Ben Solo. Son of Han Solo!"

For a moment, he could've sworn he saw a tiny hint of vulnerability on the Twi'lek's face. Then it vanishes, and she gave a sarcastic retort.

"I'm sorry. I don't fully understand." She spat. She looked around at the various containers and droid around, then at the bird that had made the Kessel Run in 12 parsecs. "This is a strange world to me."

Ben's smiled paused for just a brief instant. "You are a strange girl to me."

Then he thought of the perfect reply to throw her off her balance.

"I'm going to marry you somebody." He teased. She laughed.

She sputtered, and he didn't know whether it was the fires of Mustafar casting red shadows on her skin, or if this girl was actually blushing.

"Yeah, right," she shook her head to clear the emotion from her face, "and I'm Padme."

She jumped and bounded, him again avoiding being reduced to a bloodstain by inches.

*Han Solo would talk his way out of it.*

"Oh, you are, are you?" She said, shrugging.

"Here," He said, eyeing the huge S-11 security unit at the edge of the room, "let me show you my droid."

"Alright," she smirked, intrigued as to what this random man could do with it when it was locked and only answered to her, "show me the droid."

…

It was only after she Force-dragged him off the railing and sent him hurtling fifteen stories straight down, only to be saved by catching a rope he'd noticed and swinging onto a nearly catwalk, that maybe, perhaps, just this once, he might need an actual plan.
