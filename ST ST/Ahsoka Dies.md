Ahsoka Tano smiled, the older woman holding Rey's chin with more care than the hunter had felt in years.

…

Ahsoka's finger traced the photo of a younger Anakin Skywalker, then the saber she had left in the dirt of a frozen planet nearly six decades before. Her eyes drifted to another holotape, not of Sith, but the Imperial Archive record of Palpatine's defeat. Vader throwing the Sith Lord down the tube and saving his son.

"I fought beside Anakin in the Clone Wars. I watched my Master go from a soldier to a general. I saw all the signs without knowing them for what they were. I did know what they were, though."

She closed her eyes, seeing the memory fondly.

"They were Anakin."

The padawan learner turned Jedi turned teacher of Jedi met Rey's eyes.

"I knew Anakin. I grew up just like you did. Targets, missions, battles, war. I was pulled out of slavery before I even knew what freedom meant. I was a soldier before I even understood what it meant to be a kid."

Rey had never heard this story before. She'd suspected that Ahsoka shared some experiences with her, having served in the Clone Wars, but never anything like this.

"You… you did?" She asked.

A seventy-something year old Ahsoka Tano smiled at her. She fought back a cough for a moment in the old chair.

"You aren't the first Jedi to grow up in a war, and you won't be the last, Rey."

Then Ahsoka drew two lightsabers from the table next to her. She felt the weight of each one fondly. Her arms crossed together, and she made that familiar stance from all those decades ago.

"Master—" Rey said, only for the woman to cut her off.

"Just Ahsoka. This isn't the Jedi Council, and you aren't on trial."

Rey stiffened for just a fraction of a second. She had assumed that after Luke had gotten so angry with her, been so insistent that in no world could she stray from the Jedi, that Ahsoka would be the same way. Yet the woman, the teacher, was saying she wasn't on trial?

"But… I'm turning my back on the Jedi way. I'm about to train as a Sith, learn the Dark Side of the Force. Surely you find something wrong with that." Rey insisted.

Ahsoka laughed.

"Rey, you aren't the first Jedi to leave the Order. You aren't the first person to find that the Jedi Order… isn't where you need to be. And you won't be the last."

She reached into a drawer and pulled out what looked like a hairpin, but Rey realized it was a Padawan braid. Ahsoka offered it to her, Rey took it gratefully.

"Is this really—?" Rey asked, stunned into silence.

Ahsoka nodded.

"You told me that the Order took this away from you, that you turned this down when Anakin offered it to you again."

Ahsoka nodded again.

"So how do you still—"

"—have it?" Ahsoka finished her question for her.

Rey nodded.

Ahsoka brushed the braid like it was an old friend.

"Anakin never forgot about me. Not after 25 years, not after losing Padme, not after everything he did. After Anakin died, they went through his things, and they found it. So they gave it to Luke to hold onto. And for 25 years, after losing his father, after parting with his sister, after everything he did, Luke didn't forget either. So he gave it to me. And after everything I've done, I still haven't forgotten."

The Padawan Learner turned Jedi turned rebel turned teacher handed her the braid. Rey held it tighter than anything she'd ever held in her life.

"I won't forget." Rey promised, tears in her eyes. "I'll neever forget."

The greatest hunter and tracker on Tatooine knelt down before Ahsoka, because she didn't know what else to do.

"Luke isn't angry at you, Rey. I knew Anakin like my own blood, I know Luke, and I know you." Ahsoka promised.

Nobody had ever known Rey before. Rey didn't know how to feel.

"Luke is scared. He sees you, he sees your anger, that hatred inside of you, the call of the Dark Side, and he sees someone else. He knows that it looks like when somebody dedicates everything they have vengeance. It destroys them. He was afraid of losing his father to the Dark Side, and now he's afraid of losing you."

Rey pried the words out of her own mouth, sensing Kylo Ren standing just outside the Temple.

"I'm… I'm afraid of losing me too."

Ahsoka brushed Rey's hair out of her face, massaging the tense muscles in the back of her neck.

"I know, little one, I know."

The Holotape from earlier reached the climax they'd both forgotten they'd been waiting for. Darth Vader threw Emperor Palpatine down the tube of Endor.

"But if you are anything like my old Master, anything like Anakin, then I know you won't forget. It may take a little while, it may take a long while, and you may do things that you come to regret. But I know… I know you, Rey… and I know you will remember who you are when the time is right."

Ahsoka was taken over by a coughing fit that racked her body as she offered both lightsabers to Rey. Rey didn't want to pick them up. Rey knew what picking them up meant.

Rey didn't want to say goodbye yet.

"I have no use for these anymore, young padawan. I am an old woman now, my Jedi days are behind me, and my teaching days are too. But you do, and you need to make me a promise before I let you leave."

Rey didn't want her to let her leave. But she waited anyway.

"Promise me you know where you're going, and you know what to do when you get there." Ahsoka said very quietly.

"I promise." Rey whispered.

Rey took the lightsabers, laid her head in Ahsoka's lap, and did the only thing she could think of.

She just balled into her lap like a little kid again. Ahsoka brushed her hair in steady circles.

"Don't cry, child." She soothed. "I'm not leaving."

Ahsoka and Rey held the Padawan braid together.

"I'll be right here," Ahsoka said, shaking the braid, "waiting for you, when you come back."

Ahsoka took her last breath, inhaling it slowly, deliberately, not fearing death, but not welcoming it either.

"May the Force be with you, Rey."

And then she steadily blew it out.

"May the Force be with you, too."

And Ahsoka Tano, Padawan Learner, Jedi Apprentice, Intelligence Operative, and a thousand other names, became one with the Force.
